## What went wrong, in one paragraph

The preprint reports that deep-time archaeological sites in East Java sit **90–170 km** from volcanic
centres. The actual figure is **33–53 km**. The cause was an incomplete volcano inventory: the analysis
file contained **7** eastern East Java centres and omitted Lawu, Wilis and all of Central Java. Two
*Antiquity* reviewers identified the problem in full peer review (AQY-2026-0104, rejected 2026-06-04)
and were right. The canonical inventory used since is
`data/processed/dashboard/volcanoes_java_full.csv` (30 centres).

The distance claim is not recoverable by rewording. What survives the correction is a different and
narrower argument — that **exposure windows** (erosion, karst) rather than distance from a volcano
determine what is findable — and that argument belongs in a new paper, not in a patched version of this
one.

## TEXT OF THE NOTICE — copy from here

**Correction notice, posted by the author.**

This preprint contains an error that invalidates its central quantitative claim, and readers should not
rely on it.

The manuscript states that deep-time archaeological sites in East Java are located 90–170 km from
volcanic centres. **That figure is incorrect.** It was produced from an incomplete volcano inventory
containing only seven eastern East Java centres, which omitted Lawu, Wilis and the whole of Central
Java. Recomputed against a complete inventory of thirty Javanese volcanic centres, the distances are
**33–53 km**. The error was identified in peer review, and the reviewers were correct.

Because the distance claim underpins the manuscript's argument, the interpretation built on it does not
stand. The author is not revising this preprint. A separate analysis, framed around erosion and karst
exposure windows rather than distance from volcanic centres, is in preparation and will be posted
independently.

Readers who have cited this preprint are asked to note this correction.
