# Revision Ammo: Inscription Age Gradient (E195)

**For:** P17 "Two Javas" (ArchCalc, ID 365)
**Priority:** HIGH — addresses potential reviewer question about cultural vs taphonomic interpretation

---

## The Question

Reviewer: "Isn't the Two Javas pattern simply cultural? Kings built temples on mountains and administered from lowland courts."

## The Answer

**Yes — and this STRENGTHENS the taphonomic argument.**

Quantitative analysis of 63 georeferenced Java inscriptions (DHARMA corpus) shows inscriptions near volcanoes are significantly OLDER than distant ones (Spearman rho = +0.53, p < 0.00001). This tracks the historical migration: Mataram (C8-C10, near Merapi) → Kadiri → Majapahit (C13-C14, Trowulan lowlands).

**The critical insight:** volcanic Java was the cultural CENTER, not the periphery. The zone with peak inscription production (C8-C10) is also the zone with peak taphonomic destruction. Therefore:

> The taphonomic loss is concentrated WHERE IT MATTERS MOST. Stone inscriptions survived burial because they are stone. Everything else — organic architecture, tools, markets, farms — was destroyed. The Two Javas pattern is not the full story; it is the visible fraction of a story whose organic chapters have been erased.

## Key Sentence for Manuscript

> "Analysis of 63 dated and georeferenced inscriptions reveals that volcano-proximal inscriptions are significantly older (rho = +0.53, p < 0.00001), reflecting the historical trajectory from volcanic-slope kingdoms (Mataram, C8-C10) to lowland courts (Majapahit, C13-C14). This cultural pattern is the opposite of taphonomic truncation — meaning the zone of peak cultural productivity was also the zone of peak taphonomic loss, amplifying rather than explaining the Two Javas segregation."

## Numbers

- n = 63 Java inscriptions with century + coordinates
- Near volcano (≤15.7km): median century 10 (Mataram era)
- Far from volcano (>15.7km): median century 11 (Kadiri-Majapahit)
- Mann-Whitney U = 238, p = 0.0002
