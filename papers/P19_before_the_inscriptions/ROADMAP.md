## Theoretical Architecture

### Central Argument
Lombard (1990) described Java as a *carrefour* — a crossroads of three cultural layers (Western, Islamic/Chinese, Indian). We argue there is a **fourth layer** beneath Lombard's deepest — the pre-Indic Austronesian stratum — that is invisible not because it didn't exist, but because taphonomic and historiographic processes have erased its material traces while leaving its imprints in language, ritual, and spatial practice. This invisibility is not accidental; it is **structurally produced** by the intersection of geophysical processes (volcanic sedimentation) and knowledge-production practices (colonial periodization, the privileging of inscriptions as historical evidence).

### Debates to Engage

| Debate | Key Scholars | Our Position |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Indianization vs Localization** | Coedes (1968), van Leur (1955), Wolters (1982/1999), Kulke (1990) | Localization requires a RECEIVING culture. What was it? VOLCARCH provides the first quantitative sketch. |
| **Lombard's Three Layers** | Lombard (1990) | There is a fourth layer — the pre-Indic Austronesian basement. |
| **Colonial Knowledge Production** | Sears (1996), Bloembergen & Eickhoff (2020) | The periodization "Hindu period begins ~400 CE" is a colonial artifact, not a cultural fact. |
| **Formation Processes** | Schiffer (1987), Hertz (1907) | Archaeological visibility is not a passive record — it is shaped by formation processes. Volcanic taphonomy is a formation process. |
| **Heritage Politics** | Bloembergen & Eickhoff (2020), Tanudirjo (1995) | If the foundational layer of Javanese civilization is invisible, what does that mean for heritage policy? |
| **Pre-Indic Austronesian World** | Fox (1993/2006), Bellwood (1997/2017), Reid (1988/93) | The Austronesian world had complex societies BEFORE Indian contact — maritime, agricultural, ritualistic. |

## Paper Structure (8 sections, ~12,000 words)

### 1. Introduction: Lombard's Missing Layer (~1,500 words)
- Open with Lombard's *carrefour* metaphor — three layers, brilliantly described
- The puzzle: Lombard's deepest layer is the Indian stratum (Vol. 3, "L'Heritage des royaumes concentriques"). What lies beneath?
- Frame the question NOT as "was there civilization before 400 CE?" (that's P1's question) but as "what does the ABSENCE of evidence mean for how we construct knowledge about Java?"
- State the argument: the missing layer is not missing — it is invisible, rendered so by taphonomic and historiographic processes
- Position within decolonial turn: challenging periodizations inherited from colonial scholarship

### 2. The Indianization Debate Revisited (~1,500 words)
- Coedes' "Indianization" → van Leur's "trade" → Wolters' "localization" → current "convergence" models
- The question nobody asks: if Indianization was "localization" (Wolters), what was the LOCAL that was doing the localizing?
- The silence about the receiving culture is itself a historiographic formation process
- Engage with Kulke's "Early and Imperial Kingdom" — mandala theory presupposes existing political structures

### 3. Why the Layer Is Invisible (~2,000 words)
- NOT a technical section — a narrative about HOW evidence is produced and destroyed
- Volcanic sedimentation: the temples that disappeared (Sambisari, Kedulan — discovered by accident)
- Colonial archaeology: what the Dutch looked for, and what they didn't (Bloembergen & Eickhoff 2020)
- The inscription bias: history begins when writing begins — but writing arrived WITH Indianization
- Schiffer's formation processes: the archaeological record is not a passive archive, it is actively shaped
- The organic gap: bamboo, wood, and thatch leave no trace in volcanic soil

### 4. Traces in Language (~1,500 words)
- Wolters asked what the "local" culture was that localized Indian imports. Language provides one answer.
- Agricultural vocabulary: 91% native (not a statistical claim — a cultural observation about what Sanskrit did and didn't replace)
- Maritime vocabulary conservation in peripheral communities (Bali retains what Java lost)
- The word *surat*: writing as indigenous concept (PAN *surat, 3000-5000 BP)
- Frame: Indianization was a REGISTER phenomenon — elite/religious yes, economic/daily no

### 5. Traces in Ritual (~1,500 words)
- The slametan persistence: four religious waves modified content, not timing
- Hertz (1907) and the link between decomposition and ritual
- The tradition's OWN words about the body (Hendrajaya 2020) — not our interpretation, THEIR description
- Frame as indigenous empirical knowledge, not "primitive belief"
- Compare with Toraja, Balinese, and pan-Austronesian mortuary practices (Fox 1993/2006)

### 6. Traces in Stone (~1,000 words)
- Temple siting reveals volcanic awareness (not quantitative argument — cultural observation)
- Pranata Mangsa: agricultural calendar that encodes volcanic hazard seasons
- Architecture as indigenous environmental knowledge
- Compare: Japanese Shinto volcano deities — different volcanic landscape, different ritual response

### 7. The Politics of the Invisible (~2,000 words)
- **This is the BKI section** — where social relevance lives
- If the foundational layer of Javanese civilization is invisible, what does that mean?
- For heritage policy: where should Indonesia look? (underground, not on the surface)
- For national identity: "kerajaan pertama" narrative — Kutai's visibility, not its primacy
- For education: Indonesian textbooks (Bloembergen & Eickhoff 2020) start at the "Hindu period"
- For decolonial practice: the colonial periodization persists because the alternative is literally buried
- The irony: volcanic burial that hides the past ALSO preserves it (sealed anaerobic contexts)

### 8. Conclusion: The Carrefour Has a Basement (~500 words)
- Lombard was right about the crossroads — but there is a level below the street
- We don't claim to have found this layer — we claim to have shown WHY it is invisible and WHERE to look
- Not a discovery claim but a visibility claim: framework for understanding why evidence is absent
- Close with the honest claim: "the door is buried, but we know where to dig"
- Final footnote: cite P1 preprint (Zenodo DOI) as quantitative foundation for the narrative arguments
