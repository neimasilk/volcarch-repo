### Reframed Abstract (~150 words)

For 1,500 years, through Hindu, Buddhist, Islamic, and Christian religious transformations, the Javanese death ritual *slametan* has preserved an unchanging numerical sequence: commemorations at 3, 7, 40, 100, and 1,000 days. This extraordinary persistence reveals a hidden layer of indigenous knowledge: an empirical understanding of death calibrated to the volcanic landscape. We demonstrate this through three lines of evidence: epigraphic proof that indigenous Javanese cosmology survived religious Indianization, comparative data showing that the *structure* of gradual death is pan-Austronesian while *timing* is environmentally variable, and forensic analysis showing that the five intervals correspond to observable decomposition stages in volcanic Andosol (p < 0.001). The convergence reveals a paradox: the volcanic environment that Javanese communities understood through ritual is the same that erases them from the archaeological record.
