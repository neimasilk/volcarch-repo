# Notes

## Core claim (1 sentence)
Elemental analysis of 146 glass samples from southwestern Taiwan dated 1st-8th c. CE demonstrates Taiwan's participation in multiple long-distance glass exchange networks spanning the South China Sea to West Asia (Mesopotamia, Sasanian Empire) and the Roman Mediterranean, with South Asian m-Na-Al glass sub-type 1 being most common.

## Evidence presented
- 146 glass samples from Daoye, Daoye South, Wujiancuo sites (southwestern Taiwan)
- Techniques: LA-ICP-MS (elemental trace analysis) + SEM-EDS
- Findings: multiple glass types and origins identified
  - **South Asian m-Na-Al glass sub-type 1:** dominant
  - **Newly recognised m-Na-Al low Al low Ca** sub-type → possible small-scale workshops or less controlled recipes in the region
  - **Early v-Na-Ca glass:** origins in Mesopotamia or Sasanian territories (secondary production areas unclear)
  - **Potash glass:** chemistry doesn't align with early potash glass
  - **Roman HIMT glass:** attributable to Roman Mediterranean
- Distribution implications: Taiwan-SE Asia-South Asia-West Asia-Mediterranean network spanning 8,000+ km
- Time span: first to eighth century CE

## How it relates to VOLCARCH
**Crucial context for P0 Channel 6 (Jatim beads).** Places the Datong Jatim bead finding in a broader documented network:

1. Taiwan's glass corpus shows multi-source receipt: not all SE Asian glass comes from Java. This means the Java attribution for specific Jatim beads requires diagnostic specificity (not chemistry alone).
2. The dominance of South Asian m-Na-Al in Taiwan establishes that **m-Na-Al ≠ Java-specific** — a methodological caveat for Jia 2024 attribution.
3. Establishes the existence and reach of trans-Eurasian maritime glass network in the exact period VOLCARCH argues invisible substrate civilisation existed in Java.
4. The "newly recognised m-Na-Al low Al low Ca" sub-type with possible small-scale workshops in the region parallels VOLCARCH's thesis of dispersed organic-infrastructure workshops.
5. Roman HIMT glass reaching Taiwan confirms trans-Mediterranean-to-East-Asian maritime exchange was operating pre-500 CE, making Java's participation plausible.

## Methodological strengths
- Large sample size (146 glasses)
- Dual analytical methods (LA-ICP-MS + SEM-EDS) for cross-validation
- Clear sub-type taxonomy with explicit attribution rationale
- Dussubieux as second author = access to canonical SE Asian glass compositional database
- Newly-recognised sub-type demonstrates careful chemistry, not curve-fitting existing categories

## Methodological weaknesses / critiques
- Taiwan as terminal receiver, not production center — cannot directly inform Java production
- "Newly recognised m-Na-Al low Al low Ca" sub-type's workshop location is explicitly "unclear"
- Strontium/neodymium isotope work is absent; would further constrain raw material sources

## Chronological implications
- First-eighth century CE maritime glass trade is ROBUSTLY documented for Taiwan
- By extension, Java's participation in this network in the same period is plausible and consistent with the Jatim bead Datong finding
- No reason to suppose the network EXCLUDED Java during pre-500 CE — the silence in Javanese archaeology is consistent with taphonomic invisibility, not non-participation
