# Research Lead — "Jatim" Glass Beads (East Java) and the *peradaban vulkanik* thesis

**Date:** 2026-06-08 · **Trigger:** Pak Amien ("riset jatim beads, paper Nature") · **Status:** NEW LEAD (not previously in project) · **SIG-calibrated** (claims graded by source quality)

## What Jatim beads are
"Jatim" (= **Ja**wa **Tim**ur / East Java) = a class of polychrome glass beads associated with East Java. Four sub-types: **mosaic** (millefiori), **pelangi** (rainbow combed stripes), **spiral-striped**, **monochrome** (good-glass layer over coarse core). Made by reworking **imported Byzantine & Sasanian glass cullet** into distinctive local forms — i.e., indigenous *craft* on imported raw glass, not local glass-from-scratch.

## Sources (graded)
- **PEER-REVIEWED:** *Analysis of two glass eye (Jatim) beads … Northern Wei tomb, Dongxin, Datong*, **npj Heritage Science (2024)**, DOI 10.1186/s40494-024-01319-w — two Jatim "eye" beads in a Northern Wei tomb (**Northern Wei = 386–534 CE**) attributed to origin in the "Kingdom of Java"; read as evidence of Northern Wei (Pingcheng)–Java exchange. *(Full text paywalled-redirect at fetch time; provenance claim from abstract + 2 independent search summaries.)*
- **PEER-REVIEWED:** Lankton, Dussubieux & Rehren (2008) *A study of mid-first-millennium CE Southeast Asian specialized glass beadmaking traditions* — situates Jatim in a **mid-first-millennium CE (~400–600 CE)** specialized SEA beadmaking tradition.
- **SECONDARY:** Caitlin Green (2018) — Indo-Pacific beads, **5th–7th c CE** global distribution (Europe→Japan).
- **COMMERCIAL (low trust, dating only):** bead dealers say "~400–900 CE" / "600–800 CE". Treat as loose.
- **Distribution finds:** Berenike (Byzantine Red Sea port, Egypt), Silla royal tomb Korea (Nat'l Treasure 634, 5th/6th c), Japan, Palau (Micronesia, 600–900 CE).

## Honest relevance to the thesis (calibrated — do NOT overclaim)
1. **Dating is 5th–8th c CE → NOT pre-400 CE.** Jatim beads are *contemporaneous with / after* the inscription horizon. They do **not** directly fill the pre-400 CE gap. (Integrity: the romantic reading "ancient beads prove a hidden old civilization" is not supported by the dating.)
2. **STRONG for the *character* claim (peradaban vulkanik):** by the 5th–6th c CE East Java was a node of **specialized indigenous craft + long-distance trade** reaching China, Korea, Japan, Egypt, Micronesia. This is positive evidence that Nusantara was a real, globally-connected civilization — not a backwater "starting at 400 CE."
3. **SELECTIVE SURVIVAL (links E204 bronze drums):** durable glass survived and even travelled inter-continentally, while the bulk (perishable, bamboo/organic) vanished. Jatim beads are a rare *durable trace* of a largely invisible material culture.
4. **Inferential pre-400 root:** a mature bead-EXPORT industry by ~450–500 CE implies craft + trade development in the preceding centuries — plausible but inferential, not proof.
5. **WEAK volcanic link:** the beads use imported glass cullet, not volcanic materials. Do **not** claim a direct "volcanic resource" connection.

## The actually-pre-400 CE bead angle — VERIFIED 2026-06-08 (the real pre-400 evidence)
The broader **Indo-Pacific bead / Indian-contact** trade reaches Indonesia BEFORE 400 CE — now SIG-verified from peer-reviewed sources:
- **Sembiran & Pacung (north Bali):** Indian **Rouletted Ware** (Arikamedu Types 10 & 18), **carnelian beads**, glass artefacts; a "trading/warehousing" activity area. Direct India–Indonesia trade from **~2nd c BCE**; Rouletted Ware floruit ~AD 1–200. **Ardika & Bellwood 1991, *Antiquity* 65:221–32** ("Sembiran: the beginnings of Indian contact with Bali"); **Calo et al. 2015, *Antiquity*** ("Sembiran and Pacung … strategic crossroads for early trans-Asiatic exchange").
- This is **~600 years before the first inscriptions (~400 CE)** — solid, peer-reviewed evidence that Nusantara communities participated in trans-Asiatic exchange well before the textual horizon. **Strong support for the *peradaban vulkanik* CHARACTER claim** (sophisticated, connected society pre-400 CE). Caveat: shows trade *contact* + a trading post, not by itself a large indigenous polity; it is Bali (not Java interior).
- Still-to-verify (lower priority): Gilimanuk (Bali), Plawangan/Pacinan (Java N coast) early-metal-age beads.

## Addendum 2026-10-15 — "Is there a trace in East Java?" (PI)

**Trigger:** the PI on 15 Oct: "bagaimana dengan jatim beads? apakah ada 'jejaknya di jatim?' jatim beads ada di paper nature loh".
**Which paper:** "the Nature paper" is Jia, Cui & Cao 2024, *Heritage Science* 12:204. It is a Springer Nature journal published on nature.com, and it is the Datong paper above. A search on 2026-10-15 found no Jatim paper in *Nature* itself.

### What the Datong paper does and does not show

Read in full from the open archived copy (d-nb.info/1342627261/34).

- **The Java origin is inferred from the bead type.** It rests on the bead's style and technique and on the mixed v-Na-Ca + m-Na-Al glass, which Lankton et al. 2008 found almost only in Jatim beads.
- The paper itself writes "we can only infer that the eye beads ... originated in Java". For "the eastern Java region, where the majority of Jatim beads have been discovered" it cites Lankton & Bernbaum 2007. It names no find spot in Java.
- **It contradicts itself on date.** It says "Jatim beads originated in the Java region in the sixth century AD", while the tomb dates to 398–494.
- **Consequence:** `docs/bibliography/_SYNTHESIS_for_P0.md` C1 ("demonstrably Javanese in production") says more than the paper does. Ledger C073.

### The trace in East Java

Each item was checked at its source on 2026-10-15.

1. **Lankton & Bernbaum 2007** (*BEADS* 19:32–41, p. 36; open PDF). The link to far-eastern Java as the production area rests on four things:
   - misshapen and partially melted examples found near Jember;
   - most Jatim beads having been found in a limited area of eastern Java (Adhyatman & Arifin; Francis 2002:135);
   - Jatim beads recovered from megalithic tombs by Dutch archaeologists around the turn of the 20th century (van Heekeren 1958:46);
   - finds in Indonesian excavations since then.

   The same paper says the prehistoric archaeology of far-eastern Java is "still in its infancy", and that the bird canes seen on a few Jatim beads have no archaeological provenance.

2. **Primary colonial record, already in our OV corpus** (E091 caught the plate caption).

   *Oudheidkundig Verslag* 1921, first quarter (§6, and the plate facing p. 5, photo OD 8644):
   - Section engineer S. V. Joekes (irrigation, Pekalen-Sampean) reported two stone-covered, probably prehistoric graves. They were opened during road works between Garahan and Mrawan in Jember, and both held "fraaie gekleurde kralen".
   - The beads of one grave were strung and photographed.
   - The list of plates gives "Garahan, distr. Majang, afd. Djember, thans Mus. Batavia" (inventory number lost in our OCR).

   OV 1921, second quarter, pp. 56–58, records a third grave, opened in the second half of June 1921 on the acting Head's order, with Mr van Coolwijk present:
   - **Place:** hamlet Pasar Alas, about 3 km from Garahan station.
   - **Grave:** a cap stone on a ring of stone posts; entrance to the west; oval, 2.25 × 1.85 m.
   - **Finds:** **79 beads**, 54 teeth (at least three persons), a dagger-shaped stone object of 13.5 cm, and a small gold ring.
   - **The beads:** 3 mm to 4.5 cm across. Colours: yellow-and-green, white-and-green, indigo, yellow, green, red, gold-yellow. On the large ones a "sarong" motif (green ground with yellow checks and stripes); one green bead with yellow spiral stripes. Egg or barrel shapes with flattened ends, some "jet-shaped".
   - **Materials as described:** blue beads "glass-like"; large ones "a kind of soapstone"; the smallest of baked clay.

   **My reading, not verified:**
   - Large polychrome beads with yellow-on-green stripes, spiral stripes and flattened ends fit the Jatim *pelangi* and spiral-striped sub-types, and the pinched-tube technique.
   - The 1921 observer's "soapstone" may be opaque glass, and the small "terracotta-coloured" beads may be opaque red glass.
   - Only photo OD 8644 or the beads themselves can settle this.

   *Berkala Arkeologi* (the Pakauman settlement-pattern paper, citing Soejono 1984) describes "a dolmen excavated by de Haan" with human teeth, 79 beads of stone, glass and terracotta, and a gold ring. This is the same assemblage, so the 1921 Garahan grave is very probably what later literature calls de Haan's dolmen.

3. **Indonesian reports in the BRIN journals** (found through the E225 frame):
   - **Pakauman, Bondowoso.** Willems in 1938 excavated a batu-kenong group and found pottery, **glass beads**, an iron bracelet and bark-cloth beaters (same paper, citing Soejono 1984).
   - **Malangsari, Kalibaru, Banyuwangi** (Kasnowiharjo, *Berkala Arkeologi* 37(1), 2017):
     - Treasure hunters dig the dolmens **for beads** and gold or silver; by their account the beads lay mixed with human bones in the chamber.
     - The 2016 excavation (Balai Arkeologi DIY) found beads, iron, pottery and a whetstone.
     - The site is at 633 m asl (Desa Kebonrejo, S 8°22′53″ E 113°56′30″, at the edge of Meru Betiri), and TP1 shows a
       volcanic ash layer (caption of Fig. 4).
     - Malangsari is on the Banyuwangi side of the border and Garahan on the Jember side of the Gumitir road. They are
       probably one dolmen zone; the distance between them has not been measured.
   - **Situbondo and Jember** (*Berkala Arkeologi* 19(2) and 27(1)):
     - "Pacific-type" beads among the grave goods;
     - ¹⁴C 1250 ± 240 BP (P3G) at Krajan, Situbondo;
     - a sarcophagus inscribed 1324 Saka (1402 CE);
     - Doplang, Jember, 580 ± 100 BP.
   - None of the 13 texts read calls a bead "Jatim" or gives a composition. Three relevant PDFs are scans and were not read: *Amerta* 3934 (Bondowoso megalith distribution, 2026), *Amerta* 15216 ("Manik-manik dalam kajian arkeologi", 1994) and *Amerta* 3521 (Kendenglembu).

4. **Not found:**
   - an excavated workshop;
   - a composition for any excavated bead from these graves;
   - a date tied to a bead-bearing grave in the 4th–7th century window. Krajan's ±240 is too wide to place it.

### What it means (calibrated)

- If the Garahan beads are Jatim, East Java has a provenanced assemblage of the type, excavated in 1921 from a stone-capped grave of the far-eastern Java dolmen tradition.
  - The Datong tomb dates such beads to before 494 CE.
  - Together they would place a bead-using community in the Gumitir area (Jember–Banyuwangi) by the 5th century CE. It was probably also bead-making (the melted pieces near Jember).
  - That is about three centuries before East Java's first inscription (Dinoyo, 760 CE).
  - **It is not pre-400 CE evidence by itself.**
- It is a textbook case of the "taphonomy of records" (K6). The evidence survives in a 1921 colonial report, a museum store and the antiquities market (dolmens dug for beads), not in a dated, analysed site.
- **The decisive test needs a partner:**
  - the composition (pXRF or LA-ICP-MS) of the Garahan beads in the Museum Nasional, or of the Malangsari 2016 beads (BRIN), against the Jatim mixed-glass signature;
  - and a date for one of the graves.
