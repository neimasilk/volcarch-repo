## Hypothesis

Sacred architecture (candi) and administrative inscriptions occupy DIFFERENT volcanic distance zones, reflecting a spatial division between sacred and political functions. The "court zone" (20-40km) should concentrate inscriptions while the "volcano zone" (0-20km) concentrates candi.

## Result: SPATIAL SEGREGATION CONFIRMED (p < 0.000001)

| Zone | Candi (142) | % | Inscriptions (176) | % |
|------|-------------|---|-------------------|---|
| 0-10km | **60** | **42.3%** | 22 | 12.5% |
| 10-20km | 21 | 14.8% | 46 | 26.1% |
| **20-30km** | 44 | 31.0% | **69** | **39.2%** |
| 30-40km | 8 | 5.6% | 12 | 6.8% |
| 40-60km | 5 | 3.5% | 20 | 11.4% |
| 60-100km | 4 | 2.8% | 7 | 4.0% |

- **Candi peak: 0-10km** (42.3%) — sacred architecture on volcano slopes
- **Inscription peak: 20-30km** (39.2%) — administrative courts in fertile plains
- **Median distance: Candi 14.6km vs Inscriptions 27.6km** (MW p < 0.000001)

- P7 showed candi are 9.2km closer to volcanoes than inscriptions
- E104 shows the PEAK ZONES differ: 0-10km (candi) vs 20-30km (inscriptions)

## Connection to E103 (Pre-Indic Spatial Gradient)

E103 found that pre-Indic vocabulary recovery (rho=0.781) occurs ONLY at 20-40km — the court zone. E104 explains WHY: **this is where inscriptions are concentrated**. The court zone is where Indianization happened (Sanskrit administrative system), and where it retreated (post-929 CE indigenous recovery).

The volcano zone (0-10km) was always relatively indigenous because CANDI (not inscriptions) dominated there. Candi are physical structures; inscriptions are textual records. The archaeological darkness is doubly deep in the volcano zone: the BUILDINGS are buried, and the TEXTS were never there to begin with.
