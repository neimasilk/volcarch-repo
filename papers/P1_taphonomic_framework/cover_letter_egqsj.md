Dear Editors of E&G Quaternary Science Journal,

We submit the manuscript "Multi-Site Calibration of Volcanic Sedimentation Rates and Implications for Archaeological Visibility in Java, Indonesia" for consideration as a research article in EGQSJ.

Java contains 45 active volcanoes within 129,000 km². Despite hosting major Hindu-Buddhist polities from at least the 4th century CE, its early archaeological record is remarkably thin. This paper offers a geophysical explanation. Using four temple sites with independently documented construction dates (760–1268 CE) and measured burial depths—spanning two volcanic systems (Kelud and Merapi) separated by 350 km—we calibrate a mean volcanic sedimentation rate of 4.4 ± 1.2 mm/yr across Java. At this rate, pre-Hindu archaeological deposits now lie at depths of 6–10 m, well beyond surface survey capability. Spatial analysis of 666 sites confirms that the observable archaeological record tracks monument durability and survey effort rather than historical settlement intensity. We identify priority zones for future subsurface investigation.

The manuscript addresses geoarchaeology, volcanic geomorphology, and taphonomic processes in tropical Quaternary environments—core areas of EGQSJ's scope. To our knowledge, this is the first multi-site calibration of volcanic sedimentation rates applied specifically to archaeological visibility in a tropical setting.
