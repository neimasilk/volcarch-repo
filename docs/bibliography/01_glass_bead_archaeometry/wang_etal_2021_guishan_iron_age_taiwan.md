# Notes

## Core claim (1 sentence)
Analysis of 64 glass beads from Guishan (southern Taiwan, mid-1st millennium CE) reveals multiple workshops of origin for m-Na-Al glass (coloured by different copper-based additives) circulating through Southeast Asia to Taiwan, plus a regionally limited bone-ash-containing yellow glass type indicating small-scale Taiwan-area bead exchange.

## Key VOLCARCH-relevant findings
1. Guishan beads dominated by m-Na-Al and v-Na-Ca compositions
2. m-Na-Al often copper-coloured with multiple additive recipes → multiple workshops
3. Beads arrived via Southeast Asia (intermediary) from South/West Asian production sources
4. A novel bone-ash yellow glass appears only regionally (possible local workshop)

## VOLCARCH implications
- Taiwan's bead assemblage is multi-sourced; the same must be assumed for other SE Asian terminals
- "Workshops" plural (from multiple copper recipes) = a dispersed production landscape, not single mega-workshop
- Consistent with VOLCARCH thesis of many small organic-infrastructure production sites, each archaeologically invisible
