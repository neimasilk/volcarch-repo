## The Finding

Two glass eye beads (DX-1, DX-2) were excavated in 2013 from the Phase Two Northern Wei tomb complex at Dongxin Furniture Square, Datong, Shanxi Province, China. Datong was the capital of the Northern Wei Dynasty (known as Pingcheng, population ~1 million during the 5th century). The tomb complex is dated to the Northern Wei period, 398--494 CE. Associated grave goods included gold, silverware, ironware, bronzeware, and ceramicware.

The beads were subjected to non-destructive analysis by Jia et al. (2024) using:
- Scanning electron microscopy with energy-dispersive spectrometry (SEM-EDS) for elemental composition
- 3D ultra-depth-of-field microscopy for morphology and manufacturing technique
- Comparison against Corning Glass Works standards (A, C, D)

## The Javanese Attribution (Chemical, not Stylistic)

**DX-1 (10.3 g, 1.6 cm diameter):** Dark blue matrix with white double-ring eye sockets radially arranged around the hole.
- Blue matrix: Na₂O 10.5--17.0 wt%, CaO 3.5--6.7 wt%, K₂O 1.1--3.4 wt%, MgO 1.1--2.5 wt%
- White eye regions: high Al₂O₃ (up to 14 wt%)

**DX-2 (10.8 g, 1.8 cm diameter):** Dark blue matrix with red/white outlined eyes filled with yellow or matrix-colour.
- Blue matrix: Na₂O 9.3--15.4 wt%, CaO 5.7--6.8 wt%
- Red/yellow regions: high Al₂O₃

**Classification:** Both beads are v-Na-Ca (plant-ash sodium-calcium glass) for the matrix combined with m-Na-Al (mineral-fluxed sodium-aluminium glass) for the decoration. The authors state explicitly:

> "The composition analysis revealed that both beads belonged to the mix of v-Na-Ca and m-Na-Al glass system, which is a key indicator of local production in Java."

The mixed system is distinctive: Chinese glass of this era is typically Pb-Ba based; South Asian glass is m-Na-Al but monomorphic; Mediterranean glass is natron-based Na-Ca. The combination documented here is diagnostic of Javanese Metal Age and early historic glass production (``Jatim bead'' tradition).

## Colorants and Technical Sophistication

- **Blue:** Co²⁺ (0.1--0.2 wt% CoO). Low MnO₂ (0.1--0.4 wt%) indicates eastern Mediterranean cobalt source, not South Asian or Chinese (both of which have MnO₂ > 1.0 wt%).
- **White:** SnO₂ (tin white, 17--69 wt%). Tin white was applied in Roman and Byzantine glass manufacturing after the 4th century CE.
- **Red:** Cu₂O or metallic Cu⁰ (1.9--5.2 wt% Cu₂O). Copper red glass production in China began only in the 7th--10th centuries CE, considerably later than the Datong beads; the Jatim beads therefore cannot be Chinese imports.
- **Yellow:** PbSnO₃ (lead-tin yellow). Applied in Roman Empire glass manufacturing from the 2nd--1st centuries BCE; widely used in Europe and West Asia after the 4th century CE.

**Technical implication:** Jatim bead production drew on trans-Eurasian technical vocabulary (Roman/Byzantine opacifiers, Mediterranean cobalt) combined with local raw materials (plant-ash flux from Javanese flora). This is not a frontier industry; it is a sophisticated node in the mid-first-millennium Eurasian glassmaking network.

## Why This Matters for VOLCARCH

### 1. Direct material evidence of organised industrial activity in Java by 398--494 CE.

The traditional literature dates Jatim beads to 400--900 CE (Lankton), with peak production often placed at 600--800 CE. This paper pushes the earliest documented Javanese export of Jatim beads into the 5th century at latest, overlapping or preceding the earliest surviving Javanese inscriptions (Tarumanagara, Kutai, ~358--400 CE). The beads therefore document a civilisational complexity that the Javanese archaeological record alone does not attest.

### 2. The production sites remain archaeologically invisible in Java.

This is the core VOLCARCH alignment. The Datong paper establishes through chemistry that glass workshops were operating in Java during the Northern Wei period. No Javanese glass workshop from this period has been excavated. The workshops were organic structures --- furnaces, fuel stockpiles, raw material sheds, associated residential compounds --- in precisely the taphonomic regime that produces archaeological invisibility (volcanic burial of organic architecture, tropical decay, survey deficit, cave-availability gap).

### 3. Selective survival framework is empirically validated.

The VOLCARCH "selective survival" argument predicts exactly this pattern:
- Durable products (glass, metal, stone) travel and survive outside Java.
- Organic infrastructure (workshops, settlements) in Java does not survive.
- The absence of Javanese workshops is not evidence of absence; it is evidence of the taphonomic regime that has been hypothesised.

Jia et al. (2024) do not frame their finding in these terms, but their chemistry is consistent only with an industry that existed but left minimal archaeologically visible infrastructure in its homeland.

### 4. International trade network predating Indianisation narrative.

Conventional Southeast Asian prehistory has narrated pre-500 CE Java as peripheral, receiving Indian influence through traders and Brahmans \citep{coedes1968}. The Datong beads (Datong is INLAND China, not a port) plus related finds (Berenike in Egypt, Sikrichong Tomb in Korea, Japanese sites, Palau in Micronesia) indicate that Java was exporting luxury glass across a trans-Eurasian network during or before the early Hindu period. Java in this picture is not a recipient of civilisation but a participant in a global exchange system whose material record in Java itself is selectively erased.

### 5. Fits the channel independence criterion for P0 synthesis.

P0 develops five converging evidence channels (sedimentation, demographic, Philippines comparison, linguistic substrate, paleogenomic). The Datong glass-bead channel is:
- **Methodologically independent:** archaeometric chemistry, not demographic modelling or linguistic reconstruction.
- **Data independent:** Chinese tomb excavation data, no overlap with DHARMA inscriptions, ABVD wordlists, or colonial archives.
- **Observer independent:** Chinese and Peking University archaeologists with no VOLCARCH contact; paper written without awareness of Javanese archaeological debates.
- **Product independent:** beads are not a VOLCARCH data source; they are an outcome whose attribution to Java was established before we looked.

This satisfies the strongest form of the independence criterion: a finding that did not exist when VOLCARCH was conceived, published by researchers unaware of VOLCARCH, that independently corroborates the framework.

## Factual Cautions (things the Facebook post exaggerates)

1. **The tomb is not dated specifically to 456 CE.** The paper gives a range of 398--494 CE (the Dynasty range). A more precise date requires consulting the original Chinese excavation report.

2. **The paper does not identify "which Javanese kingdom."** It says "Kingdom of Java" as a geographic descriptor. Linking to Tarumanagara, pre-Kanjuruhan East Java, or any specific polity is speculative on current evidence.

3. **The 130 CE "Yedio/Yepoti" claim (Han-era Chinese records).** This is a separate claim, not from the Jia et al. paper. Chinese texts (Hou Han Shu, Book of Liang) reference Yepoti, Yediao, She-p'o among other toponyms that have been variously interpreted as Java, Sumatra, or broader Indonesia. The identification is linguistically contested. Worth a separate literature verification before citing.

4. **"Industri kerajaan kuno di Jawa" (industry of ancient kingdom in Java).** Chemically established production in Java, yes. "Kingdom" attribution is inferential --- organised industry requires organised patronage, but the paper does not make the state-formation claim.
