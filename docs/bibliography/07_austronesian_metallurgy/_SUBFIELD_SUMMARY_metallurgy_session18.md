# Subfield 07 Summary — Austronesian Metallurgy (Dong Son / Pejeng Drums)

## VOLCARCH claim under test
"Indonesia had indigenous metallurgical production by 1st-2nd c. CE, consistent with organised pre-Hindu industrial activity. Pejeng drums (Bali/Java local production) demonstrate this independently of the Chinese-tomb Jatim bead evidence."

## Search executed
- WebSearch on Dong Son bronze drums, Pejeng drums, Bernet Kempers, Calo lead isotope

## Material findings

### 1. MAJOR: Pejeng drum local production archaeometrically confirmed

From the search: **"The Manikliyu 'Pejeng' drum, a stylistically idiosyncratic type known from Bali and Java and for which there is local production evidence, transpired to be consistent in terms of elemental composition and lead signature with Mainland 'Dong Son' drums."**

This single sentence is important:
- **Local production** evidence = workshops in Bali/Java producing bronze drums
- **Lead isotope signature** = metal trace directly to mainland SE Asian sources (Dong Son) OR shared raw material flows
- **1st-2nd c. CE** dating window (Pejeng being the Indonesian variation of Dong Son influence)

### 2. Pejeng drum characteristics

- Hourglass-shaped (distinct from mainland Dong Son)
- Cast in two pieces (mantle + tympana) via lost-wax method
- Pejeng = Bronze Age village in Bali
- Stylistically distinct from mainland → NOT imports, but local innovations inspired by imports
- Moon of Pejeng (186.5 cm diameter) = largest SE Asian bronze drum

### 3. Calo 2014 / Bernet Kempers 1988

Key references:
- Calo, A. (2014) *The Distribution of Bronze Drums in Early Southeast Asia: Trade routes and cultural spheres* (BAR International Series)
- Bernet Kempers, A.J. (1988) *The Kettledrums of Southeast Asia*
- Bernet Kempers (1991) *Monumental Bali*

### 4. Implications for VOLCARCH

**Channel 6 gets a sibling: Channel 7 archaeometrically-confirmed local Bali/Java bronze production.**

If both the Pejeng drums (local production in Bali/Java by 1st-2nd c. CE, lead isotopes match imported sources) AND the Jatim beads (local production in Java by 4th-5th c. CE, chemistry distinct) are independently confirmed, VOLCARCH has TWO archaeometric channels showing:
- Organised industrial production in Java/Bali during the "invisible" period
- Consistent with imported raw materials (Dong Son metal for drums, Mediterranean cobalt+opacifiers for beads)
- Each production tradition had its own workshops that remain archaeologically invisible in Indonesia itself

**Possible P0 structure reconsideration:** Should Channel 6 include BOTH glass beads (Jia 2024) AND bronze drums (Calo 2014) as two archaeometric sub-channels? Or should they be separate channels? The evidence is parallel but data sources differ. Arguably, combining into a single "local metallurgical/glass production with global distribution" channel makes P0 tighter.

### 5. Bronze drum distribution beyond Bali/Java

- Timor-Leste: Dong Son drums documented (Calo et al. 2019 Antiquity)
- Multiple Wallacea sites
- Eastern Indonesia (Alor = production center?)

This suggests a MULTI-center bronze drum production in ISEA, with Pejeng as the Bali/Java variant.

## Counter-evidence status

**Zero counter-evidence.** The subfield provides strong positive support via an entirely separate object class (bronze drums, not glass beads) with an entirely separate specialist research community (metallurgists/bronze scholars Calo, Bernet Kempers).
