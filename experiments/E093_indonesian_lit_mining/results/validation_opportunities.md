## Cross-Reference with E091 OV NLP Results (22,162 mentions)

E091 extracted 22,162 mentions from 16 OV volumes. The E093 literature database identifies several publications that CITE or DISCUSS OV data. These create validation chains:

### OV-derived validation chains

1. **Bosch 1919 (OV) → E091 NLP → E070 DS-1 → E083 tephra correlation**
   - The Kelud 1919 entries flow through the entire pipeline. E093 adds the context that the FULL OV 1919 volume was dedicated to documenting Kelud destruction. E091 captured 94.2% of DS-1 entries. The remaining 5.8% (3 entries) may be recoverable with improved NLP patterns or manual reading of Bosch's narrative sections.

2. **Stutterheim 1926 (OV) → E091 NLP → E070 DS-1**
   - Stutterheim's Malang survey is partially captured in E070. E091's broader extraction may contain additional Malang-area mentions not in the curated 52-entry register. Systematic comparison between E091's `ov_site_mentions.csv` and Stutterheim 1926 could yield 10-20 additional site records.

3. **Tichelman 1929 (OV) → E091 NLP → E070 DS-1**
   - Same logic. Kediri highlands survey. E091 should have captured most mentions but manual verification against Tichelman's full article text could add detail.

### Quantitative validation opportunity

- **E091 extracted 26 numeric depth values** from OV volumes (vs DS-1's 32 measured depths)
- **E093 literature survey identifies ~8-10 publications with burial depth data independent of OV**
- **Combined potential:** 50-60 independently verified burial depth measurements across Java's volcanic zones
- This would constitute the largest validated burial depth dataset for any volcanic archaeological region worldwide

## Priority Validation Targets

### Tier 1: Obtain and extract ASAP (within existing budget/access)

1. **Pojoh 2007 (Trowulan GPR)** — Available through BIPPA. Key question: what GPR frequencies were used, and at what depth did signal attenuate in volcanic soils? This directly answers whether VOLCARCH's predictions are testable with current technology.

2. **Rangkuti 2008 (Lumajang)** and **Rangkuti 2000 (Arjuno)** — Available through Berkala Arkeologi (open access via arkeologi.kemdikbud.go.id). May contain burial depth observations in VOLCARCH's predicted Zone B.

3. **Riyanto 2014 (Liyangan comprehensive)** — Available through Amerta. Most detailed buried settlement report. Full volcanic stratigraphy data.

4. **Lukas 2012 (Kimpulan)** — Available through Archipel (open access). Independent buried temple with measured depth. Post-dates E070 colonial register.

### Tier 2: Requires institutional access or fieldwork contacts

5. **BPCB internal GPR reports** — Contact BPCB DIY and BPCB Jawa Timur. May contain unpublished GPR data from routine site assessments.

6. **ITB/UGM geophysics theses** — Search university repositories for GPR/resistivity studies at archaeological sites.

7. **Mulyaningsih 2006 (geological survey)** — May require contacting author or Geological Agency (Badan Geologi) for full deposit thickness data.

### Tier 3: Long-term targets

8. **Widianto (Sangiran deep stratigraphy)** — Different time scale but could validate very long-term sedimentation rates used in E075 model.

9. **Satari 1977 (Trowulan early excavation)** — Old report. May be in Puslitarkenas library. Valuable for historical excavation depth data before modern conservation restrictions.

## Implications for VOLCARCH Validation Strategy

### Current state
- E070 DS-1: 52 colonial site records, 32 with burial depths
- E083: 51 eruption-site correlation pairs, 24 with measured depths
- E091: 22,162 NLP mentions, 26 numeric depths

### Potential with E093 literature extraction
- **Additional 15-25 burial depth measurements** from publications identified in this review
- **GPR feasibility data** from at least 1 published study (Pojoh 2007) + potential grey literature
- **Independent geological validation** from Mulyaningsih 2006 and Newhall 2000 deposit data

### Bottom line
The literature mining reveals that EXISTING PUBLISHED DATA may already contain sufficient validation evidence for VOLCARCH's burial depth predictions. The problem is not data absence but data fragmentation — burial depth observations are scattered across dozens of publications in 3 languages (Indonesian, Dutch, English), across journals, dissertations, conference proceedings, and grey literature. E093's database begins to consolidate this scattered evidence into a queryable resource.

**The single most impactful action is obtaining and carefully reading the Pojoh 2007 Trowulan GPR paper** — it is the closest existing dataset to a direct test of whether GPR can detect buried structures at VOLCARCH-predicted depths in Java's volcanic soils.
