## Material findings

### 1. Berenike as Indo-Pacific trade hub (Sidebotham decades of excavation)

- Red Sea port active under Roman rule (30 BCE - early Byzantine)
- University of Delaware team led by Steven Sidebotham, excavations 1994-present
- Two harbors, multiple temples, residential/commercial buildings excavated
- Textual + archaeological evidence of direct trade to Indian Ocean

### 2. MAJOR: SE Asian beads documented at Berenike

> "Recent excavations are confirming the wealth and breadth of goods passing through Berenike in both directions, yielding beads from Thailand or Vietnam and even Java, indicating direct or indirect trade connections with these Southeast Asian regions during the Roman period."

Specific papers to obtain:
- Then (2014) "Beads and pendants from the late Harbor Temple and harbor temenos in the Red Sea port of Berenike (seasons 2010-2013)" — academia.edu available
- Sidebotham monograph *Berenike and the Ancient Maritime Spice Route*

### 3. Harbor Temple bead assemblage

- Dominated by South Asian glass beads, 4th through early 6th c. AD
- Also includes Indo-Pacific beads
- Includes beads attributed to Thailand, Vietnam, Java
- Drawn and rounded glass beads of South Asian origin identified in situ

### 4. Caitlin Green 2018 synthesis

> "Indo-Pacific beads from Europe to Japan? Another fifth- to seventh-century AD global distribution"

Suggests a period-specific global distribution of Indo-Pacific beads (including Jatim type) across:
- Europe (western Roman Empire)
- Red Sea (Berenike)
- Indian Ocean
- Southeast Asia (Java production)
- East Asia (China, Korea, Japan)

This is the **macro-picture** supporting VOLCARCH's Channel 6.
