## What Jatim beads are
"Jatim" (= **Ja**wa **Tim**ur / East Java) = a class of polychrome glass beads associated with East Java. Four sub-types: **mosaic** (millefiori), **pelangi** (rainbow combed stripes), **spiral-striped**, **monochrome** (good-glass layer over coarse core). Made by reworking **imported Byzantine & Sasanian glass cullet** into distinctive local forms — i.e., indigenous *craft* on imported raw glass, not local glass-from-scratch.

## Honest relevance to the thesis (calibrated — do NOT overclaim)
1. **Dating is 5th–8th c CE → NOT pre-400 CE.** Jatim beads are *contemporaneous with / after* the inscription horizon. They do **not** directly fill the pre-400 CE gap. (Integrity: the romantic reading "ancient beads prove a hidden old civilization" is not supported by the dating.)
2. **STRONG for the *character* claim (peradaban vulkanik):** by the 5th–6th c CE East Java was a node of **specialized indigenous craft + long-distance trade** reaching China, Korea, Japan, Egypt, Micronesia. This is positive evidence that Nusantara was a real, globally-connected civilization — not a backwater "starting at 400 CE."
3. **SELECTIVE SURVIVAL (links E204 bronze drums):** durable glass survived and even travelled inter-continentally, while the bulk (perishable, bamboo/organic) vanished. Jatim beads are a rare *durable trace* of a largely invisible material culture.
4. **Inferential pre-400 root:** a mature bead-EXPORT industry by ~450–500 CE implies craft + trade development in the preceding centuries — plausible but inferential, not proof.
5. **WEAK volcanic link:** the beads use imported glass cullet, not volcanic materials. Do **not** claim a direct "volcanic resource" connection.

## The actually-pre-400 CE bead angle — VERIFIED 2026-06-08 (the real pre-400 evidence)
The broader **Indo-Pacific bead / Indian-contact** trade reaches Indonesia BEFORE 400 CE — now SIG-verified from peer-reviewed sources:
- **Sembiran & Pacung (north Bali):** Indian **Rouletted Ware** (Arikamedu Types 10 & 18), **carnelian beads**, glass artefacts; a "trading/warehousing" activity area. Direct India–Indonesia trade from **~2nd c BCE**; Rouletted Ware floruit ~AD 1–200. **Ardika & Bellwood 1991, *Antiquity* 65:221–32** ("Sembiran: the beginnings of Indian contact with Bali"); **Calo et al. 2015, *Antiquity*** ("Sembiran and Pacung … strategic crossroads for early trans-Asiatic exchange").
- This is **~600 years before the first inscriptions (~400 CE)** — solid, peer-reviewed evidence that Nusantara communities participated in trans-Asiatic exchange well before the textual horizon. **Strong support for the *peradaban vulkanik* CHARACTER claim** (sophisticated, connected society pre-400 CE). Caveat: shows trade *contact* + a trading post, not by itself a large indigenous polity; it is Bali (not Java interior).
- Still-to-verify (lower priority): Gilimanuk (Bali), Plawangan/Pacinan (Java N coast) early-metal-age beads.
