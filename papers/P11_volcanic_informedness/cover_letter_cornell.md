**Title:** Temple Siting as Archaeological Proxy: Using Candi Distribution Patterns to Predict Buried Sites in Volcanic Java

**Authors:** Mukhlis Amien and Go Frendi Gunawan
**Affiliation:** Lab Data Sains, Universitas Bhinneka Nusantara, Malang, Indonesia
**Corresponding author:** Mukhlis Amien (amien@ubhinus.ac.id)

**Summary:**
This paper demonstrates that the spatial distribution of 142 Hindu-Buddhist temples (*candi*) in Java encodes information about past settlement patterns that can be used to predict the locations of buried archaeological sites. We show that candi cluster on western volcanic flanks (Rayleigh *p* = 3.4 x 10^-8), that this volcanic proximity is specific to sacred architecture rather than general settlement geography (Mann-Whitney *p* = 5.2 x 10^-8 vs. inscriptions), and that volcanic sedimentation buries sites at measurable rates (mean 3.41 m). An critical regression controlling for survey intensity confirms the volcanic-zone site deficit is substantive (*p* = 0.0015). We derive ten fieldwork candidates with predicted burial depths of 7-21 m.

**Relevance to Indonesia:**
The manuscript addresses a fundamental gap in the archaeology of Java — the systematic invisibility of sites in volcanic terrain — and proposes a practical, data-driven methodology for directing future fieldwork. It engages with Indonesian archaeological heritage, Dutch colonial documentation, and comparative evidence from Japan, making it well suited for *Indonesia*'s interdisciplinary readership.
