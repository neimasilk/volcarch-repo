# Notes

## Core claim (1 sentence)
Two glass eye beads found in a Northern Wei tomb in Datong, China are chemically and morphologically identified as Jatim beads originating from Java, providing direct archaeometric evidence of Javanese glass production and trans-Eurasian export by the mid-5th century CE.

## Evidence presented
- DX-1: 10.3 g, 1.6 cm diameter; dark blue matrix with white double-ring eye sockets (millefiori technique)
- DX-2: 10.8 g, 1.8 cm diameter; dark blue with red+white outlined eyes, yellow/blue eyeballs
- Blue matrix: Na₂O 9.3–17.0 wt%, CaO 3.5–6.8 wt%, plant-ash flux (K₂O+MgO > 1.5%) = **v-Na-Ca**
- Eye regions: high Al₂O₃ (up to 14 wt%) = **m-Na-Al**
- Mixed v-Na-Ca + m-Na-Al system with eye-bead morphology + specific colorants = Javanese Jatim signature
- Colorants: Co²⁺ (low-MnO₂, Mediterranean-sourced), SnO₂ (tin white, Roman tradition), Cu₂O (red, not yet in Chinese production 398-494 CE), PbSnO₃ (lead-tin yellow, post-4th c. Roman)
- Compared against Corning Glass Works standards for calibration
- Parallel Jatim bead find cited: Sikrichong Tomb, Gyeongju, Korea (Lankton & Bernbaum)

## Methodological strengths
- Non-destructive analytical approach
- Comparison against international standards (Corning)
- Multiple lines of evidence: chemistry + morphology + colorants + comparable finds
- Explicit trace-element discussion (MnO₂ as proxy for cobalt source)
- Proper chronological sequencing of colorant technologies

## Methodological weaknesses / critiques
- Tomb date is given as 398–494 CE (Dynasty range); no specific C14 or stylistic dating narrows this 96-year window. A more precise date would strengthen chronological claim.
- Attribution to "Kingdom of Java" is geographic label; paper does not identify a specific Javanese polity or workshop site.
- Sample size n=2; claims about the production network depend on comparison to broader Jatim corpus not fully detailed in this paper.
- Lack of Sr/Nd isotope analysis (which would further constrain raw material sources).
- **Important caveat I identified from other SLR reading:** m-Na-Al glass in SE Asia is typically South Asian-derived (Wang et al. 2021, 2023). Jia et al.'s Java attribution CANNOT rest on m-Na-Al alone; the case depends on the MIXED system + morphology + specific colorants. Paper could have been more explicit about why this particular combination is diagnostic of Java rather than, say, secondary reworking in South or Southeast Asian workshops generally.

## Chronological implications
- Traditional Jatim dating: 400–900 CE (Lankton), often 600–800 CE
- This paper confirms presence in a China-located deposit dated 398–494 CE
- Earliest plausible Jatim production: by mid-5th century CE at latest
- Overlaps with the very period VOLCARCH claims substrate civilisation existed in Java
