Dear Dr Castillo,

I am writing to propose a collaboration that combines your expertise in Southeast Asian archaeobotany with a computational framework I have developed for predicting where pre-Hindu archaeological sites lie buried beneath volcanic deposits in Java.

**The opportunity:** Your pioneering work on carbonised rice at Liyangan (Castillo 2014) demonstrated that organic material survives in volcanic contexts. My VOLCARCH framework — currently under review at Archeologia e Calcolatori and five other journals — predicts that pre-400 CE agricultural settlements in volcanic Java are buried at 3–7 m depth, invisible to conventional survey. The framework identifies 20 GPS-precise target locations ranked by burial depth and settlement probability.

**What has not been done:** No one has systematically looked for phytoliths in pre-Hindu volcanic matrices in Java. Your own Liyangan work recovered carbonised rice, but the enclosing volcanic matrix itself was not analysed for phytolith content. The literature confirms that phytoliths survive >90,000 years in tephra (Aso caldera studies), and Java's andisol pH (5–7) is favourable for phytolith preservation.

**The proposed test:** Extract phytolith samples from (a) existing Liyangan excavation matrix stored at Balai Arkeologi Yogyakarta, or (b) PVMBG volcanic cores from East Java. If rice or cultivar phytoliths are found in pre-Hindu strata, this would be the first direct evidence of agriculture in volcanic Java before the inscriptional record — a transformative finding for Indonesian archaeology.

**What I bring:** Computational site prediction (20 targets with GPS coordinates, depths, and explicit falsification conditions), a 186-experiment analytical framework, spatial modelling tools, and an arXiv preprint on linguistic evidence for pre-Hindu cultural substrates (arXiv:2604.00023).

**What I would ask:** Your expertise in phytolith extraction and identification, and your existing network with Balai Arkeologi Yogyakarta (particularly for Liyangan matrix access). The analysis itself is low-cost (~$5K for extraction and microscopy) and could be funded through an ARC Linkage application or similar.

I appreciate that unsolicited collaboration proposals require scepticism. I would be happy to share the full computational framework, prediction maps, and target coordinates for your independent evaluation before any commitment.
