## The Argument

P5 argues that ritual practices preserve pre-Indic cosmology beneath Sanskrit overlay. E112 and E058 now QUANTIFY this with a domain penetration gradient:

| Domain | Native % | Sanskrit % | Status |
|--------|:---:|:---:|---|
| Agriculture | **91%** | 9% | Indigenous fortress |
| Technology/Craft | **82%** | 18% | Indigenous fortress |
| Nature/Environment | **76%** | 24% | Indigenous strong |
| Body/Medicine | **68%** | 32% | Indigenous strong |
| Kinship/Social | **62%** | 38% | Indigenous strong |
| Religion/Ritual | 14% | **86%** | Sanskrit dominated |

**Sanskrit conquered religion (86%) but failed at agriculture (91% native).**

This directly supports P5: ritual vocabulary is the MOST Sanskritized domain, which is why pre-Indic elements (hyang, slametan) are remarkable — they survived the strongest pressure zone.

## E113: No Learning Curve

Inscription sophistication analysis (112 dated inscriptions) shows no learning curve — the earliest inscriptions (C7-C8) are already fully sophisticated. Formulaic density is stable across centuries (p=0.42). The Canggal inscription (732 CE) achieves Guiraud=17.89, higher than any century's mean.

**Implication:** The literary/ritual tradition was already mature before stone inscription. The organic-media tradition that P5 reconstructs from hyang/slametan evidence was likely accompanied by organic-media writing.
