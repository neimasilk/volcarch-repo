I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"What Ancient Texts Remember and Inscriptions Forget: Transformer-Based Evidence for Volcanic Taphonomic Bias in Indonesian Textual Records,"** for consideration as a research article in *Wacana*.

The paper sits squarely within *Wacana*'s scope at the intersection of **Indonesian linguistics, philology, and archaeology**. It is, to my knowledge, the **first application of transformer-based NLP (Sentence-BERT, BERTopic) to Old Javanese epigraphy**, using the open DHARMA EpiDoc corpus of Old Javanese, Sanskrit, and Old Malay inscriptions. Three findings are reported: (1) twelve external textual traditions converge semantically on Nusantaran themes; (2) the indigenous inscriptional record exhibits a measurable "volcanic silence"—volcanic-landscape themes score lowest in similarity while sacred-mountain discourse scores highest, a pattern robust in the original Old Javanese; and (3) a statistically significant discursive shift accompanies the 929 CE Mataram collapse. The contribution is interpretive as well as methodological: it argues that this silence reflects **genre selection**, not ignorance—a form of taphonomic bias operating at the level of discourse.

The manuscript is written in English (British spelling, -ize variant), follows the author-date reference system, and the abstract is within the 150-word limit. All data (the cross-tradition corpus and analysis scripts) and the DHARMA inscriptions are openly available; the methods are fully reproducible.

I confirm that this manuscript is **original, not previously published, and not under consideration elsewhere**. The work is single-authored with no competing interests and received no specific funding.
