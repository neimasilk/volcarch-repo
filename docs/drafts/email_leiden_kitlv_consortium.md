I am writing to explore a potential research collaboration between your group and our VOLCARCH project at Universitas Bhinneka Nusantara (Malang, Indonesia).

VOLCARCH investigates how volcanic processes have systematically erased archaeological evidence across Java and the wider Indonesian archipelago. Over 175 documented computational experiments, we have established that:

- Hindu-Buddhist temples (candi) cluster on western volcanic flanks, marking buried settlement zones invisible to surface survey
- Inscriptions and temples occupy systematically different spatial zones, revealing two geographies of cultural production
- A five-factor taphonomic cascade model explains why Java's archaeological record is dominated by monumental remains while settlement evidence is missing

Our work draws extensively on the DHARMA epigraphic database for spatial analysis of inscription distributions. We have georeferenced 170 inscriptions and demonstrated statistically significant divergence between inscription locations and candi locations — a finding with direct implications for how we interpret the epigraphic record of pre-modern Java.

We are currently exploring international funding opportunities, including:
- **Horizon Europe Cluster 2** (cultural heritage + digital methods) — call opens 12 May 2026
- **MSCA Doctoral Networks** — deadline 24 November 2026

Both mechanisms require EU-based consortium partners. Given your expertise in Southeast Asian epigraphy and digital humanities, and VOLCARCH's computational infrastructure and Indonesian institutional access, a collaboration could be mutually productive.

We have papers currently under review at EGQSJ, JCAA, Antiquity, and Oceanic Linguistics, with a preprint on arXiv (2604.00023). Our full project repository is publicly available at github.com/neimasilk/volcarch-repo.
