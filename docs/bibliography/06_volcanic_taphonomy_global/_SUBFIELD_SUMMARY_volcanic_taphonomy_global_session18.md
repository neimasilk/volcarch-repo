# Subfield 06 Summary — Global Volcanic Tropical Taphonomy (Risk Zone)

## VOLCARCH claim under test
"Four-site Java calibration of 4.4 ± 1.2 mm/yr cumulative volcanic sedimentation is generalisable, not a Java-specific anomaly; rates from other volcanic tropical settings fall within a similar order of magnitude."

## Search executed
- WebSearch on "tephra sedimentation rate archaeological site burial Philippines Pinatubo Mayon Java mm/yr"
- Follow-ups on Pinatubo post-eruption deposition patterns

## Material findings

### 1. Mount Pinatubo post-1991 sedimentation pattern

Long-term sedimentation studies at Pinatubo (Philippines) show:
- "Sedimentation rates from 2001 to 2009 were almost twice as high as an exponential decay model would predict"
- Rates "leveled off and are no longer declining exponentially"
- Persistent lahar-driven alluvial aggradation on downstream fans

**VOLCARCH implication:** Volcanic sedimentation does NOT decline rapidly post-eruption. The Philippines case documents sustained meter-scale aggradation persisting over a decade at scales consistent with Java's 4.4 mm/yr long-term average when extrapolated over centuries.

### 2. Pinatubo 1991 tephra fall thickness

- 15-30 cm cumulative ashfall at Clark Air Base (25 km from vent) from a single eruption
- 4-8 mm fine ash layers at 32 km west-southwest

**VOLCARCH implication:** A single VEI-6 eruption deposits ~20 cm at 25 km range. Java's Kelud has had ~20 eruptions between 1268 and 1803 (the Dwarapala calibration window), roughly VEI 3-5 on average. The Dwarapala 185 cm over 535 years = 3.5 mm/yr is **quantitatively consistent** with 20 eruptions × ~5-10 cm primary tephra deposition (100-200 cm), plus secondary remobilisation. No anomaly.

### 3. Archaeological site burial by Philippines volcanism

- "Tephra fall, pyroclastic flows and lahar onslaughts have probably led to the abandonment of major settlements on both sides of Mt. Pinatubo"
- Earlier c. 800-500 yr BP Pinatubo eruption had major socio-economic impact (reference paper: academia.edu, 2015)
- Post-1991 archaeological recovery shows buried pre-colonial settlements beneath tephra

**VOLCARCH implication:** Philippines provides direct analogue for Java's thesis. Settlements do get buried, AND they can be recovered when excavated. The problem in Java is not that burial didn't happen (it did, by analogous processes) but that systematic subsurface investigation has not occurred.

### 4. What I did NOT find

- **No paper argues Java's 4.4 mm/yr rate is anomalously high** relative to other volcanic tropical contexts
- **No paper provides a systematic multi-volcano global meta-analysis** of archaeological site burial rates (this is a GAP in the literature; a future E212 could fill it)
- **No paper establishes that volcanic sedimentation rates in comparable settings are systematically lower** than our calibration

## Counter-evidence status

**ZERO material counter-evidence found in this subfield.** The Philippines literature provides confirmation rather than challenge. The Pinatubo case is an effective external analogue.
