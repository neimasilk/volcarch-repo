## 1. WHAT WE'RE LOOKING FOR

At depths of 3-10 meters below the modern surface, in volcanic sediment layers near Kelud and Arjuno-Welirang, we expect to find:

### Positive indicators (any one = discovery)
- **Charcoal fragments** — burned organic material (cooking, fire pits, land clearing)
- **Ceramic sherds** — fired clay fragments from pottery
- **Phytoliths** — microscopic silica bodies from rice, bamboo, or cultivated plants
- **Cultural layer** — dark-colored soil horizon with mixed organic/lithic content
- **Brick or stone fragments** — architectural material
- **Metal artifacts** — iron or bronze fragments

### Negative indicators (expected in volcanic sediment without human activity)
- Uniform tephra layers without cultural material
- No charcoal below the modern agricultural horizon
- No phytoliths of cultivated plants at depth
- No ceramic fragments

### What we are NOT looking for
- Complete structures (too small a sample)
- Dateable artifacts (would require excavation, not boring)
- Human remains (extremely unlikely in a 10cm-diameter core)

## 3. TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS

### Borehole method
- **Type:** Standard geotechnical cone penetration test (CPT) or mechanical auger boring
- **Diameter:** 75-100 mm (sufficient for soil sampling)
- **Depth:** 4-10 m per hole (see table above)
- **Recovery:** Continuous core samples at 50 cm intervals
- **Logging:** Photograph each core segment, log: color, texture, inclusions, depth

### Sample handling
- Each core segment placed in sealed plastic bag with label (site, depth, date)
- Store in shade, transport within 24 hours
- Do NOT wash or sieve on site — lab work later
- GPS coordinates recorded with smartphone (accuracy ~3m sufficient)

### What to look for IN THE FIELD
- **Color changes** in the core — dark layers = potential cultural horizons
- **Hard inclusions** — brick, stone, ceramic fragments
- **Charcoal** — visible black fragments
- **Odor** — burned/organic smell at depth
- **Pottery fragments** — recognizable by texture, hardness, color

### What needs LAB analysis (later, separate budget)
- Phytolith extraction (~$50/sample, 20 samples = $1,000)
- C-14 dating of charcoal (~$300/sample, 5 samples = $1,500)
- XRF of ceramic fragments (~$100/sample, 5 samples = $500)

## 5. EXPECTED OUTCOMES (from E116)

| Scenario | Probability | Meaning |
|----------|------------|---------|
| **3+ boreholes hit cultural layers** | ~30% | Strong support for VOLCARCH — buried sites exist |
| **1-2 boreholes hit cultural layers** | ~45% | Moderate support — need follow-up GPR survey |
| **0 boreholes hit cultural layers** | ~7% | Against VOLCARCH — but see caveat below |
| **Ambiguous results** | ~18% | Charcoal/phytoliths present but no clear cultural layer |

**Caveat for zero hits:** 20 boreholes of 10cm diameter sample only 0.16 m^2 total at each depth. Even if buried sites are present, the probability of a 10cm-diameter core hitting a cultural feature is low unless features are extensive. Zero hits does NOT falsify VOLCARCH — it just means the sample was too small. Follow-up would need GPR (continuous subsurface imaging).

## 6. LOGISTICS

### Company selection
- Any geotechnical company with auger boring capability (common for construction projects)
- In Malang area: look for soil testing companies serving toll road / construction projects
- Key capability: 10m depth, continuous core recovery
- Request: "kami butuh bor tanah 10 meter, 20 titik, untuk penelitian geologi"

### Permits
- No archaeological permit needed (this is soil sampling, not excavation)
- If artifacts are found: STOP boring at that hole, photograph, contact BPCB Jawa Timur
- Land access: identify landowners, request permission, explain purpose

### Timeline
- 20 boreholes at 1-2 per day = 10-20 working days
- Recommended: dry season (June-September) for access to upland sites
- Pre-survey: drive to each location, verify access, identify GPS point

### Personnel
- 1 geotechnical crew (from company)
- 1 researcher (Pak Amien) for documentation and on-site assessment
- Optional: 1 student assistant for sample handling

## 7. IF POSITIVE RESULTS

If ANY borehole hits a clear cultural layer at predicted depth:

1. **Photograph and GPS-tag** the exact location and depth
2. **Collect samples** for C-14 dating and phytolith analysis
3. **Contact BPCB Jawa Timur** — report the finding
4. **Do NOT excavate** — wait for proper archaeological permit
5. **Write up** as a short paper (Nature Communications, Antiquity, or JASREP): "First subsurface evidence of pre-Hindu occupation in volcanic East Java"
6. **This finding alone** would validate the entire VOLCARCH framework and justify GPR survey funding
