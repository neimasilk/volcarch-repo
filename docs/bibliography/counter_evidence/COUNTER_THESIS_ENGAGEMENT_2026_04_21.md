> "The idea that Indian 'influence' was responsible for the socio-political development of early Southeast Asia is now largely discredited."

**Strongest version against VOLCARCH:** Cannot be constructed. Coedès's primary-civilization framing is no longer defended by contemporary SE Asia scholars. We would be steelmanning a dead horse.

**Implication:** VOLCARCH is **aligned with consensus**, not against it. P0 should NOT frame itself as overturning Coedès — that was done 30 years ago.

## 2. Pollock 2006 — Sanskrit Cosmopolis

**Stated position:** Sanskrit served as a trans-regional cosmopolitan literary-political language 300-1300 CE. Local vernacularization came later as reaction.

**Scholarly critiques found:**
- **Tieken:** Vernacularization "started much earlier" than Pollock assumed (especially in Tamil)
- **Hatcher:** Cosmopolitan/vernacular binary too stark, misses hybridity
- **SE Asia specialists:** Pollock's framework inadequate for SE Asia — *"Southeast Asia is excluded from the discursive delimitation of Bhāratavarṣa"* in Sanskrit geography itself

**Strongest version against VOLCARCH:** 

*"Pollock's cosmopolis did operate in Java (Sanskrit inscriptions from 400 CE onward). Before the Sanskrit cosmopolis reached Java, there was no 'civilization' in the textual-political sense — there were agrarian communities. VOLCARCH's 'erased substrate civilization' conflates population with political organization. The 1-2M inhabitants at 400 CE could have been pre-state farmers, not a 'civilization' in any meaningful sense. The archaeological absence reflects this lack of state formation, not taphonomic erasure."*

**Assessment of strongest version:**
- ✓ Valid kernel: "substrate ≠ civilization" terminology critique. VOLCARCH may over-claim by using "civilization."
- ✗ Fails quantitatively: 1-2M people, even as "pre-state farmers," produce shell middens, burial sites, pottery scatters. Philippines at 200-500K has hundreds. The gap holds regardless of "civilization" vocabulary.
- ✗ Pollock himself excludes SE Asia from his primary framework — so arguing "before Sanskrit there was nothing" is not actually Pollock's claim.

**Action for P0:** Tone down "civilization" language where possible. "Pre-Hindu Austronesian society" or "pre-literate substrate communities" may be more defensible. Reserve "civilization" for contexts where organizational complexity is evidenced (e.g., Buni metallurgy, trans-Eurasian bead trade).

## 3. Wolters 1999 — Localization as Primary Process

**Stated position:** Southeast Asian societies had strategic agency. They *actively* appropriated Indic/Sinic elements and adapted them locally. Localization = primary process.

**Key quote (paraphrased from multiple sources):**
> "Elements of Indic/Sanskritic, Sinitic, or Islamicate high culture would have been appropriated by the natives of Southeast Asia and 'localized'—that is, adapted to the prevalent place-specific socio-cultural and linguistic coordinates."

> "Wolters insisted on Southeast Asian agency and its dynamic processes."

**Strongest version against VOLCARCH:** **CANNOT BE CONSTRUCTED AS COUNTER.**

Wolters' framework **presupposes** a pre-existing local substrate with its own "place-specific socio-cultural and linguistic coordinates" — which is exactly what VOLCARCH argues existed and is archaeologically erased in volcanic Java. Wolters provides the **theoretical framework for substrate existence**. VOLCARCH provides **quantitative evidence for its scale and taphonomic concealment**.

**Implication:** Wolters is not counter-thesis. Wolters is **theoretical foundation** for VOLCARCH. Session 18 absorption of Ali 2011 into "compatible" was not absorption — it was accurate alignment.

**Action for P0:** Cite Wolters 1999 explicitly as theoretical foundation in §1 or §3. Do not treat him as opposition.
