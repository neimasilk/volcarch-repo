# Pre-Mortem: What If VOLCARCH Is Wrong?

**Date:** 2026-03-20
**Purpose:** Intellectual honesty exercise. Identify the strongest possible arguments AGAINST the VOLCARCH thesis, classify their severity, and determine what evidence would settle each one.

---

## The Thesis (Steel-Manned)

"The absence of pre-400 CE archaeology in volcanic Java reflects taphonomic loss (burial + decay + survey deficit), not genuine cultural absence. A multiplicative cascade of 5 factors produces ~99.97% invisibility."

## The Strongest Counter-Arguments

### Counter 1: "Maybe people just didn't live in volcanic interiors"

**Argument:** Early Austronesian settlers were coastal/riverine. Volcanic interiors were genuinely sparsely populated before Hindu-period wet rice intensification. The "3,220× gap" uses carrying capacity estimates that assume agricultural settlement, but pre-400 CE Javanese may have been maritime foragers concentrated on coasts.

**Severity:** HIGH — this would eliminate the need for a taphonomic explanation

**What would settle it:**
- Find pre-400 CE material culture in volcanic interior contexts (GPR, deep coring)
- If NOT found after systematic subsurface survey → the counter-argument wins
- The West Java smoking gun (Buni/Batujaya) partially addresses this — people DID live on Java pre-400 CE — but those are COASTAL sites, not volcanic interior

**Current evidence weight:** 60/40 in VOLCARCH's favor. Buni Complex proves pre-Hindu Javanese society existed. But the specific claim about INTERIOR occupation is still hypothesis.

**Honest assessment:** We cannot distinguish between "buried" and "never there" without digging. This is the fundamental limitation.

---

### Counter 2: "Your sedimentation rates come from monuments, not settlements"

**Argument:** The 4.4 ± 1.2 mm/yr rate is calibrated from large stone temples. Monuments may trap sediment differently (vertical surfaces deflect lahars, stone foundations create eddies, elevated platforms accumulate differently than flat village sites). Actual settlement burial rates could be 2× higher or 0.5× lower.

**Severity:** MODERATE — changes the numbers but not the logic

**What would settle it:**
- Calibrate rates from NON-monumental contexts (borehole transects, trench profiles in known settlement areas)
- E083 independent dataset (51 pairs, mean 3.41m) partially addresses this — but most of those are also monumental contexts
- If non-monument rates are 0.5× lower → pre-400 CE deposits at 2-5m, still below surface survey

**Current evidence weight:** The monument bias is real but the ORDER OF MAGNITUDE is solid. Even at 2 mm/yr, pre-400 CE deposits are at 3.2m — still invisible to surface survey.

---

### Counter 3: "The cascade model is unfalsifiable — you chose parameters to match data"

**Argument:** Any N factors can be parameterized to multiply to any desired product. The cascade model has 5 free parameters (P1-P5) and 1 data point (observed gap). With 5 degrees of freedom and 1 constraint, you can ALWAYS find a solution. This is curve-fitting, not science.

**Severity:** HIGH — this is epistemologically valid

**What would settle it:**
- The model must predict something NOT YET OBSERVED. If we increase survey intensity (F3) by 10× in a specific area, the model predicts 10× more sites found. This is testable.
- E115 Monte Carlo shows the model is robust to parameter variation — but robustness ≠ correctness
- The West Java control is the best defense: the model predicts that removing F1 (volcanic burial) increases visibility. In non-volcanic West Java, visibility IS higher. This is a correct out-of-sample prediction.

**Current evidence weight:** The model is DESCRIPTIVE (explains known data) not PREDICTIVE (predicts unknown data). It becomes predictive only when fieldwork tests its predictions. E115 shows it's robust, which is necessary but not sufficient.

**Honest assessment:** The cascade model is useful as a framework for organizing thinking about multiple factors. It is NOT a formal statistical model in the hypothesis-testing sense. Papers should present it as such.

---

### Counter 4: "Survey deficit is the real story — volcanic burial is a minor player"

**Argument:** Your own data shows survey coverage (40× leverage) dominates volcanic burial (1.7×). Indonesia's archaeological infrastructure is inadequate regardless of volcanism. Non-volcanic Kalimantan and Sumatra are ALSO poorly surveyed. The volcanic angle is a distraction from the real issue: Indonesia needs more archaeology, period.

**Severity:** MODERATE-HIGH — this doesn't falsify VOLCARCH but marginalizes it

**What would settle it:**
- Compare survey-normalized discovery rates across volcanic and non-volcanic regions of Java. E069 already does this (p=0.0015 after controlling for survey proxies). The volcanic signal persists.
- But the critique has a point: if Indonesia surveyed at Japan-level intensity, most pre-Hindu sites would be found with or without the volcanic framework. VOLCARCH's contribution is TARGETING, not necessity.

**Current evidence weight:** The critique is partially correct. VOLCARCH's value is not "volcanic burial explains everything" (it doesn't — 1.7× leverage). Its value is "volcanic burial is the only spatially predictable factor, enabling targeted recovery." This reframe is already in E110 and P1 EGQSJ.

---

### Counter 5: "The DHARMA inscription analysis is circular"

**Argument:** You use inscriptions (C8-C13) to infer that pre-inscription (pre-C7) civilization was organic-dominant. But inscriptions are by definition the product of literate, Indianized societies. Using Indianized evidence to characterize pre-Indic culture is circular.

**Severity:** MODERATE — valid for substrate arguments, not for sedimentation rates

**What would settle it:**
- This critique applies to P5, P8, P9, P18 (inscription-based inferences about pre-Hindu culture)
- It does NOT apply to P1 (sedimentation rates) or P2 (settlement model)
- The rebuttal: inscriptions mention organic materials (kayu, bambu, atap) in contexts of CONTINUITY — the materials didn't change with Indianization, the writing system did. E040's 63.4% organic mention rate describes CONTEMPORARY practice (C8-C13), from which pre-C7 practice is inferred as AT LEAST as organic
- But the inference IS an inference, not data

**Honest assessment:** The inscription-based arguments are strong for demonstrating that C8-C13 Javanese culture was heavily organic. The EXTRAPOLATION to pre-C7 is hypothesis, supported by the Austronesian comparative framework (E058: agriculture 91% native vocabulary → indigenous economic base predates Sanskrit overlay) but not directly by data.

---

### Counter 6: "The 3,220× gap uses unreliable population estimates"

**Argument:** E108 uses ethnographic carrying capacity analogues (Headland & Reid 1989, Kirch 2000, Bray 1986) applied to pre-400 CE Java. These estimates span an order of magnitude (590K-3.9M). The "3,220× gap" uses the moderate estimate. If actual population was 10× lower (59K), the gap drops to 322×, which is less dramatic.

**Severity:** MODERATE — changes magnitude, not direction

**What would settle it:**
- Even at 59K population (extreme minimum), the gap is 322× — still enormous
- Better population estimates require archaeological data (the thing we're trying to explain the absence of)
- This is inherently circular: we need data to estimate population, but the absence of data IS the problem

**Current evidence weight:** The GAP is real at any reasonable estimate. Whether it's 322× or 32,200× doesn't change the conclusion that taphonomic explanation is needed. E115 Monte Carlo shows the cascade model works across the full range.

## The Honest Bottom Line

VOLCARCH is a well-constructed hypothesis with a genuinely novel quantitative framework. It is NOT proven — it is proposed. The strongest evidence is the sedimentation rate calibration (physical measurements, reproducible). The weakest evidence is everything about pre-400 CE civilization (inferred, not observed). The project would be ENORMOUSLY strengthened by a single positive GPR result. Without fieldwork, it remains a framework — valuable, publishable, but not confirmed.

This is OK. The project's contribution is the framework itself, not the confirmation of its predictions. That's what the papers should claim: "We provide a method for identifying where to dig. Whether what we predict is there requires digging."
