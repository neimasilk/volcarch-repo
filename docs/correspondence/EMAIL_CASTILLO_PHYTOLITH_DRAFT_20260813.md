Dear Dr Castillo,

I write about a question where I believe one of your core skills could be decisive, and to ask
whether a small feasibility discussion would interest you.

The question: is the near-absence of pre-400 CE settlements in Java's archaeological record a
problem of burial, or of genuine absence? My group has spent three years building a computational
case that volcanic sedimentation and colonial-era recording bias systematically hide the settlement
record of volcanic Java — a programme of 214 documented experiments, two datasets now on Zenodo,
and three manuscripts in review. But we are honest that no amount of computation can settle this.
What would settle it is the kind of analysis you pioneered at Liyangan (Castillo 2014): direct
microbotanical evidence — phytoliths and starch — from the matrices that should preserve a
dispersed, swiddening or arboricultural pre-Indic population if one existed.

To our knowledge, no phytolith/starch study has yet been done on any prehistoric or early-historic
volcanic matrix in Java — not on the Liyangan matrices themselves (the enclosing deposits were not
analysed for microbotanical content), nor on any core from the volcanic lowlands. The distinction
matters because a dispersed cultivation economy would appear in starch and phytoliths even where it
leaves no pollen signal and no monumental record.

The concrete test, if feasible: microbotanical analysis of (a) stored Liyangan excavation matrix at
Balai Arkeologi Yogyakarta, or (b) one new soil core from a predicted high-priority cell in East
Java. The result would be the first direct evidence — positive or negative — on agriculture in
volcanic Java before the inscriptional record. Our side contributes the prediction framework, a
pre-registered target registry (five published predictions with explicit falsification conditions),
and all spatial data; the analysis and its costs would be the subject of this discussion, not a
demand.

I understand unsolicited proposals merit scepticism; I would gladly share the framework and
predictions for your independent scrutiny first.
