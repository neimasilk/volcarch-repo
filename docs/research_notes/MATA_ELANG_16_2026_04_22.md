## 1. STRUCTURAL DIAGNOSIS

### 1A. Proxy-overstacking without physical anchor

Current P0 v0.4 architecture rests on six evidence channels. All six are proxies:

| Channel | Data type | Physical anchor? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Sedimentation | Calibration from 4 monument sites | Indirect: depths of known elite structures |
| 2 Philippines comparandum | Published Philippine archaeology literature | Indirect: comparative inference |
| 3 Linguistic | Dictionary corpora, kakawin texts | Indirect: lexical reconstruction |
| 4 Genomic | Modern WGS + published Wallacean aDNA | Indirect: lineage modelling, preservation pattern |
| 5 Colonial archive | OV 1925–1949 + Delpher | Indirect: historical-text NLP |
| 6 Archaeometric | Extra-Javanese glass + bronze finds | Indirect: provenance attribution |

**None of the six channels constitutes a direct observation of a pre-Hindu settlement in Java's volcanic interior.** Every channel is a second-order argument: "this pattern is consistent with erased settlement." Reviewers keep demanding: where is the first-order observation?

The honest answer: we don't have one. Not because it's impossible to get — but because we haven't tried to. Our tools are inference tools. We've been running them harder.

### 1B. Ambition/foundation mismatch

Landmark papers in archaeology — Hodder at Çatalhöyük, Sheets at Cerén, Parcak on Egyptian buried pyramids, Evans on Angkor LiDAR, de Souza on Amazonian geoglyphs — share a structural feature: **new physical observation** at scale, presented in a new conceptual frame. The synthesis follows the discovery; it does not substitute for it.

Our current architecture attempts the opposite: synthesis (research statement v4.3 + cascade model + 6 channels) without the discovery that would make the synthesis load-bearing. This is the root reason reviewers sense something is missing. They are not wrong.

The analogous position in late-1990s Cambodia archaeology would have been: "We have strong textual, comparative, linguistic, and environmental reasons to believe a complex urban system lay beneath Angkor's jungle; we catalogue six channels of consistent evidence." The papers would still have been rejected. The LiDAR flights of 2012 changed everything. Our project needs its LiDAR-equivalent.

Each of these is AI-leverageable in hours-to-days on existing hardware. None requires external collaborator. Each carries real discovery potential.

### 1D. Echo chamber is structural, not yet addressed

Seven rejections of VOLCARCH papers (five actual editorial + three cross-model simulation on P0; plus three on P1 v3/v4/v5). Each rejection has flagged, in some form, the single-author-plus-AI structure. Reviewers can smell it even when they don't articulate it.

Domain co-author pursuit is slow (PhD tracks, Castillo, Leiden contacts) — not fully within Claude's remit. Acknowledging. But: running diamond-hunts *without* a co-author, then approaching co-authors *with* striking new results in hand, inverts the approach leverage. Today we ask co-authors to endorse our synthesis. Tomorrow we could offer them a specific new discovery to co-author.

### 1E. Cascade model is cognitive overhead without scientific purchase

E176 already showed: 3-factor models also bracket data; 83.8% of random 5-factor parameterisations also fit. We demoted cascade to "diagnostic not predictive." But it still occupies ~3pp in every paper, still functions as a rhetorical scaffolding, still invites reviewer ridicule (v0.4 DeepSeek: "gives a false impression of quantitative rigor").

Recommendation: cascade model RETIRED from papers going forward. It lives on as an internal heuristic (where does leverage sit?) and a historical footnote. Public-facing documents lead with the six evidence channels as parallel converging lines, not as a multiplicative cascade.

### 1F. "Always a reason to reject" saturation

User's own observation. Correct. Peer reviewers will always find something. The question for a research program is: **which rejections indicate genuine deficiency, which are noise?**

Pattern across 10 rejections/cross-model rejects on VOLCARCH papers:
- **Signal:** Every reviewer wants positive physical evidence. Three independent reviewers used variants of "where is the in situ find?" All three cross-model reviewers plus two of five editorial rejections made some version of this demand.
- **Noise:** Specific word choices, section ordering, cascade parameters. These vary reviewer-to-reviewer; no pattern.
- **Signal:** Concern about single-author computational echo chamber. Three reviews noted this indirectly.
- **Noise:** Length, formatting, specific citation gaps. Vary per review.

Conclusion: the **persistent** rejections track a real deficiency (no physical substrate, single-investigator structure); the **varying** rejections are reviewer noise and polish matters. We have been over-investing in the second category.

## 2. EPISTEMOLOGICAL AUDIT

### 2A. The unfalsifiability accusation has a kernel of truth

Every critical review has flagged unfalsifiability. Our response has been to specify falsification criteria per channel + at framework level. This closes the critique on paper but not in spirit. The spirit critique is:

> "If no excavation finds a pre-Hindu site, you will say 'we haven't dug in the right spot or deep enough.' Any negative field result can be absorbed into 'filter was more severe than estimated.' Therefore the thesis is armoured against disproof in practice."

This is partially defensible: all archaeological hypotheses about "what might be buried" face this structure. But the critique is real: **our framework as currently structured has no outcome that clearly refutes it.** Multiple GPR nulls? Maybe survey missed. Multiple coring nulls? Maybe wrong zones. Continued aDNA failure? Already predicted.

A discovery-hunt changes this. If InSAR time-series finds 50 anthropogenic subsidence signatures in predicted burial zones, the framework gains direct empirical anchoring. If it finds zero across 50,000 km² of Javanese volcanic basins, the "filter is severe but structured" argument weakens considerably.

Either outcome is informative. The current inference-stack has no equivalent binary.

### 2B. 200+ experiments ≠ 200+ tests

Honest count: across 208 registry entries, genuinely novel hypothesis tests are closer to 25–30. The rest are:
- Replications of prior tests with minor parameter changes
- FDR re-audits after new entries
- Cross-regional extensions of existing methods
- Dataset expansions feeding the same methods
- Robustness checks, Monte Carlo variants
- Post-hoc interpretations

Each has value. But the "200 experiments" framing reads as more evidential than it is. A reviewer who decodes this (Gemini has, in Session 19) notes the pattern. Honest framing in future papers: "25 independent hypothesis tests across N registry entries."

### 2C. The inference-density ceiling

There is a ceiling to what stacking inference can accomplish. Add a seventh channel, an eighth, a ninth — returns diminish, and the reviewer's "where is the positive evidence" becomes louder, not quieter. Our current architecture is at or near that ceiling.

Every additional polish round on P0/P1 buys marginal reviewer receptivity. A single striking new signal from a diamond-hunt could buy an order of magnitude more.

## 5. TESTING FRAMEWORK FOR CRITIQUE SELECTION

### 5A. Classify incoming critiques (reviewer or self-critique)

| Critique type | Response |
|---|---|
| "Need more positive physical evidence" | **ACCEPT.** This is the real deficiency. Diamond-hunts address it. No polish response. |
| "Single-investigator echo chamber" | **ACCEPT.** Pursue co-author + external collaboration. Long-timescale fix. |
| "Unfalsifiable in practice" | **PARTIAL ACCEPT.** Specify per-channel falsifiers AND add framework-level stop criteria that trigger on diamond-hunt results. |
| "Specific wording/framing issues" | **TACTICAL.** Fix in next polish pass if convenient. Do not prioritise. |
| "Methodology detail missing" | **TACTICAL.** Supplement in revision. |
| "Cascade is hand-waving" | **ACCEPT.** Retire cascade from public papers. |
