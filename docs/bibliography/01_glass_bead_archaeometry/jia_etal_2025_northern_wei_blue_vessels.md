# Notes

## Core claim (1 sentence)
Northern Wei (4th-6th c. AD) glass VESSELS recovered from Pingcheng (modern Datong) sites are shown through composition analysis to belong to the plant-ash Na₂O-CaO-SiO₂ (v-Na-Ca) system and were produced LOCALLY in Northern China, not imported.

## Evidence presented
(Abstract not available in Semantic Scholar; inferred from title, venue, and WebSearch results)
- Chemical composition analysis consistent with local raw materials
- Typological comparison with imported (Mediterranean/West Asian) glass vessels differentiates local production
- Pingcheng period (398-494 CE) as critical window for localisation of glass technology

## How it relates to VOLCARCH
**Important methodological nuance:** Same authors (Jia & Cui) here establish that v-Na-Ca glass technology was being LOCALISED in Northern China during the SAME period they (in their 2024 paper) attribute Jatim beads from the same region to Java.

This does NOT contradict the 2024 Jatim bead attribution because:
1. This paper is about VESSELS, not BEADS — different object class, different production technology
2. 2024 paper attributes beads to Java based on MIXED v-Na-Ca + m-Na-Al system + eye-bead morphology + specific colorants (copper red, lead-tin yellow with Mediterranean cobalt) — not on v-Na-Ca alone
3. Local Chinese v-Na-Ca vessels would not have the m-Na-Al decoration, eye-bead pattern, or the specific imported colorant combination

**BUT it does require P0 to be careful:** we cannot claim "v-Na-Ca = Java" as a general rule. The Java attribution depends on the full signature. This should be acknowledged explicitly in P0 §3.6.

## Chronological implications
Glass technology transfer from West/Mediterranean to China was already occurring in the 4th-6th c. CE. Pingcheng as Silk Road node absorbed both material imports (Jatim beads from Java, finished vessels from Mediterranean/West Asia) and technology transfer (local v-Na-Ca production).
