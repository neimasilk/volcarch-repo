# E092 Methodology Blueprint: Survey Techniques for Volcanically-Buried Archaeological Sites

**Purpose:** Extract lessons from worldwide comparanda to inform VOLCARCH fieldwork design in Java (Zone B/C targets from E080).

---

## 1. Survey Techniques by Burial Depth

### 0-1 m: Surface survey + test pits
- **Applicable sites:** Kuk Swamp, Mt St Helens (post-1980), Gedong Songa
- **Method:** Systematic pedestrian survey at 5-10m transect spacing; 0.5x0.5m or 1x1m test pits every 25-50m in high-probability areas
- **Cost:** ~$500-2,000 per km2 (labor-intensive, low-tech)
- **Success rate:** High for exposed or shallowly buried sites; useless if burial exceeds 1m
- **Java applicability:** Limited — E083 shows most Javanese burial depths are 1-9m. Only useful for preliminary reconnaissance or areas with erosion-exposed deposits.

### 1-3 m: GPR + systematic trenching
- **Applicable sites:** Ceren (initial discovery layers), Trowulan upper contexts, Dieng Plateau, Kelud 1919 lahar sites
- **Method:** Ground-penetrating radar (GPR) survey on grid, followed by targeted trenching at anomaly locations. GPR antenna frequency: 200-400 MHz for 1-3m penetration in volcanic soils.
- **Cost:** ~$3,000-8,000 per km2 for GPR survey; ~$15,000-40,000 per km2 for systematic trenching
- **Success rate:** Good in dry volcanic soils; poor in waterlogged or clay-rich lahar deposits (signal attenuation)
- **Caveats:** Tropical weathering of volcanic deposits reduces GPR contrast. Javanese volcanic soils (andisols) have variable water content affecting radar penetration. Need dry-season survey.
- **Key reference:** Conyers 2013 (GPR for archaeology); Goodman & Piro 2013

### 3-5 m: Mechanical augering + GPR + remote sensing
- **Applicable sites:** Sambisari (6.5m, but initial detection at shallower depth), Trowulan deep contexts, Candi Tikus (3.5m), Ceren deep layers
- **Method:** Combination approach: (1) satellite/aerial remote sensing for landscape anomalies, (2) GPR with lower frequency (100-200 MHz), (3) mechanical augering/coring at 50-100m intervals to sample stratigraphy
- **Cost:** ~$5,000-15,000 per km2 for combined survey; augering adds ~$200-500 per borehole
- **Success rate:** Moderate — depends heavily on contrast between archaeological deposits and surrounding matrix
- **Key insight from Ceren:** Payson Sheets' team used a combination of augering, trenching, and GPR to map the extent of buried structures. Success depended on knowing what volcanic deposit looked like vs. cultural fill.

### 5-10 m: Deep coring + geophysical survey
- **Applicable sites:** Herculaneum (15-25m), Prambanan deep contexts (9.14m), Kedulan (5-7m), Sambisari (6.5m)
- **Method:** Geological-scale investigation: (1) deep coring (hand auger or mechanical) at systematic intervals, (2) electrical resistivity tomography (ERT), (3) seismic refraction, (4) magnetometry for kiln/hearth detection
- **Cost:** ~$10,000-30,000 per km2 for ERT/seismic; coring adds $500-2,000 per borehole depending on depth
- **Success rate:** Low for traditional archaeology; moderate for geophysical detection of major structures
- **Key insight from Herculaneum:** Even 15-25m burial was eventually accessible through tunneling (Bourbon era) and modern excavation, but cost is extremely high. For Java, 5-10m is the critical depth — deep enough to be invisible, shallow enough to be potentially accessible.

### >10 m: Not feasible with current archaeological budgets
- **Applicable sites:** Herculaneum (15-25m), Cuicuilco (6-10m lava)
- **Note:** At these depths, only major infrastructure projects or geological investigations are likely to encounter remains. Focus VOLCARCH fieldwork on 1-5m targets.

## 3. Success Factors from Ceren, Akrotiri, and Pompeii

### 3.1 Ceren (El Salvador) — Most relevant to Java

**What made discovery possible:**
1. **Chance + construction:** Bulldozer hit buried structures during grain silo construction (1976). In Java, sand mining and construction similarly expose buried structures (cf. Kedulan 1993 discovery during sand mining).
2. **Shallow wet tephra:** 5-7m of moist TBJ-type ash preserved organic materials. Sealed rapidly without crushing structures.
3. **Known volcanic geology:** Previous geological mapping of Laguna Caldera deposits guided subsequent systematic excavation.
4. **Persistent excavation:** Sheets excavated for 30+ years with NSF funding, demonstrating that sustained commitment matters more than large initial budgets.

**Transferable lessons for Java:**
- **Monitor construction/mining activity** near target zones. Both Sambisari (1966, farmer's plow) and Kedulan (1993, sand mining) were chance discoveries during ground-disturbing activity. A reporting protocol with local construction companies could systematize this.
- **Map volcanic deposit thickness** before archaeological survey. E075's Pyle exponential sedimentation model provides predicted depths — ground-truth these with coring first.
- **Expect organic preservation** if tephra was moist at deposition. Java's tropical humidity means this is plausible.

### 3.2 Akrotiri (Santorini) — Multi-story preservation model

**What made discovery possible:**
1. **Quarrying exposed deposits:** 19th-century pumice quarrying for Suez Canal revealed artifacts.
2. **Deep pumice = structural preservation:** 2-40m of pumice sealed multi-story buildings without crushing (pumice is lightweight). Frescoes, furniture, pottery preserved in situ.
3. **Staged excavation:** Marinatos (1967+) used systematic trench-and-tunnel method adapted from mining.
4. **No human remains:** Population evacuated (earthquake warnings), proving that volcanic burial =/= sudden death. Relevant to VOLCARCH — buried sites may have been ABANDONED before burial, reducing artifact density.

**Transferable lessons for Java:**
- **Volcanic deposits vary in density:** Pumice preserves structures; dense lahar crushes them. Java's burial is mostly lahar/volcanic sediment (denser than pumice), so expect more structural damage than Akrotiri.
- **Multi-phase occupation:** Akrotiri had multiple occupation phases separated by volcanic events. Java shows the same pattern (Merapi sedimentation is ongoing, not single-event). Look for stratified occupation.
- **Evacuation hypothesis:** If communities were volcano-aware (P11 thesis), they may have evacuated before burial. Expect fewer human remains, more abandoned structures with portable valuables removed.

### 3.3 Pompeii/Herculaneum — The "known site" problem

**What made discovery possible:**
1. **Literary record:** Pliny the Younger's letters (writing to Tacitus) preserved precise eyewitness account. Subsequent centuries maintained memory of buried cities.
2. **Visible surface features:** At Pompeii, walls occasionally broke the surface. At Herculaneum, well digging (1709) encountered buried theater.
3. **State-funded excavation:** Bourbon monarchy (1748+) funded excavation as royal prestige project. Modern Italian state continues.
4. **Tourism revenue model:** Pompeii generates ~$50M+ annual tourism revenue, funding ongoing excavation. This self-sustaining model is unique.

**Transferable lessons for Java:**
- **Literary/epigraphic records matter:** VOLCARCH's inscription data (DHARMA, OV series) serves the same function as Pliny — pointing to where settlements SHOULD be even when archaeological evidence is absent. E082-E084 exploit this.
- **Surface expression:** Even deeply buried sites occasionally break the surface (Cuicuilco pyramid tip, Prambanan upper sections). Systematic surface survey of known temple clusters could identify partial exposures.
- **Sustainable funding:** Java will not have Pompeii's tourism model. Budget-constrained approaches (coring, magnetometry, satellite analysis) are essential. Phase approach: remote sensing first, then targeted geophysics, then selective excavation.

## 4. Recommended Approach for VOLCARCH Zone B/C

### Context
- **Target zones:** E080 identified 20 priority targets clustered near Kelud and Arjuno-Welirang
- **Predicted burial depth:** 5-8m at top targets (E075 Pyle model)
- **Constraints:** Tropical weathering, 1-10m burial, no prior GPR data for these specific locations, limited budget (academic, not state-funded)
- **Existing knowledge:** E083 documents 51 eruption-site correlations with 24 measured burial depths (mean 3.41m, median 2.50m)

### Phase 1: Desktop + Satellite (Cost: ~$100-500)
**Timeline:** 1-2 months. **Already partially done** (E076 satellite NDVI analysis).

1. **Satellite anomaly detection:** Continue E076 approach — use Sentinel-2 NDVI anomalies and Landsat thermal data to identify subsurface moisture/vegetation anomalies at E080 target coordinates.
2. **Historical aerial photography:** Obtain pre-1990 aerial photos from BIG (Badan Informasi Geospasial) or LAPAN. Pre-development photos may show surface features now obscured.
3. **Colonial report cross-reference:** Match E080 targets against E070 colonial register and E083 tephra-archaeological correlation. Identify if any targets overlap with historically documented sites.
4. **Local informant survey:** Talk to village elders, well diggers, brick makers, sand miners near target coordinates. Chance discoveries are how most Javanese buried temples have been found.

### Phase 2: Reconnaissance Coring (Cost: ~$2,000-5,000)
**Timeline:** 1-2 weeks fieldwork.

1. **Hand augering transects** at top 5-10 target coordinates from E080.
2. **Interval:** Every 50m along 2 perpendicular transects centered on each target point.
3. **Depth:** To 5m minimum (hand auger limit in volcanic soil), preferably 8m with mechanical extension.
4. **Record:** Stratigraphy, tephra layers, cultural indicators (charcoal, brick fragments, ceramic sherds, morite).
5. **Cost:** ~$200-500 per borehole x 20-40 boreholes = $4,000-20,000 total.
6. **Decision point:** If cultural material found in any borehole, advance to Phase 3. If nothing found in 40 boreholes, reconsider target selection methodology.

### Phase 3: Targeted Geophysics (Cost: ~$5,000-15,000)
**Timeline:** 1-2 weeks fieldwork (at sites with positive Phase 2 results).

1. **Magnetometry first:** Java's buried temples are brick and stone — high magnetic contrast with volcanic sediment. Magnetometry is cheaper and faster than GPR in volcanic soils. Cover 1-2 km2 around positive boreholes.
2. **GPR second:** If magnetometry shows anomalies, deploy GPR (200 MHz) along transects crossing anomalies. Schedule for dry season (June-September) to maximize penetration in volcanic andisols.
3. **ERT third:** If anomalies are at >3m depth, use electrical resistivity tomography for deeper profiling.
4. **Decision point:** If geophysical anomalies correlate with cultural material from coring, advance to Phase 4.

### Phase 4: Test Excavation (Cost: ~$10,000-50,000)
**Timeline:** Requires institutional partner (BALARJATIM, university).

1. **Open 2x2m test units** at strongest anomaly locations.
2. **Machine-assisted removal** of sterile volcanic overburden (if >2m), then hand excavation of cultural layers.
3. **Documentation:** Full stratigraphic recording, tephra sampling for tephrochronology, artifact recovery, C14 sampling.
4. **Permits:** Requires Indonesian research permit (SIP) and collaboration with BALARJATIM or university archaeology department.

### Budget Summary

| Phase | Activity | Cost (USD) | Already done? |
|-------|----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Satellite + desktop | 100-500 | Partially (E076) |
| 2 | Reconnaissance coring | 2,000-5,000 | No |
| 3 | Targeted geophysics | 5,000-15,000 | No |
| 4 | Test excavation | 10,000-50,000 | No |
| **Total** | **Phases 1-4** | **$17,100-70,500** | |

This phased approach minimizes cost by using each phase as a filter: only sites that pass Phase N advance to Phase N+1. Compare with Ceren's total excavation cost (~$500,000+ over 30 years) or Angkor's LiDAR (~$250,000 per campaign). The VOLCARCH approach is designed for academic-budget constraint.

### Why This Approach Should Work

1. **Ceren/Sambisari/Kedulan precedent:** All three were found by chance ground disturbance. Systematic coring replicates this process intentionally.
2. **Magnetometry advantage in Java:** Unlike Pompeii (stone) or Ceren (adobe — low magnetic contrast), Javanese temples are brick — high magnetic contrast with surrounding volcanic soil. Magnetometry may be the single best technique.
3. **Known baseline:** E083 provides 24 measured burial depths (mean 3.41m, median 2.50m) — the targets are within the 1-5m sweet spot where coring and magnetometry are effective.
4. **E080 targeting:** The scoring algorithm integrates 6+ upstream experiments, reducing the search area from all of East Java (~35,000 km2) to 20 specific points. This is the advantage of computational archaeology — reducing haystack before searching.
