Dear Dr. Lamqaddam and Dr. Martinez Pandiani,

Prof. Tobias Blanke (CC'd) kindly suggested I reach out to you regarding a PhD opportunity at UvA. He mentioned that, should the fit look right, you would be potential supervisors with himself serving as promoter.

I am Mukhlis Amien, a lecturer in Informatics at Universitas Bhinneka Nusantara (Malang, Indonesia). Over the past two years I have been building **VOLCARCH** — a computational project applying NLP and spatial machine learning to reconstruct pre-colonial Indonesian settlement patterns from Dutch East India Company (VOC) and late-colonial Dutch archives. The project currently has six papers under review (preprint: arXiv:2604.00023) and addresses three interconnected AI challenges: (1) NER and entity disambiguation on 17th–18th century Dutch with Malay code-switching, (2) spatial ML under extreme data loss (≥99.94% of expected pre-1600 archaeological signal missing due to volcanic taphonomy, per our cascade model E133), and (3) Bayesian integration of uncertain textual and spatial predictions under known systematic bias.

I was struck by the alignment with both of your agendas.

**Dr. Lamqaddam** — your recent work applying OCR and LLMs to convert historical catalogues into structured data maps directly onto the technical core of VOLCARCH's VOC pipeline: 22,000+ candidate settlement mentions awaiting cleanup, disambiguation, and GIS linkage.

**Dr. Martinez Pandiani** — your emphasis on ethical and explainable AI for cultural heritage data connects to a central concern in the project. VOLCARCH treats systematic bias in the archaeological record (colonial, volcanic, historiographic) as the object of study rather than a noise source. The question of whose knowledge gets preserved, and how computational methods can expose rather than reproduce those erasures, sits at its core.

I am currently exploring funding pathways. I understand HAICu and DEEP CULTURE are fully allocated, and I would welcome any guidance on upcoming EU grant calls where a VOC-archive + spatial-ML project might fit. As a parallel option, I intend to apply for Indonesia's **BPI Dosen** scholarship — a fully-funded PhD programme for permanent Indonesian lecturers (≈4 years, tuition + living). I am flexible on start date — October 2027 or October 2028 both workable.

Attached: my CV and a short concept note (2 pages) that sketches the project's scope, method, and preliminary results.
