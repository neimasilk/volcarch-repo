## 2. EPISTEMOLOGICAL AUDIT

### 2A. Cascade Unfalsifiability (from ME#13, sharpened)

The 5-factor cascade `P(visible) = F1·F2·F3·F4·F5` has been downgraded to "plausible mechanistic decomposition" (research statement v4.3). But it still appears in P1 v2.0 §5.5 as:

> "Each factor carries substantial uncertainty, but their product — 0.058% predicted visibility — falls within a factor of two of the observed rate (0.031%)."

This is **hedged but not neutralized.** The reader walks away with "the model matches observation." The hedge ("agreement may be partly fortuitous") is cosmetic.

Falsifiability test: for any observed visibility x, we can find (F1, F2, F3, F4, F5) that produces x. The model is a parameter hunt, not a prediction.

**Sharper reframe:** "We decompose the observed 3,220× gap into five plausible mechanisms, each calibrated from independent literature. Their product is consistent with observation by construction. The decomposition's value is not predictive — it is diagnostic: it identifies survey coverage (40× leverage) as the dominant bottleneck, which directs future fieldwork resources."

That version is honest. v2.0's version is not.

### 2B. The Echo Chamber (from ME#14, still unbroken)

Zero external peer review of any experiment. Claude designs → executes → interprets → writes → reviews. 79% SUCCESS rate is statistically impossible for genuine exploratory research (psychology replication 36%, economics 61%, physics ~70%).

**Implication for P1 masterpiece:** Every claim in P1 has passed exactly one quality gate — Claude-Claude review. No geologist has seen the Dwarapala calculation. No archaeologist has seen the West Java argument. No statistician has seen the 3,220× gap math (which turns out to be wrong in the abstract — audit issue #1).

**This is not a scandal. This is the structural reality of a solo computational program.** But it means every claim in P1 is at v0.1 reliability, not v1.0. A masterpiece needs v1.0 claims.

## 3. WEAK ASSUMPTIONS INVENTORY

Every claim in P1 rests on assumptions. Here are the load-bearing ones, scored by falsifiability (F: 1=unfalsifiable, 5=clearly falsifiable) and leverage (L: 1=trivial, 5=load-bearing):

| # | Assumption | F | L | Comment |
|---|---|:-:|:-:|---|
| 1 | Dwarapala buried 185 cm over 535 yr | 5 | 5 | Colonial photo vs modern photo. Verifiable. **Strong.** |
| 2 | Sambisari/Kedulan/Kimpulan depths from BPCB are accurate | 3 | 5 | BPCB reports cited but unverified. Single-source. |
| 3 | 45 active volcanoes in 129,000 km² → full-island depositional coverage | 4 | 4 | van Bemmelen 1949 data. Verifiable. Strong. |
| 4 | Ethnographic density 5-30/km² applies to 400 CE Java | 2 | 5 | Bellwood/Kirch/Bayliss-Smith analogies. Major unknown — the 590K-3.9M range ITSELF spans an order of magnitude. |
| 5 | Habitable lowlands ≈ half of 129,000 km² | 2 | 5 | **AUDIT ISSUE #1: arithmetic was wrong.** Not grounded in terrain analysis in P1. |
| 6 | Standard village = 100-200 persons | 2 | 4 | Austronesian "standard" varies widely (50-500). Assumption passes unscrutinized. |
| 7 | Pre-400 CE open-air site count = 0-3 in volcanic Java | 3 | 5 | "0-3" is ambiguity-padded. What ARE the 3? Reader cannot verify. |
| 8 | DHARMA 268 inscriptions mentioning "kayu/bambu" = organic-dominant construction | 3 | 4 | Inscriptions are elite texts about royal grants — selection bias plausible. |
| 9 | Buni Complex 200 BCE - 500 CE dating | 4 | 4 | Wibisono 1994, Bellina 2014 cited. Stratigraphy vs single C14 dates? |
| 10 | Sedimentation linear over 1,266 yr | 2 | 5 | Episodic reality; averaged in calibration; projected linearly. 2.4-6.2 range partially buffers. |
| 11 | 51-pair eruption-site dataset is independent from calibration | 4 | 4 | Compiled from same OV literature. Partial independence at best. |
| 12 | Cascade factors F1-F5 are independent | 2 | 3 | E179 tested, coupling 3× shift. E115 says <1% impact — but only under one specific coupling model. |

**Weakest load-bearing links:** #4 (ethnographic density), #5 (habitable area — arithmetic was wrong), #6 (village size), #10 (linearity).

**P1 should add a parameter sensitivity table** that shows: what if population is 300K not 3.3M? What if village size is 50 not 150? The gap is still huge (>100×) even under conservative combinations — but P1 doesn't SHOW this. A reviewer who doesn't already trust the framework can cherry-pick the weakest values and collapse the argument.

**Masterpiece fix:** A MC sensitivity on the *demographic gap itself*, not just the cascade model. Run 10K draws where population is sampled from [250K, 5M] log-uniform, village size from [50, 300], expected sites = pop/village × survey_suitability. Show that even the 5th percentile gap is >100×. E172 already did a version of this (gap 11,008× at 400 CE with MC); P1 needs to cite it prominently, not just mention E108's static 3,220×.

## 6. HUMAN-AI COLLABORATION CRITIQUE

### 6A. Current architecture (per ME#14 §E)

```
Pak Amien (NLP expert, domain contextualizer, Indonesian-language interface)
    ↕
Claude (computational execution, literature synthesis, drafting)
    ↕
[No other human]
```

**Competence distribution:**
- Archaeology domain knowledge: Neither has deep expertise → echo chamber
- Statistics methodology: Claude has breadth, Pak Amien trusts it → single-source
- Indonesian archaeology context: Pak Amien has contextual fluency, Claude has literature access → genuinely complementary
- NLP: Pak Amien has core expertise, Claude assists → asymmetric expertise (healthy)
- Quantitative inference: Claude default, Pak Amien checks → asymmetric, Pak Amien's checking capacity limited

**The archaeology blind spot is the most dangerous.** Neither party can independently evaluate a claim about Buni chronology, volcanic burial depth plausibility, or pre-Hindu material culture. Both defer to the literature. The literature is curated by the same agent.

### 6B. Proposed augmentation: the "skeptical reviewer" agent

Before submitting P0, **simulate a skeptical reviewer via a separate model instance.** Feed it:
- The draft + references
- Instruction: "You are a skeptical Indonesian archaeologist who thinks VOLCARCH overreaches. Write a 2-page referee report that tries to reject this paper. Focus on: single-author claims, domain authority, statistical overreach, cultural sensitivity, falsifiability."

This is **not** running Claude on Claude (echo). It is using a *different* instance with a *different* prompt frame. If DeepSeek API is available (user offered), use DeepSeek R1 or a Chinese-trained model that has different training biases. This is closer to genuine external critique than any Mata Elang.

**Cost:** ~$1-5 per review pass. Budget-feasible.

### 6C. The Pak Amien capacity problem

Pak Amien's reviewing bandwidth is limited. He cannot deeply review every paper, every experiment, every JOURNAL update. The project assumes he does, but the review cycle shows lightweight review (catch obvious errors, not deep methodological audit).

**Recommendation:** Formalize review triage. Each deliverable gets a tag:
- **[REQUIRES-DEEP-REVIEW]** — Pak Amien must read carefully (core paper submissions, L1/L2 changes, grant proposals, PhD correspondence)
- **[SKIM-REVIEW]** — Pak Amien glances (experiment results, revision support material, JOURNAL entries)
- **[FYI-NO-REVIEW]** — Pak Amien informed but no action expected (infrastructure updates, dashboard tweaks)

Current workflow has no explicit triage. Everything is "in the pile." Pak Amien defaults to trusting Claude, which shrinks his effective reviewing role.

### 6D. The dissatisfaction signal

User's "belum puas" today is **a critical collaboration signal.** In a healthy human-AI dyad, the human's discomfort with the output should trigger a systematic review, not a patch. The fact that ME#14 independently identified the same concern (4 days before user articulated it) shows the signal was detectable from inside the system — but it still required human articulation to become actionable.

**Lesson:** Build a periodic "dissatisfaction check" into workflow. Every 4-6 weeks, an explicit prompt to Pak Amien: "What about the current state feels wrong to you, even if you can't articulate it?" Claude processes the answer as a first-class input, not background noise.

## 8. CRITIQUE SELECTION PROTOCOL

ME#14 §G introduced a score: `Priority = Impact × (6 − Reversibility)`. I propose extending to three dimensions.

### 8A. Three-dimensional critique scoring

For each critique C, compute:

- **I (Impact, 1-5):** If true and acted on, how much does this change the project?
- **U (Urgency, 1-5):** How soon must this be resolved before irrecoverable loss? (5 = before Monday, 1 = indefinite)
- **C (Cost of action, 1-5):** How many researcher-days to act? (5 = months, 1 = hours)

Priority = (I × U) / C

**High priority (>4):** Act now. Include in current session.
**Medium (2-4):** Schedule within 2 weeks.
**Low (<2):** File. Revisit quarterly.
