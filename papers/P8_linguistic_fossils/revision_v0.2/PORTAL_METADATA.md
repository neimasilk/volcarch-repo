Uncoded basic vocabulary in six Sulawesi word lists

**Abstract** (159 words; identical to the manuscript)
In the Austronesian Basic Vocabulary Database (ABVD), part of every Sulawesi word list carries no cognate-set assignment, often read as substrate. We ask what the absence of a cognate-set number shows, using 1,357 forms of six ABVD lists from four Sulawesi subgroups, 438 of them (32.3 percent) uncoded. A classifier given the written form and the meaning ranks uncoded above coded forms (area under the curve 0.73) but barely improves on calling every form coded. Uncoded forms are about one character longer, more often carry a written glottal mark and more often denote actions. Makasar has more uncoded meanings than Bugis and Sa'dan Toraja (34.8 against 23.4 and 19.0 percent), and few of them resemble its relatives' forms; 70.9 percent of Tolaki's uncoded forms have a look-alike of the same meaning elsewhere in Bungku-Tolaki (10.5 percent by chance). Coding status is not origin: a set number is not a finding of inheritance, nor its absence one of non-Austronesian descent.
