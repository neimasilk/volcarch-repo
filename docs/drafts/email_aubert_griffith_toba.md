Dear Professor Aubert,

I write to congratulate you and your team on the extraordinary Liang Metanduno finding — 67,800-year-old rock art that rewrites the timeline of symbolic behaviour in Island Southeast Asia (Oktaviana et al. 2026, *Nature*).

Your discovery raises a question that I believe computational modelling can help answer: **why did art-producing humans survive in Sulawesi while *Homo soloensis* in Java — only 1,200 km closer to Toba — apparently did not?**

I am a researcher at Universitas Bhinneka Nusantara (Malang, Indonesia) leading the VOLCARCH project, which studies how volcanic processes have systematically erased archaeological evidence across Java. We have 175 documented experiments quantifying volcanic taphonomic bias in the Indonesian record, with papers currently under review at EGQSJ, JCAA, Oceanic Linguistics, and Antiquity.

One of our proposed extensions is **TobaSim-Nusantara**: a high-resolution FALL3D simulation of the Toba 74 ka eruption across Sundaland paleotopography (sea level −120 m), specifically modelling differential ashfall thickness between Java and Sulawesi. Our compute infrastructure (4× RTX 4080, 128 GB RAM) is ready, but we lack the volcanological and palaeoanthropological expertise to properly calibrate eruption source parameters and interpret survival thresholds.

The complementarity seems natural:
- **Your team** brings the archaeological evidence of post-Toba human survival in Sulawesi, plus deep expertise in dating and site context
- **Our team** brings computational dispersal modelling, the VOLCARCH taphonomic framework, and Indonesian institutional access (PVMBG, Balai Arkeologi contacts)

Would you be open to a conversation about whether this collaboration might be productive? I would be happy to share our TobaSim proposal document and any of our current working papers.

A joint project could also be well-positioned for an ARC Linkage or similar international funding mechanism, given the Indonesia–Australia research corridor.

Thank you for your time, and again — congratulations on a truly landmark discovery.
