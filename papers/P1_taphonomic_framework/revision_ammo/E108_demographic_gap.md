## The Critique

"Maybe there's no pre-400 CE archaeology because there weren't many people in Java before the Hindu period."

## The Response

**E108 (Demographic Null Model) rejects this hypothesis quantitatively.**

Multi-scenario carrying capacity model for Java ~200 BCE - 400 CE:

| Scenario | Population | Expected Settlements | Known Sites |
|----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| A (minimal) | 590,520 | 2,953–11,810 | 0–3 |
| B (moderate) | 1,931,730 | 9,659–38,635 | 0–3 |
| C (maximum) | 3,910,200 | 19,551–78,204 | 0–3 |

**The gap between expected and observed is 3,220× (moderate estimate).**

Even the most conservative scenario (590K, subsistence farming dominant) exceeds contemporaneous:
- Thailand Dvaravati: 300–500K
- Philippines: 200–500K
- Sriwijaya core: 50–150K

**Sensitivity:** Without wet rice (conservative assumption): still 1,452,930. Gap still > 1,500×.

## Suggested Paragraph

> "A conceivable objection is that pre-400 CE Java simply had too few inhabitants to generate a detectable archaeological record. However, demographic modeling using ethnographic population density analogues (Bayliss-Smith 1980; Kirch 2000) and Java's ecological parameters estimates a carrying capacity of 590,000 to 3,900,000 — comparable to contemporaneous Dvaravati Thailand and exceeding the Philippines or Sriwijaya. Even the most conservative estimate implies 2,953 to 11,810 settlements. The 3,220-fold gap between expected and observed sites cannot be explained by low population; it requires a taphonomic explanation."
