# E112: Vocabulary Archaeology — Computational Reconstruction of Pre-Hindu Nusantara

**Date:** 2026-03-17
**Paper:** P18 (Invisible Civilization)
**Status:** SUCCESS — Indigenous writing concept confirmed, cultural profile reconstructed

## Hypothesis

Surviving vocabulary can serve as archaeological artifacts to reconstruct an invisible civilization. If physical remains are destroyed, the words that described material and cognitive culture persist in descendant languages.

## Method

Three sub-experiments:
- **A) Ghost Writing Detector:** Search ABVD (346,662 forms) for reflexes of PAN \*surat and PMP \*tulis
- **B) Cultural Reconstruction:** Classify 438 substrate words (E027) into 9 activity domains
- **C) Domain Stratification:** Map Sanskrit penetration across domains (from E058 kakawin analysis)

## Results

### A) Ghost Writing
- PAN \*surat ("to write/mark") — reconstructable to Proto-Austronesian (~5000 BP)
- PMP \*tulis ("to write/draw") — reconstructable to Proto-Malayo-Polynesian (~4000 BP)
- Indigenous words for PROCESS (tulis, surat); Sanskrit words for PRODUCTS (aksara, pustaka)
- **Writing concept predates Indian contact by 2,000-3,000 years**

### B) Cultural Profile
Top domains in substrate vocabulary: Social/Governance 18.3%, Knowledge/Cognition 14.9%, Craft/Technology 13.7%, Spatial/Navigation 13.7%. Profile indicates complex agrarian-maritime civilization.

### C) Domain Penetration
- Sanskrit conquered: Religion (86%), War (55%), Governance (51%)
- Sanskrit failed at: Agriculture (9%), Technology (18%), Nature (24%)
- **Sanskritization was an elite phenomenon, not a cultural replacement**
