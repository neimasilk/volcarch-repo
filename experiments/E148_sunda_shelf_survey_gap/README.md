# E148: Sunda Shelf Marine Archaeological Survey Gap Analysis

**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Status:** SUCCESS
**Paper:** P1, P18
**Layer:** L2 (Coastal Submersion) — survey deficit quantification
**Mata Elang:** #11 Blind Spot B1 (L2 neglected: only 1/148 prior experiments)

---

## Hypothesis

The Sunda Shelf has received orders of magnitude less marine archaeological
survey than comparable submerged landscapes (Mediterranean shelves, North Sea
Doggerland, English Channel), creating a massive blind spot for Layer 2
(Coastal Submersion). The gap exceeds 100x.

## Motivation

E052 established that 2,089,415 km^2 of habitable land was exposed at the Last
Glacial Maximum — 16.2x Java, supporting ~500,000 people. But E052 did not ask:
*has anyone actually looked for archaeological remains under that water?*

E129 showed that 73% of known sites on LAND are temples — the survey on land is
asymmetric. This experiment asks whether the survey at SEA is even worse.

The answer quantifies the L2 survey deficit, analogous to how E110's cascade
model quantifies the L1 survey deficit (40x leverage from survey alone).

## Method

1. **Literature compilation**: Gathered published data on marine archaeological
   survey coverage for 7 regions:
   - **Benchmark regions** (decades of systematic work): Mediterranean Sea,
     North Sea (Doggerland), English Channel
   - **SE Asian regions** (near-zero settlement survey): Sunda Shelf, South
     China Sea, Strait of Malacca, Java Sea

2. **For each region**, recorded:
   - Total shelf area (km^2)
   - Number of documented marine archaeological surveys
   - Estimated total area surveyed (km^2)
   - Notable finds (distinguishing settlement vs maritime trade)
   - Survey techniques employed
   - Number of marine archaeologists (rough estimate)
   - Whether settlement archaeology (vs. shipwreck-only) has been conducted
   - Year systematic surveys began

3. **Computed**:
   - Survey coverage ratio = area surveyed / total shelf area
   - Coverage gap = benchmark coverage / Sunda coverage
   - Archaeologist density per million km^2
   - Expected site count if Mediterranean-level survey were applied
   - Years of effort needed to reach 1% Sunda coverage

4. **Honest constraints**:
   - Exact survey areas are rarely published; ranges given with reasoning
   - "Best estimate" values are CONSERVATIVE (favor smaller gaps)
   - Mediterranean site count (2500) from SPLASHCOS database, well-documented
   - Sunda Shelf survey area (~10 km^2) is GENEROUS, counting opportunistic
     finds as if they were planned surveys

### 4. Expected Sites If Mediterranean-Level Survey

- Mediterranean site density: 0.208 sites per km^2 surveyed
  (2,500 submerged sites from SPLASHCOS / 12,000 km^2 surveyed)
- If Sunda Shelf had 2% coverage: 41,788 km^2 would be surveyed
- Expected sites (direct scaling): **8,706**
- Expected sites (conservative, 0.5x for tropical taphonomy): **4,353**
- Currently known prehistoric settlement sites on Sunda Shelf: **0**

### 5. Effort Required

- Mediterranean annual survey rate: ~185 km^2/year (over ~65 years)
- Area for 1% Sunda coverage: 20,894 km^2
- Years at Mediterranean rate: **113 years**
- (This 1% target alone exceeds the entire core Doggerland area of ~23,000 km^2)

### 6. Known Marine Finds — Sunda Shelf Region

| Find | Type | Year | Significance |
|------|------|------|-------------|
| Gittins et al. hominin fossil, Madura Strait | Settlement-relevant | 2025 | First physical proof of submerged remains |
| Song Doc lithics, Vietnam offshore | Settlement-relevant | Various | Lithic artifacts near drowned coast |
| Belitung shipwreck (9th c. Arab dhow) | Maritime trade | 1998 | Not settlement archaeology |
| Cirebon shipwreck (10th c.) | Maritime trade | 2003 | Not settlement archaeology |
| Shipping lane pottery/anchors | Maritime trade | Various | Not settlement archaeology |
| Trawler finds (animal bones, possible tools) | Uncertain | Various | Unpublished, opportunistic |

**Settlement-relevant finds: 2. Maritime trade finds: 4+. Systematic settlement surveys: 0.**

The critical distinction: shipwreck archaeology (Belitung, Cirebon) searches for
TRADING VESSELS. Settlement archaeology searches for WHERE PEOPLE LIVED. These are
completely different enterprises. The Sunda Shelf has had some of the former and
none of the latter.

## Conclusion

**HYPOTHESIS CONFIRMED. STATUS: SUCCESS.**

The Sunda Shelf — Earth's largest submerged habitable landscape at 2,089,415 km^2 —
has received **4,179x less survey coverage** than the Mediterranean and **31,795x less**
than North Sea Doggerland. The gap is not merely >100x; it is >4,000x.

Key findings:

1. **Coverage ratio**: Sunda Shelf 0.0005% vs Mediterranean 2.0% vs Doggerland 15.2%.
   The Sunda Shelf has essentially ZERO systematic prehistoric settlement survey.

2. **Capacity gap**: ~1 marine archaeologist per million km^2 in SE Asia vs ~833 in
   the Mediterranean. The infrastructure to discover submerged sites does not exist.

3. **Expected sites**: If Mediterranean survey effort were applied, 4,353-8,706
   submerged prehistoric sites would be expected. Currently known: 0.

4. **Effort scale**: Reaching even 1% coverage of the Sunda Shelf would require
   113 years at Mediterranean survey rates — surveying an area larger than all
   of Doggerland.

5. **Absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence**: The Sunda Shelf record is blank
   not because nothing is there, but because nobody has looked. Gittins et al.
   2025 (hominin fossil from an OPPORTUNISTIC dredge find) hints at what systematic
   survey might reveal.

## Implications for VOLCARCH

This experiment completes the **triple survey deficit** picture:

| Layer | Deficit | Source |
|-------|---------|--------|
| L1 (Volcanic burial) | 40x survey leverage on land | E110 cascade model |
| L1 (Survey bias) | 73% of known land sites are temples | E129 |
| **L2 (Marine survey)** | **4,179x coverage gap vs Mediterranean** | **E148** |

The archaeological record of Southeast Asia is constrained to a narrow observational
window: the unburied, unsubmerged, temple-containing middle zone. Both the volcanic
highlands (L1) and the submerged lowlands (L2) are effectively unexplored.

**For P1 (EGQSJ)**: This provides quantitative ammunition for the L2 component of
the "6 Layers of Darkness" framework. The gap is not theoretical — it is measurable.

**For P18 (Invisible Civilization)**: The combination of E052 (2.09M km^2 submerged),
E129 (73% temples), and E148 (4,179x survey gap) constitutes one of the strongest
arguments in the entire VOLCARCH project: we are not merely unlucky — we have
SYSTEMATICALLY not looked.

## Limitations

1. **Exact survey areas are estimates.** Marine archaeology programs rarely publish
   total area-surveyed figures. The numbers used here are derived from published
   project descriptions, survey method papers, and institutional reports, not from
   a single authoritative database.

2. **Mediterranean numbers may be underestimates.** Some national programs (Italy,
   Greece, Turkey) have conducted surveys that are not fully represented in SPLASHCOS.
   This would make the gap LARGER, not smaller.

3. **Sunda Shelf "10 km^2" is generous.** There has been no planned, systematic
   marine archaeological survey for prehistoric settlements on the Sunda Shelf.
   The 10 km^2 counts the areas examined during opportunistic analyses (Gittins
   fossil context, Song Doc vicinity). The true planned-survey area is closer to 0.

4. **Site density scaling assumes similar preservation.** Tropical waters have
   faster sedimentation and different taphonomic conditions than the Mediterranean.
   The 0.5x conservative factor attempts to account for this but is itself an
   estimate.

5. **The gap could be even larger.** The analysis uses shelf area, not total area.
   If total sea area were used, the Mediterranean gap would be smaller but the
   fundamental message unchanged.
