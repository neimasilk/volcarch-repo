**P5:** "The Volcanic Ritual Clock" — submitted to *Bijdragen tot de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* (BKI)
**P9:** "Peripheral Conservatism as Archaeological Proxy" — submitted to *Journal of Southeast Asian Studies* (JSEAS)
**Prepared:** 2026-03-13 (Mata Elang #6, anti-salami-slicing protocol)

## 1. What Makes P5 Different from P9

P5 is an **ethnolinguistic-taphonomic** paper that proposes a specific causal mechanism: Javanese mortuary ritual timing (the slametan 3-7-40-100-1000-day sequence) was empirically calibrated to decomposition rates in volcanic Andosol soil. Its core contribution is the **Volcanic Ritual Clock hypothesis** and the **H-TOM synthesis** (Hypothetical Taphonomic Overlay Model), linking a 1000-day ritual timescale to a 1000-year geological timescale through a shared volcanic driver. P5 asks: *why these specific numbers?*

P9 is a **methodological framework** paper that proposes the **Peripheral Conservatism Framework (PCF)** for recovering pre-Hindu substrates from geographic, political, and temporal peripheries. Its core contribution is demonstrating that peripheries are involuntary archives, with Madagascar as a datable calibration point. P9 asks: *where should we look for pre-Hindu evidence?*

**The overlap:** Both papers use the DHARMA inscription corpus (268 Nusantaran prasasti) to demonstrate pre-Indic persistence. Both reference Austronesian comparative data.

**The distinction:** P5 uses DHARMA data to establish that indigenous cosmological vocabulary (hyang, manghuri, wuku) survived Indianization, supporting the claim that the slametan's temporal structure is pre-Indic. P9 uses DHARMA data to demonstrate that inscription content is predominantly organic/non-permanent material culture (63.4% organic vs 27.2% lithic), supporting the argument that centres overwrote themselves while peripheries conserved older strata. The same corpus serves entirely different analytical purposes.

## 3. Template Response to Reviewer

> **If a reviewer asks:** "This paper shares data/methods with [P9/a companion paper]. Is this salami-slicing?"

**Suggested response:**

"We appreciate the reviewer's attention to research integrity. [P9] and this paper both draw on the DHARMA inscription corpus, but they ask fundamentally different questions and reach different conclusions.

This paper (P5) asks: **Why does the Javanese slametan follow the specific numerical sequence 3-7-40-100-1000?** The answer lies in forensic taphonomy — these intervals map to decomposition stages in volcanic soil (permutation test p = 0.008). The DHARMA corpus is used here to establish that indigenous cosmological vocabulary (hyang at 43% of inscriptions) survived four waves of religious conversion, supporting the pre-Indic origin of the slametan structure. The paper's unique contributions are the H-TOM synthesis (linking ritual and geological timescales), the Pranata Mangsa seasonality analysis (E032), and the Monte Carlo decomposition simulation.

The companion paper [P9] asks: **Where should we look for pre-Hindu evidence when volcanic centres have been overwritten?** It proposes a methodological framework (Peripheral Conservatism Framework) and uses entirely different analyses: ABVD cognacy comparison across 8 Austronesian varieties, GBIF botanical distribution of Canarium, and the Malagasy time-capsule argument. The DHARMA corpus appears in [P9] only to quantify the organic-vs-lithic material culture ratio (E040), a wholly separate analysis from the ritual vocabulary screening conducted here.

The papers are submitted to different journals serving different scholarly communities: this paper to BKI (ethnolinguistic/anthropological audience) and [P9] to JSEAS (Southeast Asian studies generalist audience). Neither paper's argument depends on the other, though they are mutually reinforcing within a larger research programme on pre-Hindu Nusantaran substrates.

We are happy to provide both manuscripts to the editor for independent verification that no substantive overlap exists."
