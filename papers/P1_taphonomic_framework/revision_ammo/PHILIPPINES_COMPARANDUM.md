# Revision Support Material: Philippines as Archaeological Comparandum

**Use when:** Reviewer asks "What about other volcanic regions?" or "Is the pattern specific to Java?" or "How do you distinguish volcanic burial from organic decay?"

**Date prepared:** 2026-03-20

---

## The Argument

The Philippines provides a stronger comparandum than either Kalimantan (zero volcanism) or Japan (massive survey investment). The Philippines shares Java's Austronesian cultural heritage, tropical climate, and organic building traditions, but has **lower volcanic density** and **more karst/limestone preservation contexts**.

### Key Data Points

| Parameter | Volcanic Java | Philippines | Implication |
|-----------|:---:|:---:|---|
| Pre-400 CE sites | 0-3 | 15-25+ | Philippines richer despite less survey |
| Active volcanoes per 1000 km² | 0.35 | 0.07 | Java 5× more volcanic |
| Annual excavations | ~70 | ~30-50 | Java has MORE survey, FEWER finds |
| Mandatory rescue archaeology | No | No | Same institutional gap |
| Karst/cave sites | Very few | Abundant | Natural preservation capsules |

### Philippines Pre-400 CE Record

The Philippines preserves a continuous archaeological record spanning 67,000 years:
- **Callao Cave** (Cagayan, Luzon): 67,000 BP — *Homo luzonensis*, entirely new hominin species (Mijares et al. 2010)
- **Tabon Cave** (Palawan): 47,000-22,000 BP — earliest *H. sapiens* in Philippines (Fox 1970)
- **Ille Cave** (Palawan): 14,000 BP–present — continuous occupation with burials
- **Manunggul Cave** (Palawan): 890-710 BCE — famous "ship of the dead" burial jar
- **Sa Huynh-related sites**: 500 BCE-200 CE — trade network evidence (glass beads, double-headed earrings)

### What This Tells Us

1. **Volcanic burial IS a significant factor.** The Philippines, with 5× less volcanic density, has a demonstrably richer pre-400 CE record despite lower survey intensity. If survey deficit were the whole explanation, Philippines (less survey) should have fewer finds, not more.

2. **Cave availability is critical.** Almost ALL Philippines pre-400 CE sites are in limestone caves. Java's volcanic geology produces very few karst formations. Caves act as natural preservation capsules — stable temperature, dry, sealed from tropical weathering. Java lacks both unburied surfaces AND natural preservation environments.

3. **Organic decay is pan-Austronesian.** Even in the Philippines, open-air pre-400 CE sites are rare. This supports E040 (bamboo civilization): organic architecture decomposes regardless of volcanism. The difference is that Philippines had caves to preserve the record; Java did not.

4. **The cascade model (E110) gains a potential 6th factor:** cave/karst availability as a preservation variable. Java's double disadvantage — deep volcanic burial AND no natural preservation contexts — intensifies the cascade beyond what the 5-factor model predicts.

### Draft Response Paragraph

> "A reviewer might reasonably ask whether the pattern we describe is specific to Java or generalizable to other volcanic regions. The Philippines provides a more informative comparandum than either Kalimantan (zero volcanism) or Japan (massive survey investment), because it shares Java's Austronesian cultural heritage and tropical climate while having approximately five times lower volcanic density (0.07 vs 0.35 active volcanoes per 1,000 km²). The Philippines preserves a continuous archaeological record from 67,000 BP (Callao Cave; Mijares et al. 2010) through the pre-Hindu period, including multiple cave sites with rich organic material. Java's volcanic interior has none of this. The critical difference is not survey intensity — Indonesia conducts more excavations annually than the Philippines — but the interaction of two factors: volcanic sedimentation rate and the availability of natural preservation contexts (caves). The Philippines has abundant karst; volcanic Java has very little. This comparison supports rather than undermines the taphonomic framework: Java's archaeological blank reflects the compounding of deep burial with the absence of natural preservation environments that elsewhere provide a record of pre-Hindu Austronesian civilization."
