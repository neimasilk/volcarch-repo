# P18: What Words Remember — Computational Cultural Reconstruction of an Archaeologically Invisible Civilization

**Target:** World Archaeology (Q1, Taylor & Francis) — 8,000-10,000 words

**Type:** Synthesis/theoretical paper
**Author:** Mukhlis Amien
**Status:** OUTLINING

## Unique Contribution

**Vocabulary as archaeological artifact.** When physical remains are destroyed by volcanic burial, organic decay, survey deficit, recognition bias, and publication barriers, surviving vocabulary constitutes the only recoverable record of material and cognitive culture. This paper demonstrates that the pre-Hindu Nusantaran civilization — invisible to conventional archaeology — can be computationally reconstructed from 438 substrate vocabulary items, 189 kakawin domain terms, and 25,244 village names.
