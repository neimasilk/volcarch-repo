## Material findings

### 1. Confirmation (VOLCARCH-supporting)

**Leang Panninge, South Sulawesi (Carlhoff et al. 2021, Nature):** The oldest aDNA-successful Wallacea sample is from a 7.3 kyr BP hunter-gatherer burial in a KARST CAVE setting. Petrous bone recovery. This is the *exception* that proves the rule: aDNA recovers from karst-cave contexts, not from open-air tropical sites.

**Broader SE Asia aDNA landscape (McColl et al. 2018, Lipson et al. 2018, Larena et al. 2021):** "Only two low-coverage pre-Neolithic human genomes have been sequenced from [SE Asia], both from mainland Hoabinhian hunter-gatherer sites" (Carlhoff et al. 2021, direct quote). The tropical-climate challenge is universally acknowledged.

**Java itself:** No aDNA successes for Holocene or pre-400 CE human remains, despite:
- Rich Homo erectus fossil record (Sangiran, Trinil, Ngandong)
- Numerous Holocene archaeological sites
- Multiple modern WGS projects (Maulana et al. 2024 for West Javanese)

### 2. Methodological framing (Adam Brumm, Griffith, quoted)

"The humid tropics are very unforgiving on DNA preservation in ancient human bones and teeth."

Universal acknowledgment in the field that tropical contexts are DNA-hostile. **The karst/cave vs volcanic/open-air differential is precisely VOLCARCH's predicted pattern:** protected microenvironments preserve DNA; exposed tropical volcanic contexts destroy it.

### 3. Does this subfield contradict VOLCARCH?

**No.** The empirical pattern documented by paleogeneticists (aDNA from caves, not from Java's volcanic open-air sites) is the exact pattern VOLCARCH predicts from its taphonomic framework. The subfield provides independent confirmation through a fully separate observer community (paleogeneticists, not archaeologists).
