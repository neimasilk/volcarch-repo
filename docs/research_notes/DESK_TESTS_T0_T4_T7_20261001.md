## T0 — Tanggal awal Liyangan

**Tugas:** T0: verify the early dates of Liyangan (Liangan, Purbosari, Ngadirejo, Temanggung; NE slope of Sindoro). Is there secure evidence of human occupation before 400 CE?


### Verdict

POSSIBLE BUT INSECURE, and in practice not demonstrated: there is no secure evidence of pre-400 CE occupation at Liyangan. Confidence in the verdict is MEDIUM-HIGH, based on every page I could open. It rests on published text only, since the 2016 report, the Abbas monograph chapters, and Suhendro et al. 2026 were not accessible.

What the evidence consists of: the only pre-400 CE candidates are three entries from an unpublished 2016 series: 181 +/-34 CE (soil), 234 +/-50 CE (wood charcoal), and, older still, 23 BCE (soil) and 1113 BCE (soil), plus the 2231 +/-25 BP paleosol inside the Liangan F1 burial feature (cal 383-202 BCE by my IntCal20 calculation). None has a lab code, BP value, depth, calibration statement, or stratigraphic relation to a floor, structure or hearth. Three of the six 2016 dates and the F1 date are bulk soil, which dates soil organic matter, not occupation. The excavator himself called the 1113 BCE result 'too old' and the BCE chronology something to be investigated. The F1 date is explicitly the age of the soil that the 9th-10th c burial pit cut through. The 234 CE charcoal coincides with the ~1720 BP (c. 230 CE, or c. 250-410 cal CE if radiocarbon) charcoal in the pyroclastic flow, so it may date a volcanic event, not settlement (hypothesis). An independent paper in the same journal (Tanudirjo et al. 2019) calls the published dating inaccurate and confusing. The '2nd-11th century' range is repeated in Riyanto 2017, 2022 and 2023 from one source, with no new data.

What is reasonably secure: the settlement is 6th-10th c CE at most by radiocarbon (587, 742, 846, 913, 921, 971 CE; lab BATAN for the 2013 set, no codes), and 9th-10th c by artefacts (Tang ceramics, temple style); that is post-400 and is the 'oldest dated interior settlement' figure the synthesis already uses. Even the 587 CE date lacks context. The 1061 +/-38 CE sample postdates what the Suhendro 2026 title implies for the destruction (8th-9th c) and conflicts with Riyanto 2022 (11th c eruption), so the destruction date is itself unsettled.

For H1a and H1b: Liyangan supports H1a only for the AD 9th-10th c surface (4-7 m, or 6-10 m by another reading, of stacked pyroclastic and lahar units, at least four sequences, no published dated section). It gives H1b no first case. The honest label is that a pre-400 CE occupation floor is possible at the site, but the data do not test it. Do not cite 'Liyangan occupied from the 2nd century' as evidence for pre-400 settlement. If cited, state that it is unreplicated, undocumented and contested.


### Catatan per butir


**[HIGH] R1. Riyanto 2011 radiocarbon date, bamboo charcoal (earliest dating campaign)**

Sample: bamboo charcoal, excavation 2011. Uncalibrated 1060 +/- 110 BP (1950). Reported calibrated 'calendar age' 971 CE. No lab code, no sample location or stratigraphic context given, calibration curve not named. My own IntCal20 calibration (not in the source): 774-1217 cal CE at 93.2%, so the single value '971 CE' hides a wide range. Post-400 CE, and consistent with the 9th-10th c Tang ceramics. Riyanto 2017 (p.144) says the figure is taken from Riyanto 2013 p.775 and Riyanto 2014b pp.58-59. Riyanto 2013 is the UKM Bangi seminar paper 'Letusan Gunung Sindoro Mengubur Situs Mataram Kuno Liyangan' (proceedings pp.767-781), which I did not read.

*Sumber:* Riyanto 2017, Berkala Arkeologi 37(2):141-158, doi:10.30883/jba.v37i2.177. PDF read via Wayback: https://web.archive.org/web/2024if_/https://berkalaarkeologi.kemdikbud.go.id/index.php/berkalaarkeologi/article/download/177/pdf_1. IntCal20 file: https://intcal.org/curves/intcal20.14c


**[HIGH] R2. 2013 radiocarbon series (BATAN), source of the '6th-century' claim: 587, 742, 846, 913 CE**

Material: wood and bamboo charcoal ('arang bambu dan kayu') from FOUR excavation locations. Lab: Badan Tenaga Nuklir Nasional (BATAN); no lab codes in any source I found. Reported only as 'angka kalender Masehi' 587, 742, 846 and 913 CE. No BP, no sigma, no depth, no context (floor, structure or burial), calibration not stated. Riyanto 2015 (p.51) lists 587, 742, 846, 913 and 971 together as 'sampel arang bambu dan kayu ... dari beberapa lokasi' and concludes occupation 'sejak abad VI hingga X'. Riyanto 2017 splits them: 971 from 2011, the other four from 2013 (cites Riyanto 2015:47). Tanudirjo et al. 2019 (p.108-109) states these are secondary-source numbers with no sample material type, no lab, no mean or deviation, no stratigraphic position, and no statement of calibrated or not. All four are post-400 CE. The 587 CE value is the oldest of this set, and it is a 6th-century CE date, not pre-400.

*Sumber:* Riyanto 2015, Berkala Arkeologi 35(1):33-58, doi:10.30883/jba.v35i1.37 (PDF: https://berkalaarkeologi.kemdikbud.go.id/index.php/berkalaarkeologi/article/download/37/75, read via Wayback); Riyanto 2017 as in R1; Tanudirjo, Yuwono & Adi 2019, Berkala Arkeologi 39(2):97-116, doi:10.30883/jba.v39i2.474


**[HIGH] R3. 2016 radiocarbon series: six dates, including three from 100 BCE to 300 CE (the origin of the '2nd-11th century' claim)**

Listed oldest to youngest by Riyanto 2017 (pp.144-145), quoting the unpublished 'Laporan Penelitian Situs Liangan 2016' (Tim Penelitian 2016: 88-89). Values are given as calendar years with +/-, not BP, and calibration status is not stated:
(a) 1113 BCE +/-60, SOIL sample, box S50B10, block LT1603
(b) 23 BCE +/-28, SOIL, box U11T29, block LT1602
(c) 181 CE +/-34 ('abad II'), SOIL, box S24B2, block LT1501
(d) 234 CE +/-50 ('abad III'), wood charcoal, box U15T35, block LT1601
(e) 921 CE +/-27, bamboo charcoal, box S49B10, block LT1603
(f) 1061 CE +/-38, bamboo/wood, box U345T28, block LT1601.
No lab codes, no depths, no layer numbers, no stratigraphic relation to floors or structures. Riyanto's own comment: the 1113 BCE result is 'terlalu tua' (too old) and the BCE chronology 'harus didalami'. He still states the range as 'abad II-XI Masehi' and draws the pre-Hindu inference from it. The (a) and (e) samples come from adjacent 1 m boxes (S50B10 versus S49B10) and differ by about 2,000 years. That points to different strata, probably a deeper paleosol versus the settlement layer (my inference). The 234 CE charcoal is from block LT1601 U15-18 T34-37, which Noerwidi 2017 describes as lying about 30 m north of the temple ruin and fauna-rich, from layers 2 and 3. The 921 CE sample is from LT1603 S46-48 B10-14, about 90 m south of the temple ruin, layer 2. Neither source says which layer each date came from.

*Sumber:* Riyanto 2017, doi:10.30883/jba.v37i2.177 (as R1); Noerwidi 2017, Berkala Arkeologi 37(1):35-50, doi:10.30883/jba.v37i1.116, for block locations and layers (PDF via Wayback: https://berkalaarkeologi.kemdikbud.go.id/index.php/berkalaarkeologi/article/download/116/PDF). Primary report 'Tim Penelitian 2016' is unpublished ('Tidak terbit', Balai Arkeologi DIY) and was NOT seen.


**[HIGH] R4. Paleosol 2231 +/- 25 BP in burial feature Liangan F1 (Noerwidi)**

Conventional 14C, bulk paleosol sediment (dark brown sandy clay, organic soil) from INSIDE the burial feature, run at PATIR-BATAN Jakarta. No lab code printed. Noerwidi's own reading: this is the age of the soil (2nd-3rd c BCE, by his rounding), NOT of the skeleton. The 9th-10th c individual, with a Tang-dynasty small bottle as grave good, was buried in a pit cut into an older soil. The skeleton is a secondary burial and was recovered in 2013 at a sand-mining face that no longer exists, so the stratigraphy could not be reconstructed. The feature lies at the same height as the surface under the neighbouring building foundations, so he dates it to 9th-10th c. He says the rest of the site mostly gives 9th-10th c. Tanudirjo et al. 2019 repeat that the date is 'umur tanah', 'kurang tepat' for dating the settlement. My IntCal20 calibration (not in the source): 383-202 cal BCE (95.4%). It is BCE and is not an occupation date.

*Sumber:* Noerwidi 2016, 'Aspek Biokultural Sisa Rangka Manusia dari Situs Liangan', Berkala Arkeologi 36(1):83-98, doi:10.30883/jba.v36i1.226 (PDF via Wayback: https://berkalaarkeologi.kemdikbud.go.id/index.php/berkalaarkeologi/article/download/226/238); Tanudirjo et al. 2019 (p.103, p.108)


**[MEDIUM] R5. Charcoal about 1720 years old in the pyroclastic flow deposit (Nurnusanto 2014 / Sukhyar et al. 1992)**

Charcoal inside the dark-grey pyroclastic flow deposit (breadcrust bomb fragments) that buries the UPPER part of the temple; the brownish-yellow, strongly weathered scoria/lithic tephra buries the LOWER part. Reported only as '1,720 tahun' (Nurnusanto 2014: 117-122); no lab, no sigma, and no statement of BP versus calendar. Tanudirjo 2019 converts it naively to 'sekitar 230 M' and matches it to Sap-5 (the Sundoro pyroclastic flow deposit on the Sukhyar et al. 1992 geological map), noting Liyangan lies on the edge of that flow, and to a '230 CE / 470 CE' eruption listing in Wunderman 2013. If 1720 is a radiocarbon age, my IntCal20 calibration of 1720 +/-30 gives about 250-411 cal CE. That is the dated age of a volcanic event, not of any occupation. Purwantara et al. 2025 repeat the figure and say 1,720-year-old pyroclastic deposits occur on all sectors of Sundoro. Degroot 2017 warns that this date dates only the eruption that produced the pyroclastic material. The 234 CE +/-50 wood charcoal (R3d) coincides with this figure (an observation, not a proven link). A possible reading: the '3rd-century' charcoal is from, or equivalent to, the ~230 CE flow, not occupation (HYPOTHESIS, untested).

*Sumber:* Tanudirjo et al. 2019 (pp.108-109); Purwantara et al. 2025, Southeast Asian Studies 14(2), doi:10.20495/seas.25004 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/seas/14/2/14_25004/_pdf), pp.325, 332; Degroot 2017, Archipel 94:191-209, doi:10.4000/archipel.456 (https://journals.openedition.org/archipel/456), text and fn.31. Nurnusanto 2014 (in Abbas 2014/2016, pp.117-122) NOT seen. Calibration is mine, IntCal20.


**[MEDIUM] R6. Prambada et al. 2016 Sundoro eruptive history: Liyangan and Kledung eruptive groups**

Twelve eruptive groups since 34 ka. As summarised by Tanudirjo et al. 2019: Liyangan group 1280-1180 BP (about 670-770 CE, uncalibrated) and Kledung group 1095-979 BP (about 855-971 CE, uncalibrated); last groups (Summit, Sibayak) 515-453 BP. Purwantara 2025 gives the 12-group range as 33,619 cal BP to 515-453 cal BP. I could read only the Springer abstract page, so the Liyangan-group ages come second-hand and the primary lab data were not seen. The abstract confirms 'stratigraphy, radiocarbon dating, petrography, and whole-rock geochemistry' and lists Liyangan as the 9th group.

*Sumber:* Prambada, Arakawa, Ikehata, Furukawa, Takada, Wibowo, Nakagawa, Kartadinata 2016, Bull. Volcanol. 78:81, doi:10.1007/s00445-016-1079-3 (Springer page: abstract only, paywalled); Tanudirjo et al. 2019 (pp.108-109)

**[HIGH] R8. Origin and circularity of the '2nd-11th century' claim**

The range first appears in Riyanto 2017 (abstract: 'pengumuran melalui analisis karbon ... setidaknya dari abad II hingga XI Masehi'; submitted 30 May 2017), from the 2016 series R3. Riyanto 2022 (J. Konservasi Cagar Budaya 16(2)) and Riyanto, Saptono, Intan & Taniardi 2023 (ASSEHR 756:519-532) repeat 'at least the second century' to 11th c, citing only Riyanto 2017 p.145 (and 'Berkala Arkeologi 37(1) 141-158', a citation slip for 37(2)). They publish no new dates and no lab codes. Purwantara 2025 then cites Riyanto 2015, 2017 for 'second to ninth centuries'. So the claim is single-sourced from one unpublished 2016 field report. Riyanto 2015 (earlier) gave 6th-10th c; Noerwidi 2016/2017 and Tanudirjo 2019 (same journal, same site) use '6th-10th c' / '9th-10th c' and treat early dates as problematic.

*Sumber:* Riyanto 2017 (doi:10.30883/jba.v37i2.177); Riyanto 2022 (doi:10.33374/jurnalkonservasicagarbudaya.v16i2.296; abstract page and PDF via Wayback https://web.archive.org/web/20250318043134if_/https://borobudur.kemdikbud.go.id/index.php/jurnalkonservasicagarbudaya/article/download/296/232); Riyanto et al. 2023, doi:10.2991/978-2-38476-070-1_47 (https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/125988181.pdf); Purwantara et al. 2025


**[HIGH] R9. Critical appraisals of the Liyangan dating**

(1) Tanudirjo, Yuwono & Adi 2019: the C14 dates have been presented in a way that is 'tidak akurat'; 'belum ada kepastian yang meyakinkan' for placing the site in history; the existing dating 'menimbulkan kerumitan penafsiran atau bahkan kerancuan'; the oldest date is soil-age; the volcanic dates do not fix the burial time precisely; they conclude the burying eruption was in or after the 10th c, on the basis of artefacts (most artefacts are 9th or 10th c, giving a terminus post quem for the burial). (2) Degroot 2017 (Archipel 94), review of Abbas ed. 2016: Nurnusanto and Fadhlan give no stratigraphy, so the relation between structures and geological units is unclear; Prambada et al. suggest at least two pyroclastic flows and one lahar; fn.31: the early date shows 'how careful we need to be when using C14 results in a geologically unstable environment', since only the eruption that produced the pyroclastic material was dated and the later landslide brought the debris. IMPORTANT: the review is by Veronique Degroot (Archipel 94, 2017). I found no Griffiths 2019 review; the task's 'Griffiths 2019' appears to be a misattribution (UNVERIFIED that Griffiths wrote anything on this).

*Sumber:* Tanudirjo et al. 2019, doi:10.30883/jba.v39i2.474 (PDF via Wayback https://berkalaarkeologi.kemdikbud.go.id/index.php/berkalaarkeologi/article/download/474/458); Degroot 2017, doi:10.4000/archipel.456 (HAL copy: https://shs.hal.science/halshs-03137457)


**[MEDIUM] R10. Burial thickness and stratigraphic sequence above the site**

Thickness of pyroclastic cover: 4-7 m (Tanudirjo 2019; Riyanto et al. 2023: pyroclastic fall plus 'hot cloud' flow deposits 4-7 m). Noerwidi 2017 quoting Nurnusanto 2014: 'material lahar setebal 6-10 meter'. Tanudirjo 2019, citing Putra et al. 2013 (Buletin Berkala Merapi 13(3):42-52): up to 7 m, at least four layers/sequences, with similar intervals between them. Putra et al. 2013 state that most ceramics and burnt bamboo weave lie in pyroclastic flow deposit 1 (lower), whereas pyroclastic flow deposit 2 (upper) contains little (the passage is truncated in my copy). Riyanto et al. 2023: the site rests on pyroclastic fall deposits and is mostly covered by pyroclastic flow deposits. Nurnusanto (via Purwantara 2025): weathered brownish-yellow tephra buries the lower part of the temple; dark-grey pyroclastic flow with charcoal (1720) buries the upper part. Site elevation 1100-1200 m. Hence the repository note 'about 10 m' is not what these sources say (4-7 m; 6-10 m for a different reading). The cover is a stack of several pyroclastic and lahar units, with no published dated section.

*Sumber:* Tanudirjo et al. 2019 (pp.97-98, 103, 112); Riyanto et al. 2023 (geological data section, p.524 approx.); Noerwidi 2017 (p.35-36); Purwantara et al. 2025 (p.332)

## T4-meja — Penampang bertanggal (kedalaman permukaan ±400 M)

**Tugas:** T4-DESK: depth below the present surface of the ~0-400 CE land surface in the volcanic ring plains of Merapi, Sindoro-Sumbing, Kelud and the Borobudur/Kedu basin. Desk compilation of published dated sections, cores, logs and temple sondages. Read-only; no repo file was touched. Scratch copies of the open-access PDFs were saved to the session scratchpad, outside the repo.


### Verdict

The desk pass does NOT yet reach the D6 criterion of at least 10 dated sections giving a median depth for the ~400 CE surface 5-30 km from a vent. I compiled 12 site or section records, but only about three carry any 0-400 CE-age horizon with a depth, and none is a clean single dated profile:

- the Merapi composite Fig. 6.7 reading, 0-400 CE at about 4.5-7.5 m, composite and read by eye;
- the Borobudur paleo-swamp valley, 1-3 m, with 1700+/-160 yr, second-hand;
- the Sindoro PDC dated about 1720 yr, which is only a thickness (>4 m).

Everything else gives the depth of a 9th-10th c temple floor (Sambisari, Kedulan, Kimpulan, Morangan, Pendem, Liyangan). Under simple superposition those are lower bounds on the 400 CE surface depth, not measurements of it.

Measured versus inferred, by landform (rough conversion: 0-400 CE is about 2050-1650 14C BP):

- Proximal slope and interfluve, up to about 15 km (Merapi flank, Sindoro Liyangan):
  - Measured: Liyangan has 4-7 m of PDC and fall over a 9th-10th c occupation. The Merapi composite puts 0-400 CE roughly 4.5-7.5 m below the top of the composite.
  - Inferred: a 400 CE surface here is plausibly 4 m or more, probably more near vents with thick PDC.
  - Not established: the composite is a stack of localities with no stated distances.
  - Plausibly <2 m? Unlikely on slopes with continuous tephra and PDC accumulation. Possible on eroded interfluves, where no section was found.

- Medial and distal ring plain in the lahar corridors, S/SE Merapi, about 14-24 km (Kedulan, Sambisari, Morangan, Kimpulan):
  - Measured: the 9th-c floor is about 7 m (Kedulan), 6.5 m (Sambisari), 4.1 m to the base (Kimpulan, about 15 km), and 6.5 m (Morangan, secondary).
  - Dated cover: at Kedulan and Sambisari most of this cover post-dates AD 1285-1400, so little aggraded between about 800 and 1285 AD there.
  - Inferred: the 400 CE surface is >=4-7 m here, and deeper if 400-800 CE deposition occurred (Gumuk ashes and a Kedulan 1445 BP PDC suggest some did).
  - Plausibly <2 m? No datum supports it. The shallowest candidate is Pendem at 2 m, which is a 9th-c object depth.

- Lake basin and floodplain, Kedu/Borobudur, about 28 km:
  - Measured, second-hand: swamp deposit at 1-3 m dated 1700+/-160 yr.
  - Inferred: a 400 CE-age surface of about 1-3 m is plausible in quiet basin margins. Lahar-fed reaches (Progo-Elo, Sigug) are very different (>25 m of volcanics over Pleistocene lake clay).
  - Plausibly <2 m? Yes in the swamp valley, but this is one second-hand datum.

- Kelud: nothing found. Gap.

Is <2 m plausible anywhere 5-30 km from a vent? Yes in principle in quiet lake or basin margins and on eroded, non-corridor interfluves, where I found one second-hand datum (Borobudur) and no section. In the Merapi lahar corridors of the southern and south-eastern ring plain, the data I have do not support it. Five temple floors there give 2-7 m, with the median about 6.5 m, and the 400 CE surface is at least that deep under superposition.

On the D6 test as written (median depth of the ~400 CE surface across at least 10 dated sections 5-30 km from a vent below 2 m): my data do not trigger it. That is not a test pass: the sample is the wrong kind (9th-c floors, not dated 400 CE surfaces), biased toward lahar corridors and toward sites found by sand miners, mostly one corridor, and not independent. D6 stays open until dated 0-400 CE sections are obtained.

Correction to the repo synthesis (OBJECTIVE_ANSWER_20261001.md): the '5-9 m' range for the 9th-c surface at 17-24 km is too narrow and too deep at the top. Reported values are about 2-7 m (Pendem 2, Kimpulan 4.1, Sambisari 6.5, Kedulan about 7). I did not find a 9 m figure. The Sambisari '18 km' in the source is also not the same as my approximate haversine of 23-24 km.

Confidence: HIGH on Kedulan and Sambisari thickness and on Merapi's persistent explosive activity in the window; MEDIUM on the Borobudur 1-3 m figure and on Kimpulan; LOW on everything for Sindoro, Liyangan, Morangan, Kadisoko, Plaosan and Pendem. Calibrations are rough and not run through OxCal.

**[MEDIUM] R13. Groundwater, geotechnical and borehole logs for the Yogyakarta-Sleman basin and Kedu basin: no dated log found**

### Verdict

No: d cannot be computed from what is in the repo today. The denominator is available and partly reproducible: 111 inscriptions dated 8th-10th c. Kedu/Prambanan is feasible as a desk project (about 2-4 sessions, $0) once a gazetteer and an external register are assembled, and the design sketched here is pre-registerable. It would not prove H3 or H1b. Confidence: HIGH on the repo-holdings and E129 findings, MEDIUM on the counts, which come from regex extraction plus partly manual dating, and MEDIUM-LOW on the feasibility estimate.

**[MEDIUM] Sketch of pre-registerable design (T7)**

Unit: attested village (distinct toponym within a named watak/region), 8th-10th c. CE. FROZEN BEFORE LOOKING AT NUMERATOR: (1) frame V_r = hand-curated list of village names from DHARMA edition text (two coders, rules above), date window 701-1000, regions Kedu, Prambanan-Mataram, Brantas; (2) identification tier for each name: T1 modern location published by epigraphers (Boechari, Damais, Sarkar, Wisseman Christie, DHARMA metadata), T2 unique modern desa name match within the same kabupaten, T3 ambiguous/unlocated; (3) 'recorded settlement' defined operationally: open-air habitation deposit (floors, post-holes, hearths, domestic assemblage) dated 8th-10th c. by radiocarbon or diagnostic ceramics, excludes temples, inscriptions, tombs, find-spots of single objects; (4) register frozen in advance (SRN Cagar Budaya, BPK Wilayah X/XI lists, Balai Arkeologi DIY/Jateng reports, Wikidata/OSM) with matching radius rho=1 km (sensitivity 0.5 and 2 km), blind coder. Estimates: d_hat = S/|G| (G = T1+T2), Jeffreys 95% interval; bounds over unlocated names: S/|V| <= d <= (S+|V\G|)/|V|. Extrapolation to pre-400: if zero pre-400 settlements are recorded, N_95 = ln(0.05)/ln(1-d) (rule of three, ~3/d): d=0.2 -> 14, 0.1 -> 29, 0.05 -> 59, 0.02 -> 149, 0.01 -> 299 villages. With uncertainty in d use the beta-binomial predictive, P(0 detected|N)=B(a,b+N)/B(a,b) with a=S+0.5, b=n-S+0.5: (S,n)=(5,50) -> N_95=35; (3,50) -> 66; (2,30) -> 68; (1,30) -> 190; (1,15) -> 94; (0,30) -> ~12,000 (unconstrained). Decision rule (to be fixed by PI): let N_ref = |V_r| (village count attested for 8th-10th c. in that region = 'pre-400 population as large as the later one'). Use d_pre = m*d with m in {1, 0.5, 0.25} to allow older sites to be more buried/eroded. (A) If N_95(m=0.25) >= N_ref: a zero count of pre-400 settlements is UNINFORMATIVE; H3 (low visibility) is not excluded and 'no settlement found' must not be read as 'no people'. (B) If N_95(m=1) < N_ref and N_95(m=0.25) < N_ref: zero count is inconsistent with an 8th-10th c.-sized population and supports H6 (lower density), still conditional on the survey effort being comparable. (C) Between: report as ambiguous. Pre-register also: S=0 of n>=30 identified villages is itself a result (d<8%) and implies A. Positive control: Liyangan. Falsifier for the whole idea: if S/|G| in Kedu/Prambanan is >= 0.25 with G>=30, the 8th-10th c. record is not village-blind there and the H3 'control' argument is dead.

*Sumber:* computed in session (scipy beta/betaln, math); design is Claude's proposal, not yet reviewed by PI or a domain expert


**[MEDIUM] Methodological threats to d (must be stated in the pre-registration)**

1. Selection of the frame: sima charters name villages that were taxable, politically salient and near the royal core; inscriptions/stones are found near temples, where survey effort is highest, so d is likely OVERestimated for ordinary villages (makes the N_95 bound too tight, anti-conservative). 2. Time direction: pre-400 sites are 500-1000 years older than 8th-10th c. ones, so d_pre <= d_8-10 in the same landscape (more burial, erosion, rebuilding); hence the multiplier m. 3. Circularity with H1a: Kedu/Prambanan are exactly the zones where burial (Sambisari ~6 m, Kedulan ~7 m, Liyangan up to ~10 m per OBJECTIVE_ANSWER) is claimed, so a low d there mixes 'light buildings' (H3), burial (H1a) and survey effort (H2); d therefore cannot separate H3 from H1a, only tests whether absence of records is informative. 4. Identification vs detection: failure to match an Old Javanese toponym to a modern desa is an identification failure, not an archaeological non-detection; keep the T1/T2/T3 tiers and the bounds. 5. Continuity: modern villages may have moved; matching radius sensitivity needed. 6. Language/transcription: DHARMA conventions (A for long a, I as locative i) and spelling variants need normalisation; the extraction here covers single-token names only. 7. Corpus coverage: 268 encoded editions of a larger corpus; the Brantas has about 5 distinct names in the local corpus. 8. 'Settlement' register quality: Indonesian heritage registers are temple-centred, so S may be near zero by registry construction; counts from literature (Balai Arkeologi reports) are needed or the test only measures registry bias.
