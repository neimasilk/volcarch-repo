# Liangan (Liyangan) — VOLCARCH's Strongest Validation Case

**Date:** 2026-03-21
**Idea Registry:** I-120
**Status:** Research note complete. Experiment E121 (sedimentation rate calibration) pending.

---

## Site Summary

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Location** | Dusun Liyangan, Purbosari Village, Ngadirejo District, Temanggung Regency, Central Java |
| **Coordinates** | ~7.5 km from Mount Sindoro peak, elevation ~1,080 m asl |
| **Discovered** | 2008, accidentally by local sand miners |
| **Buried by** | Pyroclastic density current (PDC) from Mount Sindoro |
| **Burial event** | "Liyangan eruptive group" — 9th of 12 recognized Sindoro eruptive groups, ~1.1-1.2 ka BP (Prambada et al. 2016) |
| **Burial depth** | 6-8 m (up to 12-16 m in some areas); 2024 ERT survey: alluvial deposits 3-16 m below surface |
| **Occupation span** | C-14 dates: 587 AD to 971 AD (~6th-10th century) |
| **Period** | Mataram Kuno (Ancient Mataram / Medang Kingdom) |
| **Excavated area** | ~3 hectares (as of 2016) |
| **Lead researcher** | Sugeng Riyanto, Balai Arkeologi Yogyakarta |
| **Emplacement temperature** | 295-487°C (Harijoko et al. 2018, charcoal reflectance analysis) |

## What Was Found

### Structures
- **Hindu temple complex** — stone terraces, yoni, lingga (Shaivite worship)
- **Wooden houses** — carbonised beams, upright post-holes, plank floors. One building 8m × 8m with room partitions (2018 excavation)
- **Pendopo** (open pavilion) — pillar holes, communal gathering structure
- **Hydraulic infrastructure** — stone and earthen channels, storage tanks, reservoirs
- **Pedestrian pathways** between dwellings
- **Bathing structure** with possible "showerheads"

### Organic Material (CRITICAL for VOLCARCH)
- **Carbonised rice grains** — identified as **tropical japonica**, first evidence of this subspecies in Indonesia (Castillo 2014). Of 56 caryopses, 64% fit japonica L/W range.
- **Fossilised maize** — found inside a bamboo basket. Non-endemic to Java → evidence of international agricultural trade networks.
- **Charred wood and timber** — building beams preserved in situ
- **Roof fibres, corn cobs with leaves**
- **Seeds and plant residues**

### Artifacts
- ~40 Tang Dynasty Chinese ceramics (9th century)
- Inscribed copper plates
- Iron tools: hoes, sickles, axes, knives
- Iron slag and furnace stones → **on-site metallurgy** (smelting and forging)

### Human Remains
- Individual "Liangan F1" — discovered 2013 in Cluster F. Published: dental modification (antemortem cultural practice), perimortem taphonomic evidence (Berkala Arkeologi 36(1), 2016).

### Key Finding: Organised Evacuation
**No evidence of mass casualties or panic.** Inhabitants apparently recognised warning signs and evacuated before the PDC arrived. This parallels Cerén (El Salvador), where residents also evacuated.

## Why Liangan Matters for VOLCARCH

### 1. VALIDATES the core prediction
Volcanic burial CAN and DOES preserve complete settlements including organic material. Wood, rice, bamboo, fibres — exactly the materials VOLCARCH argues are systematically lost from the pre-Hindu record — survived here because of volcanic sealing.

### 2. PROVES the discovery mechanism
The site was found **accidentally during sand mining** — not through systematic archaeological survey. This confirms VOLCARCH's argument that Indonesia's buried sites are found by chance construction/extraction, not by directed archaeological search. It validates the need for VOLCARCH's predictive targeting methodology.

### 3. Provides burial depth calibration
6-8 m burial from an event ~1,000 years ago. This is consistent with VOLCARCH's sedimentation rates (4.4 ± 1.2 mm/yr would predict ~4.4 m in 1,000 years at average rate; Sindoro's proximal rate is clearly higher — expected for a proximal volcanic flank site).

**Potential E121 calculation:**
- If burial depth = 7 m (midpoint) and age = ~1,050 years (midpoint of 971-1080 CE):
  - Rate = 7,000 mm / 1,050 yr = **6.7 mm/yr**
- This is within VOLCARCH's calibrated range (3.6-13.1 mm/yr) and comparable to proximal Merapi rates
- BUT: this represents a single catastrophic event, not gradual sedimentation. The rate is not directly comparable to the cumulative rates used in P1.

**Important distinction:** Liangan burial is **catastrophic** (single PDC event), while VOLCARCH's sedimentation model is **cumulative** (many small events over centuries). Both produce burial, but the mechanisms differ. Liangan demonstrates that volcanic burial preserves organic material; P1's sedimentation rates demonstrate that GRADUAL volcanic deposition also buries sites, just more slowly.

### 4. Shows what pre-Hindu sites MIGHT look like
If we could find a pre-Hindu settlement preserved under volcanic deposits, it would look something like Liangan — houses, agriculture, water management, trade goods — minus the Hindu temple and Sanskrit inscriptions. The domestic infrastructure (wooden houses, irrigation, metalworking, rice agriculture) has Austronesian precedent and would have existed before Indianisation.

### 5. Demonstrates the "iceberg" principle
Liangan was a complete settlement. Before its discovery, only the stone temple elements might have been detectable. The wooden houses, agriculture, and domestic infrastructure were invisible until excavation. This is exactly the "iceberg" argument P11 makes about candi distribution.

### 6. Gap in literature: NO formal Cerén comparison
The Cerén comparison has NOT been published formally. Both sites share:
- Rapid volcanic burial preserving organic material
- Complete settlement (not just monumental architecture)
- Agricultural evidence in situ
- Evidence of organised evacuation (no mass casualties)
- Burial by pyroclastic flows (not lava)

**Difference:** Cerén is pre-Columbian (~600 CE, pre-state agricultural village), Liangan is Mataram-era (Hindu-Buddhist state context). Cerén is in Central America (Loma Caldera eruption), Liangan is in Java (Sindoro eruption).

This comparison is a potential publication opportunity or a strong paragraph in P19.

## Sedimentation Rate Gap

**No published sedimentation rates exist for Liangan or the Sindoro volcanic system** in the context of archaeological site burial. The Prambada et al. (2016) study provides eruptive history and stratigraphy but not rates. The Archipel review (Griffiths 2019) notes that Abbas (2016) lacks proper stratigraphy.

This is a gap VOLCARCH can fill (E121). Approach:
1. Use published burial depths (6-8 m, up to 12-16 m) and C-14dates (587-971 AD)
2. Calculate effective burial rate from Sindoro for comparison with Merapi calibration
3. Note the catastrophic vs. cumulative distinction
4. Compare with 2024 ERT geophysical data (Hidayat et al. 2024)

## Pre-Hindu Layers?

Suggestive but unconfirmed:
- C-14 date of **587 AD** from bamboo predates typical Hindu period inscription evidence in this region (8th century)
- One source claims occupation extends to **2nd century AD** — but Griffiths (2019, Archipel) warns this may date pyroclastic material, not cultural occupation
- All documented religious artifacts are Hindu-Shaivite (8th-10th century)
- The question remains open. If pre-Hindu layers exist at Liangan, they would be deeper than current excavation (below 8 m?)

**VOLCARCH implication:** If Liangan has pre-Hindu layers at depth, this would be direct evidence for the core thesis. But claiming this without evidence would be dishonest. Note as a possibility, not a fact.
