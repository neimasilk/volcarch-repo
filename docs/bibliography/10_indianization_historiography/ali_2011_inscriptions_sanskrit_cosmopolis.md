# Notes

## Core argument
Ali examines how South Asian political concepts shaped early Southeast Asian societies via inscriptions. His central finding: the relationship between language practices (Sanskrit, Old Malay, Old Javanese) and state structures is "highly complex and shifting" — **NOT** a straightforward importation of Indic political order.

## Relation to Pollock's Sanskrit cosmopolis
Ali **nuances rather than contradicts** Pollock's framework. He:
- Identifies "major analytical problems" in linking cosmopolitanism to political structures
- Views Pollock's framework as a starting point, not a conclusion
- Suggests the SE Asian evidence "offers a new perspective" on theories of linguistic and cultural cosmopolitanism

## How this helps VOLCARCH positioning

**The positioning triangle:**
- **Coedès 1968:** Indianization brought civilization (rejected by almost everyone now).
- **Pollock 2006:** Sanskrit cosmopolis spread by cultural prestige; no armies, no coercion. Politics follows language.
- **Ali 2011:** Pollock's language-to-politics link is complex, not straightforward.
- **VOLCARCH:** There was a pre-Indic substrate civilization; Sanskrit was an elite overlay on top of an existing complex society that had its own material organisation and maritime reach.

VOLCARCH is broadly COMPATIBLE with Ali's nuanced reading of Pollock. Both Ali and VOLCARCH reject the straightforward "Indianization = civilization" equation. The key addition VOLCARCH makes is empirical: the pre-Indic substrate wasn't just culturally present — it produced material culture (Jatim beads, bronze drums, maritime trade to Han China in 132 CE) that Pollock's framework doesn't specifically address.

**P0 should engage Ali seriously** because Ali represents the sophisticated contemporary form of the Indianization debate. Dismissing only Coedès is too easy.

## What Ali does not address (VOLCARCH fills this gap)
- Taphonomic erasure of pre-Indic material culture
- Demographic magnitude of pre-Indic populations
- Archaeometric evidence of pre-Indic industrial production
- Comparative evidence (Philippines) showing pre-Indic complexity
