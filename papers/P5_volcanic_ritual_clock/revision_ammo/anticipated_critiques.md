## Critique 1: "No Javanese-specific decomposition data"

**Severity:** HIGH — single biggest evidentiary gap

**Expected form:** "The decomposition timeline is drawn from general forensic literature (Rodriguez & Bass 1985, Galloway 1989, Vass 2001), not from Javanese cemeteries. Tropical volcanic soil conditions differ from the Arizona/Tennessee contexts where most forensic taphonomy is conducted."

**Response:**
- Acknowledged as limitation. No published Javanese decomposition study exists — this is precisely the fieldwork prediction the paper generates (Prediction 3: "A forensic taphonomy study in active Javanese cemeteries would confirm that soft tissue dissolution in volcanic-derived andosol is accelerated relative to non-volcanic tropical soils").
- However, the relevant soil parameters ARE Javanese: pH 4.5-5.5 from ISRIC SoilGrids for East Java volcanic soils, volcanic parent material (andosol), tropical temperature/moisture regime. These are the primary drivers of decomposition rate.
- The Primbon Betaljemur Adammakna Entry 334 describes body states at specific intervals ("blood, flesh, marrow perfected" at 40 days) with specificity that implies observational knowledge, not theological abstraction. This is independent corroboration from within Javanese tradition.
- The permutation test (p = 0.008) shows the correspondence is statistically unlikely to be coincidental regardless of the exact decomposition rates used.

**Suggested revision language:** "We acknowledge that no published decomposition study has been conducted in Javanese volcanic soils. However, the soil parameters driving decomposition rate (pH 4.5-5.5, andosol mineralogy, tropical temperature regime) are empirically documented for East Java, and the forensic literature provides reliable stage-transition estimates for comparable conditions."

## Critique 2: "This is a 'just-so story' — clever but unfalsifiable"

**Severity:** MEDIUM-HIGH

**Expected form:** "Any set of numbers can be mapped to some natural process with sufficient creativity. The taphonomic correspondence is suggestive but could be coincidental."

**Response:**
- The paper explicitly addresses this with three quantitative tests:
  - **Permutation test:** Only 1 of 120 possible assignments produces 5/5 stage match (p = 0.008)
  - **Monte Carlo:** Only ~11 of 500,000 random five-interval sets match all five stages (p < 0.001)
  - **Cross-tradition comparison:** 6 other mortuary traditions tested; ONLY slametan achieves 5/5 match
- The paper provides four falsification criteria (Predictions 1-4). A theory that generates testable predictions is not unfalsifiable.
- The "just-so story" critique applies to narrative arguments. This paper uses statistical testing to move beyond narrative plausibility.

**Suggested revision language:** "We note that the taphonomic mapping is accompanied by three independent statistical tests (permutation, Monte Carlo, cross-tradition comparison), all significant at p < 0.01. The paper generates four testable predictions that can be pursued through forensic taphonomy fieldwork, epigraphic corpus expansion, and ethnographic comparison."

## Critique 3: "How do you know the intervals haven't changed over time?"

**Severity:** MEDIUM

**Expected form:** "Modern slametan practice may differ from historical practice. The 3-7-40-100-1000 sequence could be a post-Islamic rationalisation or a relatively recent standardisation."

**Response:**
- The Primbon Betaljemur Adammakna (17th century or earlier) treats the sequence as a fixed computational formula (Entry 332), not as a negotiable custom. The computational treatment implies deep standardisation.
- Multiple scholars document the pre-Islamic origins of the slametan sequence: Geertz (1960), Beatty (1999), Koentjaraningrat (1994). The Islamic framing (*tahlilan*, *haul*) wraps around the pre-existing temporal structure without altering the intervals.
- Crucially, Islamic authorities classify several of these intervals as *bid'ah* (innovation). If Islam had created the sequence, they would not condemn parts of it.
- The pan-Austronesian comparative evidence (Pulotu: 30 cultures with full mortuary package) shows the STRUCTURE is inherited; only the specific timing varies — consistent with environmental calibration rather than recent invention.

## Critique 4: "The olfactory detection mechanism is speculative"

**Severity:** MEDIUM

**Expected form:** "You claim that decomposition odours can be detected through soil at burial depths, but provide no empirical evidence for Javanese burial contexts. This is hand-waving."

**Response:**
- Acknowledged as the most speculative element. The paper presents three plausible pathways, not all olfactory:
  1. Olfactory detection of putrefactive gases through porous volcanic soil (documented in forensic literature at shallow depths)
  2. Incidental exposure through grave reuse in densely used burial grounds
  3. Institutionalised grave visitation (documented in Primbon Entry 334)
- The paper does NOT claim that olfactory detection is the sole or primary mechanism. It proposes that any combination of these pathways over many generations could have produced the empirical knowledge encoded in the slametan.
- This is a prediction, not a claim: "Prediction 3: Forensic taphonomy fieldwork in Javanese cemeteries can test whether decomposition odour is detectable at typical burial depths in andosol."

## Critique 5: "Correlation is not causation — the intervals match decomposition, but that doesn't prove communities GENERATED the intervals from observation"

**Severity:** MEDIUM

**Expected form:** "The paper shows correspondence, not causation. The intervals could be derived from numerological, astrological, or calendrical systems that happen to coincide with decomposition stages."

**Response:**
- The paper addresses this by showing that NO known numerological or calendrical system produces the 3-7-40-100-1000 sequence. Hindu shraddha uses 10-13 days; Buddhist cycles use 49 days; Islamic haul uses annual markers; Christian traditions use none of these intervals.
- The Primbon's own description of body states at each interval ("blood, flesh, marrow" at 40 days) suggests the tradition itself encodes observational knowledge. If the intervals were derived from numerology, why would the descriptions match decomposition so specifically?
- The causal arrow is the CLAIM, not the PROOF. The paper proposes this as the best available explanation and generates falsifiable predictions. If a reviewer can propose a better-fitting alternative origin, the paper explicitly invites this.

## Critique 7: "AI disclosure — how much of this is AI-generated?"

**Severity:** LOW (BKI may not have strong AI policy yet)

**Expected form:** "The AI disclosure mentions Claude for 'manuscript drafting assistance.' How can we be sure the interpretations are the author's own?"

**Response:**
- AI was used for: literature search across 12 traditions, Monte Carlo simulation code, corpus analysis scripts, and manuscript drafting assistance.
- All hypotheses (taphonomic calibration, pre-Indic origin, olfactory mechanism), domain interpretations (Primbon analysis, Pulotu coding), and scholarly judgments were made by the human author.
- The AI cannot interpret the Primbon Betaljemur Adammakna, assess the anthropological significance of slametan timing, or evaluate Javanese mortuary ethnography. These require domain expertise that the author provides.
- Full transparency strengthens rather than weakens the paper. The alternative — concealing AI use — would be the actual ethical concern.
