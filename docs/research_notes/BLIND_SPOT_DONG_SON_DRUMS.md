# Research Note: Dong Son Bronze Drums as Pre-400 CE Dataset for VOLCARCH

**Date:** 2026-03-20
**Status:** PRELIMINARY — needs verification via Bernet Kempers 1988 and museum catalogs
**Relevance:** HIGH — potentially the strongest pre-existing pre-400 CE material culture dataset for western Indonesia

---

## Why This Matters for VOLCARCH

VOLCARCH's core problem: no pre-400 CE archaeological data from volcanic Java. The 3,220-fold gap (E108) between expected and observed settlements is the project's central finding. But Dong Son drums ARE pre-400 CE objects found in Java. They have been cataloged. They have find-spots. If drums have been found in volcanic zones, they represent DIRECT EVIDENCE of pre-Hindu material culture surviving in areas the taphonomic model predicts should be archaeologically blank.

---

## Known Distribution

### The Definitive Catalog
**A.J. Bernet Kempers (1988)** *The Kettledrums of Southeast Asia: A Bronze Age World and Its Aftermath.* Modern Quaternary Research in Southeast Asia, Vol. 10. Rotterdam: Balkema.

This is THE comprehensive catalog. Bernet Kempers documented **~200 Dong Son-type drums** across Southeast Asia, with significant numbers in Indonesia.

### Indonesian Distribution (from literature)
- **Java:** Multiple finds, concentrated in:
  - **Pejeng drum** (Bali, actually) — the "Moon of Pejeng," largest known (186.5 cm diameter), possibly locally cast. In Pura Penataran Sasih temple.
  - **West Java / Banten coast:** Several finds (non-volcanic zone — consistent with Buni Complex maritime network)
  - **East Java:** Finds documented but details need verification from Bernet Kempers
  - **Central Java:** At least several documented finds

- **Sumatra:** Multiple finds, including Lampung and highlands
- **Sulawesi (Selayar, etc.):** Multiple finds
- **Lesser Sunda Islands:** Alor (rich find), Sangeang, other islands
- **Kalimantan:** Few or none (interesting — despite being "archaeologically visible")

### Dating Range
- **Dong Son culture:** ~600 BCE – 200 CE (northern Vietnam / Red River Delta)
- **Indonesian drums:** Mostly dated ~300 BCE – 200 CE by typology
- **Some drums may be locally manufactured** — not all are Vietnamese imports
- **Critical point:** These are DEFINITIVELY pre-Hindu objects in the Indonesian archaeological record

### Discovery Context
Most Indonesian Dong Son drums were found:
- By accident (farming, construction, dredging)
- As heirlooms in traditional communities (Alor)
- In temple collections (Pejeng)
- During colonial-era surveys (Dutch period, pre-1942)

Very few were found through systematic excavation. This is EXACTLY what VOLCARCH predicts: accidental discovery is the primary mechanism for deeply buried material culture.

## Implications for VOLCARCH

### If drums found in volcanic zones of Java:
- **STRENGTHENS** the thesis: pre-Hindu material culture EXISTS in volcanic Java, proving the 3,220-fold gap is taphonomic, not cultural
- **WEAKENS** the "differential development" counter-argument: Java had Bronze Age exchange networks
- **PROVIDES** datable pre-400 CE reference points for the burial depth model

### If drums found ONLY in non-volcanic zones:
- **CONSISTENT WITH** but does not prove taphonomic model: drums in non-volcanic zones survived because no burial
- **Could also mean** drums were traded primarily to coastal/non-volcanic communities

### Key Questions to Resolve
1. How many drums found in East Java specifically? Find-spot coordinates?
2. Were ANY found below surface (excavated vs surface finds)?
3. Can we correlate drum find-spots with volcanic proximity zones (A/B/C)?
4. Are there museum catalogs with provenance data? (Museum Nasional Jakarta, Tropenmuseum Leiden)
