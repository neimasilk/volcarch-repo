## Project Title

**Locating Buried Pre-Hindu Settlements in Volcanic Java: A Computational Prediction-to-Fieldwork Test**

## One-Sentence Summary

Using computational models that predict where ancient settlements are buried beneath Java's volcanic deposits, we will conduct the first systematic subsurface survey of predicted sites to test whether Indonesia's "missing" pre-Hindu civilization is underground.

## Project Summary (~300 words)

Java has 45 active volcanoes across 129,000 km2. Its early archaeological record is almost empty before 400 CE — yet linguistic, demographic, and comparative evidence suggests a population of 590,000-3,900,000 people. The gap between expected and observed sites is 3,220-fold.

The VOLCARCH project (120 computational experiments, multiple peer-reviewed papers) has established that volcanic sedimentation buries archaeological sites at 2.4-6.2 mm/year across Java. At this rate, remains from 400 CE now lie beneath 7+ meters of volcanic overburden — invisible to surface survey. A five-factor visibility cascade model predicts archaeological visibility of 0.058%, matching the observed rate (0.031%) within a factor of two.

We have identified 10 high-priority fieldwork candidates where computational models predict both high settlement suitability AND deep volcanic burial. This proposal requests funding for a pilot subsurface survey at the top 5 targets using ground-penetrating radar (GPR) and electrical resistivity tomography (ERT), to test whether buried cultural layers exist at predicted depths.

The accidental discovery of Liangan (2008) — a complete Mataram-era settlement buried under 6-8m of pyroclastic deposits, found by sand miners — validates our prediction that systematic subsurface survey would recover evidence invisible to current methods.

## Objectives

1. Conduct GPR and ERT surveys at 5 predicted burial sites on the western flanks of Kelud and Penanggungan volcanoes
2. Test the VOLCARCH burial depth model against field measurements
3. Identify candidate sites for future excavation by Indonesian heritage authorities (BPCB/Balai Arkeologi)
4. Demonstrate the methodology's applicability for volcanic archaeological landscapes worldwide

## Methodology

### Site Selection (already completed)
10 GPS-referenced targets derived from intersection of:
- Candi distribution clustering (142 temples analyzed)
- Machine learning settlement suitability model (AUC = 0.768)
- Volcanic sedimentation rate projections (4.4 ± 1.2 mm/yr)
- Two independent anomaly detection methods (65% convergence)

Top 5 targets for this proposal: Kelud western flank (2 sites), Penanggungan western flank (2 sites), Arjuno-Welirang (1 site).

### Fieldwork Protocol
1. **GPR survey** — 400 MHz antenna, grid spacing 0.5m, penetration 2-5m in volcanic ash. Each site: 100m x 100m grid = 10,000 m2.
2. **ERT survey** — Wenner array, electrode spacing 1-2m, penetration 10-20m. Targets anomalies below GPR range.
3. **Targeted coring** — At GPR/ERT anomalies, extract 10m cores for stratigraphic analysis + phytolith sampling.
4. **Documentation** — GPS coordinates, depth profiles, anomaly catalogs. All data shared with BPCB Jawa Timur.

### Analysis
- Compare detected anomaly depths with predicted burial depths
- Phytolith analysis of core samples (collaboration with archaeobotanist)
- Stratigraphic dating (C-14 if organic material found)

## Expected Outcomes

**If buried cultural layers found (our prediction):**
- First systematic confirmation of VOLCARCH's burial depth model
- Candidate sites for full excavation by Indonesian authorities
- Publication in high-impact journal (Nature Communications / Antiquity)
- Foundation for large-scale survey program proposal

**If nothing found at predicted depths:**
- Model falsification data (equally publishable — Michelson-Morley precedent)
- Refined burial depth parameters for future modeling
- Negative result still advances the field by constraining possibilities

## Broader Impact

- **First systematic subsurface archaeological survey in volcanic Java** — 275+ years after Dutch colonial archaeology began
- **Methodology transferable** to volcanic landscapes worldwide (Philippines, Central America, Mediterranean)
- **Heritage management tool** — prediction maps help BPCB prioritize rescue archaeology
- **Public engagement** — YouTube series + interactive maps already in development
