# E106 — Colonial Two Javas Validation

**Status:** SUGGESTIVE (low N, directionally consistent)
**Date:** 2026-03-17
**Layer:** L1 × L3 (colonial-era validation of Two Javas)
**Papers:** P17 discussion point, P1 revision ammo
**Experiment #107**

---

## Hypothesis

The "Two Javas" spatial pattern (E100-E105) should be visible in colonial-era archaeological reports (E070, 1912-1929). Dutch archaeologists, working without spatial theory, should have found different types of remains at different volcanic distances.

## Result: Directionally Consistent but Small N

Only 43 georeferenced colonial entries (3 volcano zone, 25 court zone, 15 periphery). Too small for robust statistics but patterns are suggestive:

### Burial condition by zone
| Zone | N | % Buried |
|------|---|---------|
| Volcano (<15km) | 3 | 0% |
| **Court (15-30km)** | **25** | **52%** |
| Periphery (>30km) | 15 | 27% |

The court zone has the highest proportion of buried sites (52%) — consistent with it being the most actively sedimented zone at intermediate volcanic distances.

### Volcanic context by zone
| Zone | % Volcanic context |
|------|-------------------|
| Volcano (<15km) | **100%** |
| Court (15-30km) | **80%** |
| Periphery (>30km) | 40% |

Volcanic context drops monotonically with distance — as expected.

### Key finding: Colonial data is COURT-BIASED too
25/43 georeferenced entries (58%) are in the court zone. The Dutch archaeological service, like the ancient inscriptional system, concentrated its attention on the 15-30km zone. This is NOT because there are fewer sites in the volcano zone — it's because the court zone has roads, villages, and infrastructure that facilitate archaeological discovery.

The bias is recursive: ancient courts built where discovery is easy, and modern archaeologists discovered where ancient courts built.

## Status

**SUGGESTIVE** — Patterns are directionally consistent with Two Javas model but N=43 is too small for statistical significance (chi-square p=0.217). The colonial data confirms the court-zone bias operates across centuries: both ancient inscriptions and colonial archaeology concentrate at 15-30km.
