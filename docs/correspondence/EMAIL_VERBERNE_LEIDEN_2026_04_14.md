Dear Professor Verberne,

I am writing to inquire about the possibility of pursuing a PhD under your supervision. Your work on domain-specific NLP for archaeological text mining — particularly the EXALT project and "Digging in Documents" — addresses the exact intersection of NLP and heritage data that my main research project requires.

I am developing VOLCARCH: a multi-layered research program that reconstructs pre-colonial Indonesian settlement patterns by mining Dutch East India Company (VOC) archives with NLP and combining the extracted information with geospatial modelling. The project has three computational components:

First, a text mining pipeline for 22,000+ colonial-era mentions of Indonesian settlements in Dutch archival sources. The core NLP challenges include named entity recognition for 17th-18th century Dutch (spelling variation, code-switching with Malay), temporal information extraction, and disambiguation of colonial place names against modern GIS coordinates.

Second, a spatial machine learning system that predicts site locations in a landscape where approximately 99.97% of expected archaeological signals are missing due to measurable volcanic processes. The system uses Bayesian inference across 186 experimental configurations with 100,000 Monte Carlo simulations each.

Third, the integration layer: linking NLP-extracted settlement mentions to spatial predictions to produce a unified model of where sites should exist but cannot be found through conventional survey.

The project currently has 6 papers under review and an arXiv preprint (arXiv:2604.00023). I believe VOLCARCH constitutes a natural extension of your archaeological text mining work — from mining modern excavation reports to mining the primary colonial sources themselves.

I am seeking funding through BPI Dosen (Indonesia's government scholarship for permanent university lecturers) or LPDP. I hold an M.Sc. in Informatics, serve as a lecturer at Universitas Bhinneka Nusantara, Malang, Indonesia, and maintain additional research in legal NLP (corpus of 693 Indonesian court verdicts with sentencing analysis) and a neuro-symbolic legal reasoning system.

Would you be open to a brief exchange to assess fit?
