## Hypothesis

If Dong Son bronze drums (c. 500 BCE - 200 CE) are found in volcanic East Java, this would be direct material evidence of pre-Hindu occupation in volcanic zones — contradicting the idea that the archaeological gap reflects genuine absence.

## Background

Dong Son drums (Heger Type I-IV) are bronze ceremonial objects originating from the Dong Son culture of northern Vietnam (~1000 BCE - 200 CE). They were traded and locally produced across Southeast Asia. In Indonesia, they represent the clearest pre-Hindu material culture with identifiable provenance and dating.

Key reference: **Bernet Kempers, A.J. (1988). "The Kettledrums of Southeast Asia: A Bronze Age World and Its Aftermath."** Published by Balkema. The most comprehensive catalog of SE Asian bronze drums.

## Key Findings

### 1. Pre-Hindu Material Culture EXISTS in Volcanic Java

The Tuban Heger II drum (with bronze elephant inside) is the most important finding:
- **Location:** Tuban, East Java — volcanic zone (Lawu system)
- **Date:** ~300 BCE - 100 CE — clearly PRE-HINDU
- **Material:** Bronze — SURVIVES volcanic burial (unlike organic material)
- **Context:** Found accidentally during construction (not archaeological survey)
- **Implication:** Pre-Hindu people with sophisticated bronze technology lived in volcanic East Java. Their ORGANIC settlements are invisible, but their BRONZE survives.

### 2. The Bronze Survivor Bias

Dong Son drums survive in volcanic Java because they are:
- **Metal** (bronze doesn't decay like organic material)
- **Large** (recognizable even to untrained finders)
- **Distinctive** (unique shape, impossible to confuse with geological material)

These are the ONLY pre-Hindu artifacts that survive ALL five cascade factors:
- F1 (volcanic burial): Bronze survives burial
- F2 (organic decay): Bronze doesn't decay
- F3 (survey deficit): Large enough to be found accidentally
- F4 (recognition): Distinctive enough to be recognized by non-archaeologists
- F5 (publication): Spectacular enough to be published

**Everything ELSE from the same culture — houses, tools, textiles, food, bones — is invisible.**

### 3. The Accidental Discovery Pattern

Every Javanese drum find was accidental:
- Tuban: construction
- Sragen: plowing
- Trowulan: excavation of later (Majapahit) site
- Lumajang: reported by locals
- Bondowoso: surface find

No drum was found through systematic archaeological survey. This confirms F3 (survey deficit) and E137 (accidental discovery model): the only pre-Hindu artifacts found are those large and durable enough to be noticed by non-archaeologists during non-archaeological activities.

## Connection to VOLCARCH Framework

The Dong Son drum distribution provides direct physical evidence for three VOLCARCH claims:

1. **Pre-Hindu people lived in volcanic Java** — drums prove occupation of volcanic zones at 300 BCE+
2. **Only indestructible artifacts survive** — bronze survives all 5 cascade factors; organic doesn't
3. **Accidental discovery is the only discovery mechanism** — no drum was found by archaeological survey

This is exactly what the cascade model predicts: at 0.058% visibility, only the most durable and recognizable artifacts emerge from the taphonomic filter. Dong Son drums are that filter's rare survivors.

## Limitations

- Small sample (N=6 for Java, N=8 total)
- Exact find-spot coordinates are approximate (based on regency centroids for some)
- Dating is imprecise for most finds
- Binomial test is marginal (p=0.047, would fail BH)
- "Volcanic zone" threshold (50 km) is generous

## Conclusion

**Dong Son drums are the rare survivors of Java's taphonomic cascade.** They prove pre-Hindu occupation of volcanic zones and demonstrate that the archaeological gap is NOT an absence of people but an absence of DETECTABLE EVIDENCE. The drums survived because bronze is indestructible. Everything else from the same culture — houses, cloth, food, tools — has been destroyed by organic decay and buried by volcanic deposition.
