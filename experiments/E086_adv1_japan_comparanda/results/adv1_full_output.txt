4. LAHAR AMPLIFICATION: TROPICAL vs TEMPERATE
----------------------------------------

  Merapi (Java) post-eruption lahars: >250 in 2 rainy seasons
  Typical Japan post-eruption lahars: ~20 (variable)

  Java annual rainfall in volcanic zones: ~2500 mm
  Japan annual rainfall in volcanic zones: ~1600 mm
  Rainfall ratio: 1.6x

  Java peak intensity: 40 mm/hr
  Japan peak intensity (lahar-triggering): 26 mm/hr
  Intensity ratio: 1.5x

  KEY MECHANISM: Tropical convective rainfall in Java triggers far more
  frequent and intense lahars than Japan's temperate frontal rainfall.
  Snow packs in Japan can PROTECT tephra from remobilization.
  No such protection exists in tropical Java.

5. THE INTERACTION: BURIAL DEPTH x SURVEY INTENSITY
----------------------------------------

  The VOLCARCH thesis depends on TWO factors interacting:
    (a) Deeper burial in Java than Japan (confirmed: ~30x background rate)
    (b) Lower survey intensity in Indonesia than Japan (confirmed: ~100-200x)

  Combined effect:
    Java sites are buried ~30x deeper AND searched for ~100-200x less.
    Japan sites are buried ~30x shallower AND searched for ~100-200x more.

  This explains why Japan's volcanic zones yield rich records
  while Java's volcanic zones appear archaeologically sparse.
  The difference is not the presence/absence of burial,
  but the combination of burial depth and recovery effort.

6. KIKAI-AKAHOYA: JAPAN'S OWN 'INVISIBLE MILLENNIUM'
----------------------------------------

  The Kikai caldera VEI-7 eruption (7,300 BP) demonstrates that
  volcanic events CAN create archaeological gaps even in Japan:

  - Southern Kyushu depopulated for ~500-1000 years
  - Archaeological record shows clear gap below Akahoya tephra
  - Recovery sites are small, coastal, culturally impoverished
  - Cultural continuity partially preserved (Nishinozono tradition)
  - Gap was DETECTED only through systematic tephrochronology

  This is exactly what VOLCARCH proposes happened in Java,
  but at smaller scales repeated over many eruption cycles.
  Japan FOUND its gap. Indonesia hasn't looked.
