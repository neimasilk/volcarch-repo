# Subfield 04 Summary — Indonesian Archaeometry (Gap Finding)

## VOLCARCH claim under test
"Non-bead archaeometric studies of Indonesian materials (pottery, metal sourcing, organic residues) should show sophisticated craft production consistent with organised invisible civilisation."

## Search executed
- WebSearch on "Indonesia ceramic pottery archaeometric compositional analysis Majapahit Mataram Tarumanagara ICP sourcing"

## Material findings

### 1. Indonesian archaeometry is a THIN literature

Compared to the mature specialist communities in glass bead archaeometry (Lankton, Dussubieux, Wang) or bronze drum studies (Calo, Bernet Kempers), Indonesian CERAMIC archaeometry appears to be sparse.

Examples found:
- Samudra Pasai (Aceh) ceramic salt-production archaeometry (15th c. CE, post-Sriwijaya) — recent
- Lampung post-Sriwijaya granulometric analysis of highland pottery
- General archaeometric methodology works (Tite, Wilson, et al.) but Indonesia under-represented

**No major published compositional sourcing study** for Majapahit, Mataram, or Tarumanagara-era Javanese ceramics that I surfaced in this search. This is striking — for a civilisation widely discussed, its pottery has received minimal analytical attention.

### 2. What this ABSENCE means for VOLCARCH

Two interpretations possible:

**(a) Gap consistent with VOLCARCH:** Indonesian archaeology has historically privileged monumental architecture and inscriptions (stone temples, DHARMA texts) over "mundane" craft production. Archaeometry of pottery/metal/organic residue — the analyses most likely to reveal invisible craft civilisations — has been under-done. This is a methodological gap that PREDICTS we have not yet seen the pre-Hindu material signal.

**(b) Neutral:** Could mean pre-400 CE ceramic production was indeed minimal in Java and the absence reflects genuine absence. But this interpretation is weak because it conflates absence-of-analysis with absence-of-material.

VOLCARCH can reasonably argue (a) — the gap in Indonesian archaeometry is part of the same "invisibility by methodological neglect" pattern that characterises the broader archaeological survey deficit.

### 3. Recent signs of field development

- Samudra Pasai, Aceh: archaeometric study of 13th-15th c. CE ceramics (XRF, XRD, SEM-EDS)
- Indonesian Center for Human Evolution (BRIN, 2024): new institutional infrastructure
- Post-2020 uptick in archaeometric publications (per Scopus-level broad impression)

**Implication:** Indonesian archaeometry is an emerging field. In 5-10 years, systematic analytical work could surface the invisible civilisation's material signal. This is consistent with VOLCARCH's thesis that we are in an early stage of recovery.

## Counter-evidence status

**None found.** Absence of published studies is a GAP, not a counter-argument.

## Strategic implication for VOLCARCH

**Opportunity for future work:** A systematic archaeometric study of pre-Hindu pottery samples (if any survive at Buni, Batujaya, or other pre-400 CE sites in West Java) would be a direct empirical contribution. Budget ~$5K-10K for compositional analysis of ~20 sherds via LA-ICP-MS at a participating lab (could partner with Dussubieux at Field Museum Chicago or Wang at NTNU Taiwan).

This could be a future experimental program (call it E212 or integrate into a future paper proposal).
