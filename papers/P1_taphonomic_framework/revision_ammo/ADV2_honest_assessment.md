# ADV-2 Honest Assessment

**What we tested, what we found, and what it means for L1.**
**Prepared:** 2026-03-13

---

## What We Tested

We asked: do volcanic regions of Island Southeast Asia have a different archaeological site-type distribution from non-volcanic regions? Specifically, if volcanic burial preferentially destroys open-air sites, volcanic regions should show a higher proportion of enclosed sites (caves and rockshelters).

We compared 67 sites in volcanic regions (Java, Sumatra, Sulawesi, Nusa Tenggara, Philippines, Maluku) against 13 sites in non-volcanic controls (Kalimantan, Madagascar) using the 80-site mini-NusaRC v3 dataset. Fisher exact test on enclosed-vs-open classification.

## What We Found

**No difference.** Fisher exact p = 0.760. Volcanic regions: 62.7% enclosed. Non-volcanic controls: 69.2% enclosed. The distributions are statistically indistinguishable.

Moreover, the per-region data revealed that cave dominance is driven by karst availability, not volcanism. Kalimantan (non-volcanic, karst-rich) is 100% cave sites. Madagascar (non-volcanic, no karst tradition) is 80% open-air. Sulawesi and Maluku (volcanic, karst-rich) are 83-100% caves. Java, despite being the most volcanically active region, has the LOWEST enclosed proportion (36.8%) — because its record includes deep-time river-terrace sites (Sangiran, Trinil).

## Does This Undermine L1?

**It undermines one specific argument that L1 should never have been making.** The claim that "volcanic regions show more caves because volcanoes destroyed the open-air sites" is not supported and should not appear in any VOLCARCH paper.

**It does not undermine the core L1 claim.** The core claim is that volcanic sedimentation buries archaeological sites to depths below standard survey detection, creating systematic gaps in the archaeological record. This claim rests on burial depth evidence, not site-type ratios.

## Why L1 Still Stands

The depth-based evidence is strong and comes from three independent sources:

1. **Colonial measurements.** Dutch colonial archaeologists physically measured burial depths at 32 sites across Java (E070). Mean: 2.88 m. Maximum: 9.14 m (Prambanan). These are observations, not models. When a colonial archaeologist writes that a Vishnu statue was found 9.14 m underground during well digging (OV 1925), that is direct evidence of volcanic burial.

2. **Sedimentation modeling.** A Pyle (1989) exponential thinning model fit to GVP eruption records for 7 East Java volcanoes achieves r = 0.951 against observed burial depths (E075). The model predicts that 32.3% of East Java has cumulative burial >1 m and 12.8% has >3 m. These are minimum estimates because the GVP severely under-records pre-1800 eruptions and the model excludes lahars.

3. **Eruption-site correlation.** A systematic dataset of 51 eruption-site pairs across 12 volcanic systems (E083) documents specific volcanic events affecting specific archaeological sites. Of 24 sites with measured burial depths, the mean is 3.41 m. The evidence is 86% primary — directly observed and documented, not inferred from proximity.

Additionally, ADV-3 (E069) confirmed that volcanic proximity significantly reduces site density even after controlling for three independent survey intensity proxies (roads, BPCB offices, university archaeology departments): quasi-Poisson p = 0.0015.

## Bottom Line

A reviewer who asks "doesn't the lack of site-type difference undermine your volcanic taphonomy claim?" deserves a direct answer: "Yes, site-type ratios do not distinguish volcanic from non-volcanic regions, and we do not claim they do. Our taphonomic framework is based on burial depth measurements — 32 colonial-era observations averaging 2.88 m, a sedimentation model validated at r = 0.951, and 51 documented eruption-site correlations. Volcanic burial does not selectively destroy site types; it buries everything deeper."

This is a stronger position than the pre-ADV-2 framing, because it is honestly grounded in physical evidence rather than distributional inference.
