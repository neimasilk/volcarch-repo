## Material findings

### 1. MAJOR: Ten+ Jatim beads in Gyeongju Korea tombs

> "At least TEN Jatim beads were unearthed in Gyeongju, Korea, from tombs belonging to members of the upper social class, and were dated between the late fourth and mid-sixth centuries AD."

This is a substantial corpus, not an isolated find. **Before the 2024 Datong paper was published, the Korean corpus was already known** — Lankton & Bernbaum published on Korean National Treasure 634 (a Jatim bead from Sikrichong 5th/6th c. Silla tomb).

### 2. Lankton & Bernbaum, Korean National Treasure 634 analysis

> "Korean National Treasure 634 exemplifies the complex integration of archaeological, chemical, and iconographic analysis. Compositional analyses, derived from LA-ICP-MS, SEM-EDS and EPMA measurements, can help answer questions about what types of glass were used for manufacture, whether it was imported or locally produced, and whether the glass was colored locally."

Full paper available on academia.edu. This is the precursor to Jia et al. 2024.

### 3. Implications for VOLCARCH

**Channel 6 corpus expanded dramatically:**
- Datong (China, 398-494 CE): 2 Jatim beads (Jia et al. 2024)
- Gyeongju (Korea, late 4th - mid 6th c. CE): 10+ Jatim beads (Lankton & Bernbaum)
- Berenike (Egypt, Roman): Jatim-type beads from Java (subfield 09)
- Palau (Micronesia): circulating as currency (Lankton)
- Japanese sites: Kofun period finds (to verify)

**Trade dating window:** late 4th to 6th century CE for the Korean corpus — overlaps with:
- Pre-inscription Java (before 358 Tarumanagara in West Java)
- The invisible period in East Java's volcanic interior
- Northern Wei Datong (398-494)

Combined: Javanese glass bead industry had **international high-status market presence by 4th-5th c. CE** serving elite tombs in China, Korea, Egypt. This is not speculative — it is peer-reviewed archaeometric fact.
