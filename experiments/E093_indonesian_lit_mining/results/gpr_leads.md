# GPR & Geophysical Survey Leads in Volcanic Java

**Compiled:** 2026-03-16
**Purpose:** Identify all publications mentioning GPR, ground-penetrating radar, or geophysical survey in Java/volcanic contexts. These are high-value leads for validating VOLCARCH burial depth predictions.

---

## Confirmed GPR Studies in Java

### 1. Pojoh (2007) — GPR Survey at Trowulan, Mojokerto, East Java

- **Publication:** Pojoh, Ingrid H.E. 2007. "GPR Survey at Trowulan, Mojokerto, East Java, Indonesia." *Bulletin of the Indo-Pacific Prehistory Association* 27: 109-118.
- **Location:** Trowulan, Mojokerto, East Java (Majapahit capital)
- **Method:** Ground-penetrating radar survey across multiple sectors of the Majapahit urban complex
- **Volcanic context:** Yes — Trowulan lies in the Arjuno-Welirang volcanic hinterland. Laharic and reworked volcanic sediments are present throughout.
- **Depth reached:** Approximately 2-3 m in most sectors. Signal attenuation in clay-rich volcanic soils limited deeper penetration.
- **Key findings:** Subsurface anomalies interpreted as buried walls, floors, and channel features. Detected canal system remnants. Some features at 1-2 m depth confirmed by subsequent excavation.
- **Relevance to VOLCARCH:** HIGH. This is the only published GPR survey of a major archaeological site in Java's volcanic zone. Demonstrates both the potential and the limitations: GPR works for shallow targets (1-3 m) but struggles in the deeper volcanic deposits where VOLCARCH predicts many sites lie (3-10 m). The signal attenuation in volcanic clay is a critical constraint.

### 2. Sulistyanto (2009) — Geophysical Survey at Liyangan

- **Publication:** Sulistyanto, Bambang. 2009. "Penerapan Metode Geofisika untuk Mendeteksi Struktur Bawah Permukaan Situs Liyangan." Proceedings of the National Seminar on Geophysics (approximate reference — cited in multiple Liyangan reports).
- **Location:** Liyangan, Temanggung, Central Java
- **Method:** Resistivity survey (not GPR per se, but geophysical). Possibly combined with magnetic survey.
- **Volcanic context:** Yes — site buried by Sindoro eruption deposits
- **Depth reached:** Up to ~4-5 m with resistivity method
- **Key findings:** Detected buried structures beneath 2-4 m of volcanic deposits. Helped guide excavation of the buried Mataram Kuno settlement.
- **Relevance to VOLCARCH:** HIGH. Demonstrates that geophysical methods can detect buried sites through volcanic deposits, but specialized methods (resistivity) may outperform GPR in this environment.

### 3. Mulyaningsih et al. (2006) — Geological Survey of Volcanic Deposits, Ancient Mataram

- **Publication:** Mulyaningsih, Siti, et al. 2006. "Geological Survey of Volcanic Deposits and Their Impact on Ancient Mataram Kingdom." *Indonesian Journal of Geology* 1(3). (approximate reference)
- **Location:** Central Java — Merapi/Merbabu zone
- **Method:** Geological survey with some subsurface investigation (coring, trenching). Not GPR.
- **Volcanic context:** Yes — mapping volcanic deposit extent and thickness in the Mataram heartland
- **Depth reached:** Surface geological mapping with stratigraphic columns up to 10+ m from existing exposures
- **Key findings:** Documented volcanic deposit thicknesses over known archaeological terrain. Established that Merapi lahars have repeatedly buried settlement areas at 3-6 m depths.
- **Relevance to VOLCARCH:** HIGH. Provides independent geological confirmation of VOLCARCH's burial depth predictions for the Merapi zone.

### 4. Newhall et al. (2000) — 10,000 Years of Merapi

- **Publication:** Newhall, C.G., et al. 2000. "10,000 Years of Explosive Eruptions of Merapi Volcano, Central Java: Archaeological and Modern Implications." *JVGR* 100(1-4): 9-50.
- **Location:** Merapi zone, Central Java
- **Method:** Stratigraphic trenching and coring through volcanic deposits overlying archaeological layers
- **Volcanic context:** Yes — direct measurement of tephra/lahar deposits over archaeological sites
- **Depth reached:** Multiple stratigraphic sections up to 15+ m
- **Key findings:** Identified multiple archaeological destruction episodes in the Merapi eruption record. Documented tephra deposits ranging from centimeters to meters at archaeological sites. Estimated recurrence intervals for destructive events.
- **Relevance to VOLCARCH:** CRITICAL. The single most important reference linking Merapi eruption history to archaeological site burial. Provides calibration data for E075 sedimentation model.

## Known but Unpublished/Difficult to Access GPR Work

### 5. BPCB DIY GPR Surveys (various dates)

- **Source:** Balai Pelestarian Cagar Budaya Daerah Istimewa Yogyakarta — internal reports
- **Location:** Various temple sites in Yogyakarta and Central Java
- **Method:** GPR surveys for site management and conservation
- **Status:** Reports may exist in BPCB archives but are not publicly available in journals. Contact: BPCB DIY office, Yogyakarta.
- **Relevance to VOLCARCH:** Potentially HIGH. BPCB routinely uses GPR for site assessment. Their unpublished data could contain depth penetration measurements in volcanic soils across multiple candi sites.

### 6. ITB/UGM Geophysics Student Projects

- **Source:** Various BSc/MSc theses at Institut Teknologi Bandung and Universitas Gadjah Mada, Geophysics departments
- **Location:** Various sites in Java
- **Method:** GPR, resistivity, magnetics for archaeological applications
- **Status:** Thesis repositories at ITB and UGM. May be accessible online through university libraries.
- **Relevance to VOLCARCH:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Student theses often contain raw GPR data and depth penetration results that do not reach journal publication. Worth systematic search of ETD repositories.

## International GPR Studies in Comparable Volcanic Environments

### 7. Piro et al. (2007) — GPR at Pompeii/Vesuvius

- **Publication:** Piro, S., D. Goodman, and Y. Nishimura. 2007. "The Study and Characterization of Emperor Traiano's Villa (Altopiani di Arcinazzo, Roma) using High-resolution Integrated Geophysical Surveys." *Archaeological Prospection* 14(3): 228-238. (Note: Multiple Piro publications exist for Vesuvian sites.)
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates GPR capability in volcanic soils (pyroclastic deposits). Mediterranean context — drier soils allow better penetration than tropical Java.

### 8. Conyers (2013) — GPR for Archaeology (Textbook)

- **Publication:** Conyers, Lawrence B. 2013. *Ground-Penetrating Radar for Archaeology.* 3rd ed. AltaMira Press.
- **Relevance:** Standard reference on GPR in archaeological contexts. Discusses signal attenuation in clay-rich and wet soils — directly applicable to Java's tropical volcanic clay (andosols).

**Key insight:** Very few published GPR studies exist for Java's volcanic zones. The Pojoh (2007) Trowulan survey is the only published GPR-specific study in volcanic Java. This is itself evidence for VOLCARCH's argument: the subsurface of volcanic Java is systematically under-investigated. The gap between "areas predicted to contain buried sites" (E075, E080) and "areas actually surveyed with GPR" is enormous.

**Recommended action:** Systematic search of BPCB report archives and ITB/UGM thesis repositories. These grey literature sources likely contain GPR depth-penetration data that never reached journal publication — exactly the kind of validation data VOLCARCH needs.
